| 8.8 | Theorem (Fubini), pp.164–166 | Assume σ-finite factors and S⊗T-measurable f. **(a)** For f:X×Y→[0,∞], the everywhere-defined section integrals φ(x)=∫fₓdλ and ψ(y)=∫fʸdμ are measurable and ∫φdμ=∫f d(μ⊗λ)=∫ψdλ. **(b)** For complex f, finiteness of ∫(∫|f(x,y)|dλ(y))dμ(x) implies f∈L¹(μ⊗λ). **(c)** If f∈L¹(μ⊗λ), its sections are integrable for almost every outer variable; the a.e.-defined section integrals belong to L¹ of the respective factors and satisfy the same integral equalities. | 8.9 | Counterexamples, pp.166–167 | **(a)** On the unit square with both measures Lebesgue, choose 0=δ₁<δ₂<⋯→1 and real continuous gₙ supported in (δₙ,δₙ₊₁) with integral 1. Set f(x,y)=Σₙ[gₙ(x)−gₙ₊₁(x)]gₙ(y). Each point has at most one nonzero summand. Both iterated integrals exist, but integrating y then x gives 1, while x then y gives 0. The absolute double integral is infinite; f is continuous away from (1,1). **(b)** On [0,1]² take Lebesgue μ, counting λ, and f=1 on the diagonal and 0 otherwise. It is product-measurable, its x-integral is always 0 and y-integral always 1, so iterated integrals differ; λ is not σ-finite. **(c)** Conditional on CH, choose a bijection j:[0,1]→W onto a well-order whose elements have countably many predecessors. For Q={(x,y):j(x)<j(y)} and f=1_Q, every vertical section is cocountable and every horizontal section countable; all sections are Borel and their integrals are the constants 1 and 0. Iterated integrals differ despite bounded f and probability factors: joint product measurability fails. **Do not introduce CH as a Lean axiom:** prove the conditional statement under the explicit well-order/bijection hypothesis; existence under CH is set-theoretic extra scope. |
| 8.10 | Exposition / counterexample, pp.167–168 | Completeness of both factors need not make the uncompleted product complete. In particular, Lebesgue m₁⊗m₁ is not complete and is not equal as a measure space to complete planar Lebesgue m₂; its completion is m₂. The explicit distinction between Borel, Lebesgue-completed, and product σ-algebras must be retained. |

## Faithfulness risks that require explicit accounting

1. Completeness in 8.10–8.12 is a statement about σ-algebras, not merely equality of measures on Borel sets.
2. 8.9(c) explicitly depends on CH in print. It cannot be unconditionally instantiated by adding a prohibited axiom.
3. 8.16 allows +∞ values and only local absolute continuity; a globally smooth finite function variant is weaker.
4. 8.18 uses Rudin's centered ball maximal function in every finite dimension, not an arbitrarily renamed or pre-assumed Lᵖ function.
5. Chapter 9 normalizes measure by (2π)^(−1/2) and uses exp(−ixt). Mathlib's conventional real Fourier kernel uses exp(−2πixt); any restatement must implement and prove the scaling bridge.
