## Statement hazards and conventions requiring disclosure

1. **19.6: printed affine constant.** On printed p. 377/PDF p. 390, the book gives `B_g=aB_f` for `g(x)=f(ax+b)`, while requiring every `B` to be positive and without restricting `a>0`. For `a<0`, that proposed witness is negative. A concrete mathematical counterexample to the witness formula is `Mₙ=1`, `f(x)=exp(ix)`, `β_f=B_f=1`, `a=-1`, `b=0`: `g(x)=exp(-ix)` has derivative norm 1, whereas the claimed bound for `n=1` has right side -1. The class-invariance conclusion is true; a corrected witness is `|a|B_f` for `a≠0`, or `max(1,|a|)B_f` for all real `a`, retaining `β_g=β_f`. No Lean counterexample or corrected theorem has been proved by this inventory. Under the brief, this node cannot be counted complete until the correction is proved and disclosed.

2. **20.2: kernel diagonal.** Printed p. 387/PDF p. 400 universally quantifies the second strict estimate over `z∈Ω` and `ζ∈D`, but does not say `z≠ζ`. Ordinary division makes the formula undefined on the diagonal. This is not repaired by Lean's totalized division: at `z=ζ`, its real right-hand side is 0, so the strict inequality is impossible. Since `E` is a compact subset of an open disk, there are points of `D\E`; thus the problematic diagonal really occurs. Formalize the uniform first estimate for all relevant finite points and the kernel estimate for `z≠ζ`, or use the equivalent multiplied bound on the polynomial expression where appropriate. Record this as an explicit domain correction rather than presenting it as the literal quantified statement.

3. **20.2: the point at infinity.** `Ω` is a subset of the Riemann sphere and includes infinity. A statement only asserting `DifferentiableOn ℂ g (Eᶜ)` omits holomorphy at infinity. One may encode the latter through an extension of `w↦g(1/w)` at 0, with the value supplied by the construction, or use an appropriate sphere notion. Infinity is not an ordinary complex input to the norm/kernel formulas. Preserve this content explicitly.

4. **19.11: extended values.** `Q` and `q` may be infinite. A bare real `tsum` takes a default value when a series is not summable, and a bare real supremum does not automatically represent `+∞`; these are not faithful definitions of the printed `Q` and `q`. Use extended nonnegative values or an equivalent explicit finiteness/partial-sum formulation, and provide measurable logarithm/integral semantics. Positivity of every `Mₙ` remains a standing hypothesis inherited from 19.6 even though 19.11 does not repeat that word.

5. **20.4: hypotheses and support.** Do not add `NormalSpace X` as a permanent theorem hypothesis and do not replace compact support by mere boundedness or vanishing at infinity. The one-point-compactification plus cutoff route preserves the printed locally compact Hausdorff generality.

6. **19.1–19.3: normalization and representatives.** The chapter deliberately changes measure convention from Chapter 9. The factor `1/(2π)`, the sign of the exponent, the distinction between a.e. L² classes and pointwise holomorphic functions, and the equality of the extremal norm in 19.2 are part of the statement. Endpoints of the support interval may be changed only with the appropriate null-set explanation.

7. **20.5: degenerate case in the proof.** The printed intermediate strict error estimates using the modulus of continuity need a separate zero-function case when that modulus vanishes. The theorem's `ε>0` statement itself has no such defect. A formal proof may split this case rather than repeat a strict `0<0` estimate.

8. **19.6: unrestricted regularization assertion.** Printed p. 378/PDF p. 391 claims that every class defined by a positive sequence can be represented by a positive sequence satisfying normalization and log convexity. A direct mathematical counterexample is `Mₙ=exp(-n²)`. For `f(x)=exp(ix)`, every iterated-derivative norm is 1. Membership would require `1≤β Bⁿ exp(-n²)` for all natural `n`, impossible for any fixed positive `β,B` because the right side tends to 0. On the other hand, every positive normalized log-convex sequence `N` satisfies `Nₙ≥N₁ⁿ` by induction using the monotone successive ratios. Thus this same `f` belongs to **every** such `C{Nₙ}`, taking `β=1` and `B=1/N₁`. Consequently no positive normalized log-convex `N` represents `C{exp(-n²)}`. This is a counterexample to the unrestricted equivalence itself, not merely to a proposed witness. A correction must constrain the original sequence or permit a degenerate regularization; neither a Lean counterexample nor a corrected equivalence has yet been proved. The subsequent theorems can retain their explicitly stated positive normalized log-convex hypotheses, but the unqualified no-loss claim cannot be counted proved.

### Research proof and missing hypothesis for the 19.6 regularization claim

**Not yet Lean-proved.** This argument is a source-level mathematical counterexample, independently checked against the unrestricted positive-sequence hypothesis on printed pp. 377–378.

For a positive normalized log-convex sequence `N`, put `rₙ=Nₙ₊₁/Nₙ`. The inequalities `Nₙ²≤Nₙ₋₁Nₙ₊₁` imply `rₙ≥rₙ₋₁` for `n≥1`, because all denominators are positive. Therefore each `rₙ≥r₀=N₁`, and multiplication of the first `n` ratios gives `Nₙ≥N₁ⁿ`, including equality at `n=0`. For `f(x)=exp(ix)`, direct induction gives `Dⁿf(x)=iⁿexp(ix)` and hence `‖Dⁿf‖∞=1`. Choosing `β=1` and `B=1/N₁` yields `β BⁿNₙ≥1` for every `n`, so `f∈C{Nₙ}` for every candidate normalized positive log-convex sequence.

Now let `Mₙ=exp(-(n:ℝ)²)`, which is positive for all `n` and satisfies `M₀=1`. If the same function belonged to `C{Mₙ}`, fixed positive `β,B` would satisfy `1≤exp(log β+n log B-n²)` for all natural `n`. The exponent tends to negative infinity because its leading term is `-n²`, so the right side tends to zero, a contradiction. Thus `C{Mₙ}` is different from every candidate `C{Nₙ}`.

A **necessary missing hypothesis** for equivalence to a positive normalized log-convex class is that the original sequence admits an exponential lower bound up to a positive constant: there must exist `c,r>0` with `c rⁿ≤Mₙ` for all natural `n`. Indeed, any such equivalent class contains `exp(ix)`, and its original derivative bound rearranges to this condition with `c=1/β` and `r=1/B`. Conversely, this lower bound is exactly what makes `exp(ix)` a member, taking `β=1/c` and `B=1/r`. For a positive sequence this condition is equivalently `liminf Mₙ^(1/n)>0` (with the liminf over positive `n`, permitting infinity); finitely many early terms can be absorbed into `c`. The counterexample has this liminf equal to zero. This document does **not** claim to have proved that the added condition suffices for the full regularization theorem. No corrected equivalence is being substituted silently, and 19.6 remains incomplete until the defect/correction requirements in the brief are handled formally.
