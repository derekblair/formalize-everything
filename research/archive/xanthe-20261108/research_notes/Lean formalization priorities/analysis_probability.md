# Analysis and probability book candidates

## Which books are right-sized and valuable rather than merely famous?

### Takeaway

The best new openings are **David Williams, _Probability with Martingales_**, and **J. R. Norris, _Markov Chains_**: compact, class-tested books with high reusable value and no complete ordered formalization located. Stein–Shakarchi and Le Gall are worthwhile but much harder than their page counts suggest; Durrett and Folland are multi-year programmes, not quick follow-ons to Rudin PMA.

### Cited Findings

#### Counting and selection convention

- The calibration is the coordinator-supplied **Rudin PMA ≈330 main-text nodes**. I use “node” for a main-text definition, theorem, lemma, proposition, or corollary; I exclude end-of-chapter exercises and ordinary worked examples unless they introduce a definition. This is a scoping convention, not a claim that examples are mathematically unimportant. The programme should freeze its exercise policy before inventory sign-off. [Assignment brief](/root/formalize-research/BRIEF.md)
- I mechanically counted unique numbered labels in accessible PDFs, then allowed for unnumbered definitions and named constructions. Thus the ranges below are **estimates**, not inventories. Counts: Norris 106 theorem/lemma/corollary labels; Stein–Shakarchi 132 theorem/lemma/proposition/corollary labels; Le Gall 180 including 40 definitions; Durrett 461 theorem/lemma/corollary labels plus 195 excluded examples; Folland 312 theorem/lemma/proposition/corollary labels. [Norris PDF](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf); [Stein–Shakarchi PDF](https://www.cmat.edu.uy/~mordecki/courses/medida2013/book.pdf); [Le Gall PDF](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/server/api/core/bitstreams/f97859c7-d5d3-439c-8f4c-c2b6aeb8155c/content); [Durrett PDF mirror used for the count](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/MATH493/Durrett%20Probability%202019.pdf); [Folland PDF](https://ia802900.us.archive.org/17/items/real-analysis-textbooks/Real%20Analysis%20-%20G.%20B.%20Folland.pdf)
- Williams has no conventional theorem numbering, so I counted **219 unique numbered section labels** in its complete TOC and estimated roughly 1.4–2.0 main-text nodes per section rather than pretending there is an exact theorem count. [Williams publisher preview/TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)

#### 1. David Williams, _Probability with Martingales_ — strongest overall

- **Identity:** David Williams, _Probability with Martingales_, Cambridge University Press, **1991**, ISBN 9780521406055; the 2012 digital issue is not a new mathematical edition. CUP describes a selective, class-tested 265-page account centred on discrete-time martingales, with the strong law, three-series theorem, and a characteristic-function proof of the CLT. [CUP book page](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/probability-with-martingales/B4CFCE0D08930FB46C6E93E775503926)
- **Teaching/canonical evidence:** the preface says it is essentially the lecture notes for a Cambridge third-year undergraduate course, and Chapter E reproduces the homework sheet. A current UT Austin syllabus calls Durrett widely used and Williams a beautiful introduction to martingale theory; Stanford’s theory course assigns Williams Chapters 1–8 and 16–18. [Williams preface/TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf); [UT Austin syllabus](https://www.ices.utexas.edu/media/academics/course/229_Syllabus_CSE_384K_Zitkovic.pdf); [Stanford STAT310A](https://adembo.su.domains/stat-310a/)
- **TOC scope:** Chapter 0 branching processes; Chapters 1–8 measure spaces, events, random variables, independence, integration, expectation, an easy strong law, and product measure; Chapters 9–15 conditional expectation, martingales, convergence, L² martingales, uniform integrability, UI martingales, and applications; Chapters 16–18 characteristic functions, weak convergence, and the CLT; appendices A1, A3, A4, A5, A9, A13, A14, A16; separate exercises. [Complete TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Estimated scope/effort:** **300–450 nodes; large single-book project**. The appendix on Kolmogorov/Strassen laws of the iterated logarithm and the application chapters prevent this being a small project. [TOC and estimator basis](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Downstream value:** a single source-compatible layer for conditional expectation, discrete martingales, UI, strong laws, weak convergence, likelihood ratios, branching processes, and the characteristic-function toolkit feeds directly into Norris, Durrett, Le Gall, mathematical finance, and stochastic-process work. These are the actual chapter topics, not merely generic “useful mathematics.” [TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Risks:** regular conditional probabilities and versions; the martingale transform and angle-bracket process; three-series; optional sampling for UI martingales; Lévy downward; Kakutani product martingales; the LIL appendices; Kalman–Bucy and harness applications. Some of these have individual formalizations, but that is not whole-book coverage. [Williams TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf); [ATLAS per-theorem audit](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/report.json)

#### 2. J. R. Norris, _Markov Chains_ — best compact, distinct opening

- **Identity:** J. R. Norris, _Markov Chains_, Cambridge University Press, **1997**; first paperback edition 1998, ISBN 9780521633963. The publisher records xvi+237 pages and describes an advanced-undergraduate/master’s text developed from a repeatedly taught Cambridge course; measure theory is deliberately not a prerequisite. [CUP listing/preface](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/markov-chains/A3F966B10633A32C8F06F37158031739/listing); [book front matter](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Teaching evidence:** the preface says the exercises formed the basis of classwork tested over several years; Illinois Math 564 in Fall 2024 listed Norris as the first textbook, ahead of Levin–Peres–Wilmer and Grimmett–Stirzaker. [Norris preface](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf); [Illinois Math 564](http://psdey.web.illinois.edu/Math564FA24.html)
- **TOC scope:** Chapter 1 discrete-time chains; Chapter 2 continuous-time chains I (Q-matrices, Poisson/birth processes, jump chain/holding times, explosion, forward/backward equations, non-minimal chains); Chapter 3 continuous-time chains II; Chapter 4 martingales, potential theory, electrical networks, Brownian motion; Chapter 5 biology, queues, resource management, Markov decision processes, MCMC; Chapter 6 probability/measure appendix. [Complete TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Estimated scope/effort:** **160–230 nodes; large single-book project, with schedule risk**. The mechanical lower bound is 88 theorems + 16 lemmas + 2 corollaries; 60 examples and 11 exercises were excluded, leaving unnumbered definitions to estimate. This is about half to two-thirds of PMA in node count, but not in missing infrastructure. [PDF and estimator basis](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Downstream value:** the book supplies a coherent path from countable-state chains to Poisson processes, queueing networks, MDPs, and MCMC, plus unusually valuable electrical-network and potential-theory material. Its publisher’s Chapter 5 summary explicitly lists those five application areas. [CUP Chapter 5 summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/markov-chains/A3F966B10633A32C8F06F37158031739/listing); [TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Risks:** current Mathlib has useful kernels and hitting times but not a Norris-level chain library; continuous-time non-minimal chains and most applications require new abstractions. The book’s small size is therefore misleading if one expects a wrapper-only project. [Mathlib kernel irreducibility](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Kernel/Irreducible.html); [Mathlib hitting times](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Process/HittingTime.html); [Norris TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)

#### 3. Stein and Shakarchi, _Real Analysis_ — excellent but deceptively hard

- **Identity:** Elias M. Stein and Rami Shakarchi, _Real Analysis: Measure Theory, Integration, and Hilbert Spaces_, Princeton Lectures in Analysis III, **2005**, ISBN 9780691113869. It is the third volume in Princeton’s integrated four-course analysis sequence. [Princeton University Press](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691113869/real-analysis)
- **Teaching/canonical evidence:** the foreword says the series elaborates four one-semester Princeton courses begun in 2000; Johns Hopkins used this exact text for Real Variables in 2017 and Rutgers assigned it in 2019. [Book foreword/TOC](https://www.cmat.edu.uy/~mordecki/courses/medida2013/book.pdf); [JHU course](https://math.jhu.edu/~sire/605_Fall_2017.html); [Rutgers syllabus](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~alexk/2019F501/syllabus.html)
- **TOC scope:** Chapter 1 measure theory; Chapter 2 integration; Chapter 3 differentiation/integration; Chapter 4 Hilbert spaces; Chapter 5 Fourier transform, Hardy space, constant-coefficient PDE, Dirichlet principle; Chapter 6 abstract measure/integration, signed measures, ergodic theorems, spectral theorem; Chapter 7 Hausdorff measure, self-similarity, space-filling curves, Besicovitch sets and the Radon transform. [Complete TOC](https://www.cmat.edu.uy/~mordecki/courses/medida2013/book.pdf)
- **Estimated scope/effort:** **220–320 nodes; multi-year for the whole book**, despite only seven chapters. The lower bound is 37 theorems + 38 lemmas + 32 propositions + 25 corollaries, before unnumbered definitions and constructions. Chapters 5 and 7 contain several research-scale constructions each. [PDF and estimator basis](https://www.cmat.edu.uy/~mordecki/courses/medida2013/book.pdf)
- **Downstream value:** few candidates combine Lebesgue differentiation, Hilbert-space infrastructure, ergodic theory, Hausdorff dimension, and fractal geometry in one teaching order. The publisher explicitly emphasizes that final fractional-dimensional material. [Princeton University Press](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691113869/real-analysis)
- **Risks:** Brunn–Minkowski; Hardy–Littlewood maximal function in the book’s formulation; rectifiable curves, Minkowski content and isoperimetry; Fatou/Hardy-space results; weak PDE and Dirichlet principle; pointwise/maximal ergodic theorems; self-similar sets, Peano curves, Besicovitch sets and Radon transforms. Chapters 4–6 also overlap the already queued functional-analysis programme, so the distinct case is measure/geometric analysis, not another generic Hilbert-space project. [TOC](https://www.cmat.edu.uy/~mordecki/courses/medida2013/book.pdf); [assignment brief](/root/formalize-research/BRIEF.md)

#### 4. Jean-François Le Gall, _Brownian Motion, Martingales, and Stochastic Calculus_ — best advanced long-term target

- **Identity:** Jean-François Le Gall, _Brownian Motion, Martingales, and Stochastic Calculus_, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 274, **first edition, 2016**, hardcover ISBN 9783319310886, eBook ISBN 9783319310893. Use the text together with its publisher erratum. [Springer bibliographic page](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-31089-3); [Springer chapter list including erratum](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/brownian-motion-martingales-and-stochastic-calculus/10075778)
- **Teaching/canonical evidence:** Springer says the material was taught for several years in graduate courses and gives full proof details; Richard Durrett’s MAA review calls it a clear, concise, rigorous introduction for readers already knowing measure theory and conditional expectation. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-31089-3); [Durrett’s MAA review](https://old.maa.org/press/maa-reviews/brownian-motion-martingales-and-stochastic-calculus)
- **TOC scope:** Chapters 1 Gaussian variables/processes; 2 Brownian motion; 3 filtrations/martingales; 4 continuous semimartingales; 5 stochastic integration, Itô, Lévy, time change, BDG, martingale representation, Girsanov/Cameron–Martin; 6 Markov/Feller processes; 7 Brownian motion and PDE; 8 SDEs; 9 local times. [Full chapter/section TOC](https://dokumen.pub/brownian-motion-martingales-and-stochastic-calculus-1nbsped-3319310887-9783319310886-9783319310893.html); [publisher chapter summaries](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/brownian-motion-martingales-and-stochastic-calculus/10075778)
- **Estimated scope/effort:** **200–280 nodes; multi-year**. The mechanical count is already 180 labels including 40 definitions, before unnumbered constructions and results; the difficulty is concentrated in general semimartingales and local times. [PDF and estimator basis](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/server/api/core/bitstreams/f97859c7-d5d3-439c-8f4c-c2b6aeb8155c/content)
- **Downstream value:** this is the cleanest canonical route to reusable continuous-semimartingale integration, SDE/Markov-process connections, Feynman–Kac/Dirichlet methods, and Tanaka/local-time infrastructure, with mathematical finance an explicit publisher-described application. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-31089-3)
- **Risks:** current Lean results cover Brownian construction and restricted Itô calculus, not this book’s full generality. The hard residue includes quadratic variation/brackets, general continuous local martingales, BDG and representation, Girsanov, Lévy processes/continuous-state branching, PDE, strong SDE/Markov results, and local time/Tanaka/Trotter. [BrownianMotion project](https://github.com/RemyDegenne/brownian-motion); [restricted Itô development](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15089); [Le Gall TOC](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/brownian-motion-martingales-and-stochastic-calculus/10075778)

#### 5. Rick Durrett, _Probability: Theory and Examples_ — comprehensive multi-year probability spine

- **Identity:** Rick Durrett, _Probability: Theory and Examples_, **fifth edition, 2019**, Cambridge University Press, hardback ISBN 9781108473682; author’s Version 5 dated 11 January 2019. [Author PDF](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf); [Cambridge contents DOI page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/probability/contents/20F44A88DE9216C73D82C0CD8F97A5C5)
- **Teaching/canonical evidence:** the preface says the book emphasizes application-relevant results through roughly 200 examples and almost 450 exercises; MIT says its graduate course more or less follows Durrett, and UT Austin calls it widely used for graduate probability. [Author preface](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf); [MIT 18.175](https://math.mit.edu/~sheffield/fall2016math175.html); [UT Austin syllabus](https://www.ices.utexas.edu/media/academics/course/229_Syllabus_CSE_384K_Zitkovic.pdf)
- **TOC scope:** Chapter 1 measure theory; 2 laws of large numbers, renewal and large deviations; 3 CLTs, stable/infinitely divisible laws and Poisson processes; 4 martingales; 5 Markov chains including general state space; 6 Birkhoff and subadditive ergodic theorems; 7 Brownian motion and Itô; 8 Donsker, martingale/stationary CLTs, empirical distributions, Brownian bridge and LIL; 9 multidimensional Brownian motion, heat/Feynman–Kac/Dirichlet/Green/Poisson/Schrödinger equations; measure-theory appendix. [Author TOC](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf)
- **Estimated scope/effort:** **520–650 nodes; multi-year**. The mechanical count is 370 theorems + 88 lemmas + 3 corollaries = 461, plus an allowance for unnumbered definitions; the 195 examples are excluded. [Durrett PDF mirror used for the count](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/MATH493/Durrett%20Probability%202019.pdf)
- **Downstream value:** this is the broadest coherent graduate-probability source in the shortlist and would consolidate laws of large numbers, limit theory, Markov chains, ergodic theory, Brownian motion, and probabilistic PDE rather than producing isolated theorem files. [Author TOC](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf)
- **Risks:** the starred material is not optional under complete coverage: stable laws, infinite divisibility, local limits, general-state Markov chains, subadditive ergodic theory, Donsker/empirical processes, LIL and multidimensional PDE all matter. The author also records that Lemma 3.4.19 wrongly says the distributions have mean zero (their means do not exist), although the conclusion survives because they are differentiable at zero; an errata policy is essential for faithful statements. [TOC and author’s erratum note](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf)

#### 6. Gerald B. Folland, _Real Analysis_ — broad analysis integration, only after overlap review

- **Identity:** Gerald B. Folland, _Real Analysis: Modern Techniques and Their Applications_, **second edition, 1999**, Wiley-Interscience, ISBN 0471317160 / 9780471317166. [Book front matter/TOC](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~matchbox/[Gerald_B._Folland]_Real_Analysis__Modern_Techniq(1).pdf); [Library of Congress TOC](https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/onix03/98037260.html)
- **Teaching/canonical evidence:** the preface explicitly targets a graduate analysis course and says Chapters 1–7 cover the core of most graduate curricula; Virginia’s 2023 analysis general-exam syllabus and Hawaii’s qualifying-exam page both use Folland. [Folland preface](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~matchbox/[Gerald_B._Folland]_Real_Analysis__Modern_Techniq(1).pdf); [UVA syllabus](https://math.virginia.edu/graduate/docs/Syllabus%20for%20Analysis%20General%20Exam%202.pdf); [Hawaii qualifying exams](https://math.hawaii.edu/wordpress/qualifying-exams-in-analysis/)
- **TOC scope:** prologue; Chapters 1 measures, 2 integration, 3 signed measures/differentiation, 4 point-set topology, 5 functional analysis, 6 Lp spaces, 7 Radon measures, 8 Fourier analysis, 9 distributions, 10 probability, 11 Haar/Hausdorff/self-similarity/integration on manifolds. [Complete TOC](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~matchbox/[Gerald_B._Folland]_Real_Analysis__Modern_Techniq(1).pdf)
- **Estimated scope/effort:** **380–480 nodes; multi-year**. The mechanical lower bound is 84 theorems + 153 propositions + 36 lemmas + 39 corollaries = 312 before definitions. [Folland PDF and estimator basis](https://ia802900.us.archive.org/17/items/real-analysis-textbooks/Real%20Analysis%20-%20G.%20B.%20Folland.pdf)
- **Downstream value:** its distinctive return is a single ordered bridge from abstract measure/topology to Radon measures, Fourier/distribution theory, probability, Haar measure, and Hausdorff/self-similar sets. [TOC](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~matchbox/[Gerald_B._Folland]_Real_Analysis__Modern_Techniq(1).pdf)
- **Risks:** major overlap with the already queued Rudin _Real and Complex Analysis_ and _Functional Analysis_, plus Stein–Shakarchi. The unique residual is mainly Chapters 7–11; interpolation/weak Lp, distribution theory, self-similarity, and manifold integration are substantial builds. [Folland TOC](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~matchbox/[Gerald_B._Folland]_Real_Analysis__Modern_Techniq(1).pdf); [assignment brief](/root/formalize-research/BRIEF.md)

#### Attractive rejects

- **Achim Klenke, _Probability Theory: A Comprehensive Course_, third edition, 2020:** a 26-chapter, 716-page comprehensive text, but ReasBook already has all 26 chapter directories, 815,853 Lean lines and project-reported 99.29% theorem/lemma proof completion, with 150 declarations still containing `sorry`; it is excluded from the current release profile, so it is not strict-complete, but it is a poor new-book choice relative to genuinely open targets. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-030-56402-5); [ReasBook Klenke statistics](https://github.com/optpku/ReasBook/tree/v4.29.0/ReasBook/Books/ProbabilityTheory_Klenke_2020); [26 chapter directories](https://github.com/optpku/ReasBook/tree/v4.29.0/ReasBook/Books/ProbabilityTheory_Klenke_2020/Items); [ReasBook catalog](https://github.com/optpku/ReasBook/)
- **Terence Tao, _Analysis I_ and _Analysis II_:** Tao’s own _Analysis I_ companion covers all main chapters while intentionally leaving exercises as `sorry`; ReasBook’s _Analysis II_ (fourth edition, 2022) is also near-complete, with its catalog reporting only a few placeholders depending on the version entry. This is an existing-coverage reject, not a claim that every exercise meets the requested strict standard. [Tao _Analysis I_ repository](https://github.com/teorth/analysis); [ReasBook _Analysis II_](https://github.com/optpku/ReasBook/tree/v4.30.0/ReasBook/Books/Analysis2_Tao_2022); [ReasBook catalog](https://github.com/optpku/ReasBook/)
- **Patrick Billingsley, _Probability and Measure_, third edition, 1995 / Anniversary Edition, 2012:** still a comprehensive classic, but the strongest new-programme reasons mostly duplicate Williams (measure/martingales) and Durrett (limit theory, Brownian motion and ergodic theory). My label audit found 207 numbered theorems plus 212 examples, so a rough main-text range is **280–380 definition/theorem nodes**, larger if examples are included; it is not a small substitute for Durrett. Reject for low distinct marginal value, not for lack of canonical status and not for an alleged complete formalization. [Wiley Anniversary Edition description](https://www.wiley.com/en-in/Probability+and+Measure%2C+Anniversary+Edition-p-9781118341919); [Billingsley PDF used for label audit](https://www.colorado.edu/amath/sites/default/files/attached-files/billingsley.pdf); [Durrett TOC](https://sites.math.duke.edu/~rtd/PTE/PTE5_011119.pdf); [Williams TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Bernt Øksendal, _Stochastic Differential Equations: An Introduction with Applications_, sixth edition, 2003:** 12 chapters plus appendices, roughly **300–420 nodes** by section/label density, but a less attractive strict target than Le Gall. The preface deliberately accepts some basic results without proof; for example Kolmogorov’s extension and continuity theorems point elsewhere for proofs. Complete faithful formalization would therefore require substantial proof reconstruction beyond the text, in addition to filtering, optimal stopping, stochastic control and finance applications. Le Gall supplies fuller proofs and a more coherent core. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-14394-6); [Øksendal preface and TOC](https://people.stat.ucla.edu/ywu/research/documents/StochasticDifferentialEquations.pdf); [Le Gall publisher page](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-31089-3)

### Inferences

- **Recommended order:** Williams first; Norris second when the team is ready to build a real chain API rather than merely restate finite-matrix results; Stein–Shakarchi third if geometric measure/fractal material is strategically wanted; Le Gall as the advanced stochastic-calculus programme; Durrett and Folland only as explicitly multi-year commitments. These judgements follow from the cited scopes and the readiness evidence below, not from author fame.
- **Best value per node:** Williams and Norris. **Best long-term infrastructure:** Le Gall. **Best broad analysis source:** Stein–Shakarchi before Folland, because Folland has more direct overlap with the queued Rudin books. [Williams TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf); [Norris TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf); [Folland TOC](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~matchbox/[Gerald_B._Folland]_Real_Analysis__Modern_Techniq(1).pdf); [assignment brief](/root/formalize-research/BRIEF.md)
- **Do not split a book merely to make it look easy.** A sensible implementation can land reusable prerequisite libraries first, but “done” still requires every source definition and theorem in order, including appendices and starred sections, with faithful statements and no `sorry`. This is the coordinator’s stated target standard. [Assignment brief](/root/formalize-research/BRIEF.md)

### Gaps

- No candidate has a hand-audited node inventory. The ranges are defensible estimates from the cited mechanical counts, not completion metrics. Williams and Billingsley are the least precise because many definitions are embedded in prose. A pre-project pass must number every source item and settle whether exercises are in scope.
- Teaching evidence is a sample of official course pages and the authors’ own class-testing statements, not a systematic adoption survey. It is sufficient to reject “merely famous,” not to rank institutions or estimate adoption frequency.

## What exact existing projects cover all or substantial parts, with repo/paper/site URLs and activity/completeness evidence?

### Takeaway

I found **no complete or near-complete ordered formalization of any of the six recommended books** in Lean or another proof assistant. That is an **unverified negative**, not an existence proof. Klenke and Tao are different: they already have near-complete Lean book projects. ATLAS, BrownianMotion, MarkovProcess, Isabelle AFP, and Coq MathComp-Analysis provide substantial components, but none may be described as whole-book coverage.

### Cited Findings

#### Cross-project checks and what “complete” means here

- **ATLAS v1:** 26 books, 2,855/4,007 selected source statements formalized (71.3%); its own paper says explicitly that **none of the books is fully formalized**. The archived v1 remains available while v2 is in development. [ATLAS v1 statistics](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/tree/main/v1); [ATLAS root/status](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean); [paper, especially §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1)
- ATLAS’s `RealAnalysis` is introductory real-analysis material, not Stein–Shakarchi or Folland; its `TheoryOfProbability` follows MIT 18.175 lecture notes, not Durrett’s whole book. MIT says the course more or less follows Durrett, so this is valuable **related** coverage, not evidence of Durrett completion. [ATLAS `TheoryOfProbability` targets](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/targets.yaml); [MIT course](https://math.mit.edu/~sheffield/fall2016math175.html); [ATLAS `RealAnalysis` targets](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/RealAnalysis/targets.yaml)
- I parsed ATLAS’s per-theorem JSON rather than treating `targets.yaml` as proofs. Kolmogorov three-series, Skorokhod representation and backwards martingales are marked failed and carry `sorryAx`; Doob decomposition is marked failed on review; Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem passes ATLAS’s own review but still carries `sorryAx`; regular conditional probabilities and the Markov-chain construction pass without `sorryAx`. Therefore ATLAS does not meet the programme’s zero-`sorry` gate even for several individual headline theorems. [Raw per-theorem report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/report.json)
- **ReasBook:** its current catalog lists _Probability Theory: A Comprehensive Course_ as “source only (excluded from the current release profile)” and _Analysis II_ as an active book. [ReasBook catalog](https://github.com/optpku/ReasBook/)
- **formal-conjectures:** it is a repository of formalized **open conjecture statements**, not a textbook-coverage project. ## What would actually have to be built before each book?

### Takeaway

Williams can reuse much of Mathlib immediately, but still needs clean bridges and several hard martingale/LIL components. Norris needs a genuine discrete- and continuous-time chain library. Stein–Shakarchi and Folland need geometric/functional-analysis infrastructure beyond their comfortable measure-theory beginnings; Le Gall and Durrett need substantial stochastic-process machinery that current projects only partially supply.

### Inferences

#### Top candidate 1 — Williams, chapter-by-chapter implementation sketch

The implementation should preserve Williams’s source numbering but create shared bridge modules first: textbook probability spaces vs `Measure`, random variables vs measurable functions, expectation vs Bochner integration, and source functions vs almost-everywhere equivalence classes. The book explicitly chooses genuine functions rather than equivalence classes, so conditional-expectation versions and a.e. statements need deliberate review. [Williams terminology note](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)

- **Chapter 0, branching-process example:** define offspring PMFs, generating functions, the doubly indexed independent family and recursive generation sizes; formalize extinction as a measurable event, the fixed-point characterization, and the normalized martingale. **Hard point:** the chapter deliberately anticipates conditional expectation and martingale convergence, so implement its statements early but allow a documented dependency loop back after Chapters 9–14 rather than adding unproved axioms. [Chapter 0](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Chapter 1, measure spaces:** bridge algebras/σ-algebras, set functions, π-system uniqueness, Carathéodory extension, Lebesgue measure on `(0,1]`, and monotone-convergence properties to `MeasurableSpace`, `OuterMeasure`, `Measure`, and extension lemmas. **Hard point:** exact semiring/interval conventions and the deferred proofs in A1. [Chapter 1](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Chapter 2, events:** model probability triples, almost-sure events, limsup/liminf of events and BC1. **Hard point:** translating `En i.o.`/`En ev.` into `Filter`/`limsup` idioms without changing the event-level statements. Use the first Borel–Cantelli theorem identified in [`Martingale.BorelCantelli`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/BorelCantelli.html).
- **Chapter 3, random variables:** measurability, generated σ-algebras, laws/distribution functions, existence of a variable with a prescribed CDF, Skorokhod representation, and the monotone-class theorem. **Hard point:** Skorokhod must be built cleanly; ATLAS’s corresponding target fails with `sorryAx`. [ATLAS report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/report.json)
- **Chapter 4, independence:** π-system formulations, BC2, coin-tossing models, IID families, tail σ-algebras and Kolmogorov’s 0–1 law. **Hard point:** constructing infinite independent families and reconciling pairwise/mutual independence with generated σ-algebras. [Williams TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Chapter 5, integration:** simple functions, nonnegative integration, monotone convergence, Fatou, integrable functions, dominated convergence, Scheffé, UI preview, and density measures. **Hard point:** prove equivalence between the textbook’s staged definition and `lintegral`/Bochner `integral`, then reuse rather than reprove the convergence stack. The same bridge strategy is recommended by the Shum probability-book project. [Shum paper §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27298v1)
- **Chapter 6, expectation:** expectation and convergence, Markov, Jensen, Lp norms, Schwarz/covariance, completeness, orthogonal projection and Hölder. **Hard point:** integrability hypotheses and source use of `L^p` vs Mathlib’s quotient-space `Lp`; do not let total-function fallback values silently strengthen finite-mean claims. [Shum’s variance case study](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27298v1)
- **Chapter 7, easy strong law:** independence-means-multiply, the first strong law, Chebyshev and Weierstrass approximation. **Hard point:** deriving Williams’s mutual-independence formulation from Etemadi’s pairwise-independence `ProbabilityTheory.strong_law_ae`, with exact indexing and integrability. [Strong law API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/StrongLaw.html)
- **Chapter 8, product measure:** product σ-algebras/measures, Fubini, joint laws, independence, finite and infinite products. **Hard point:** infinite-product probability triples and existence of joint laws; use the trajectory/projective-family architecture exposed by [`Kernel.IonescuTulcea.Traj`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Kernel/IonescuTulcea/Traj.html), but check its hypotheses against Williams’s statement.
- **Chapter 9, conditional expectation:** Kolmogorov definition/existence, least-squares prediction, properties, regular conditional probabilities/densities and independence. **Hard point:** versions and regular conditional distributions, not merely `condExp` as an L² projection. Mathlib has conditional-expectation and disintegration APIs, while ATLAS has a clean regular-conditional-probability target worth reviewing. [Mathlib conditional expectation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/ConditionalExpectation.html); [ATLAS report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/report.json)
- **Chapter 10, martingales:** filtered spaces, adapted/previsible processes, discrete stochastic integral, stopping times, stopped processes, optional stopping, random-walk hitting times and superharmonic functions. **Hard point:** the martingale transform `(A · M)_n` and hitting-time-specific algebra. A formal-mathfin audit says its local transform fills a Mathlib gap, offering a reviewable starting point rather than a book solution. [Bridge audit](https://github.com/formal-applied-math/formal-mathfin/blob/main/docs/bridges.md); [optional stopping API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/OptionalStopping.html)
- **Chapter 11, convergence theorem:** upcrossings, Doob’s upcrossing lemma and forward convergence. **Hard point:** align Williams’s real-valued process and limit with `Filtration.limitProcess` and `Submartingale.ae_tendsto_limitProcess`; this should be a short bridge if statements remain faithful. [Convergence API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/Convergence.html)
- **Chapter 12, L²-bounded martingales:** orthogonality, independent centered sums, symmetrization, three-series, Cesàro/Kronecker, variance-constrained SLLN, truncation, Kolmogorov SLLN, Doob decomposition, angle brackets and generalized Borel–Cantelli. **Hard point:** three-series and angle-bracket machinery are the first major schedule blockers; ATLAS’s three-series has `sorryAx`, while its Doob decomposition fails review. [ATLAS report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/report.json); [generalized Borel–Cantelli](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/BorelCantelli.html)
- **Chapter 13, uniform integrability:** absolute-continuity characterization, UI families, conditional-expectation UI, convergence in probability, bounded convergence and the L¹ criterion. **Hard point:** bridge the book’s UI definition to `MeasureTheory.uniformIntegrable_iff` and avoid confusing a.e., L¹ and in-probability limits. [Convergence/UI discussion in Mathlib](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/Convergence.html)

Build small verified interfaces for each and review every hidden independence/integrability assumption; do not count mathematical-finance library breadth as coverage of these exact statements. [Williams Chapter 15 TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf); [formal-mathfin bridge audit](https://github.com/formal-applied-math/formal-mathfin/blob/main/docs/bridges.md)
- **Chapter 16, characteristic functions:** definitions, elementary properties, atoms, Lévy inversion and the distribution table. **Hard point:** exact Fourier constants and inversion hypotheses; reuse [`Analysis.Fourier.Inversion`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Fourier/Inversion.html) only after proving the probability-measure normalization bridge.
- **Chapter 17, weak convergence:** elegant/practical formulations, Skorokhod representation, sequential compactness and tightness. **Hard point:** Helly/Skorokhod in the source’s distribution-function language, related to but not identical with Prokhorov’s measure-space statement. [Prokhorov API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Prokhorov.html); [failed ATLAS Skorokhod target](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/report.json)
- **Chapter 18, CLT:** Lévy convergence, little/big-O estimates, the CLT and the CF proof of Lemma 12.4. **Hard point:** reconcile the book’s mean/variance normalization with `ProbabilityMeasure.tendsto_iff_tendsto_charFun` and the two CLT statements in Mathlib; likely one of the more contained chapters. [Lévy convergence](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/LevyConvergence.html); [CLT](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/CentralLimitTheorem.html)
- **Appendices A1/A3/A5/A9/A13/A14/A16:** complete non-measurable-set/Vitali construction, Dynkin and outer-measure details, monotone class, infinite products, convergence-mode relations, stopped σ-algebras/optional sampling, and differentiation under the integral. **Hard points:** Vitali as a named construction and UI optional sampling; a recent probability-book project also identifies named Vitali sets and Riemann–Stieltjes as library gaps, though that is not evidence of their current absence everywhere. [Shum paper §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27298v1); [Williams appendix TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)
- **Appendix A4:** Kolmogorov and Strassen LIL plus the Markov-chain model. **Hard point:** this is the largest isolated appendix risk and should be scheduled as its own milestone, not discovered after all other chapters are “done.” [A4 TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)

#### Top candidate 2 — Norris, chapter-by-chapter implementation sketch

First build a common countable-state interface with two presentation layers: Norris’s elementary stochastic matrices/row measures and Mathlib’s kernels. Use `traj`/`trajMeasure` for the canonical infinite-path law and `hittingBtwn`/`hittingAfter` for times; prove the finite-trajectory product formula once and derive both elementary and kernel formulations. [Ionescu–Tulcea API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Kernel/IonescuTulcea/Traj.html); [hitting-time API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Process/HittingTime.html)

- **Chapter 1.1–1.2, definitions and class structure:** Markov(λ,P), finite-dimensional path probabilities, n-step transitions, communicating/closed classes, irreducibility and period. **Hard point:** countable index types and infinite row sums vs finite `Matrix.rowStochastic`; do not accidentally restrict the whole book to finite state spaces. [Norris Chapter 1](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf); [finite matrix API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Stochastic.html)
- **Chapter 1.3–1.4, hitting/absorption and strong Markov:** hitting probabilities, expected hitting times, minimal nonnegative solutions of the associated linear systems, and restart at a stopping time. **Hard point:** proving minimality and handling infinite expected times faithfully; the strong Markov proof can use the canonical trajectory law rather than conditional probabilities with zero denominators. Hölzl’s Isabelle strong-Markov development is a useful non-Lean comparison, not reusable Lean coverage. [Hölzl paper](https://www.cs.vu.nl/~jhl890/pub/hoelzl2017markovprocesses.pdf)
- **Chapter 1.5–1.6, recurrence/transience and random walks:** return-time distributions, expected number of visits, recurrence criteria, and recurrence/transience of random walks. **Hard point:** excursions, monotone limits and Stirling asymptotics in the appendix; these are analytical, not just matrix algebra. [Norris TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 1.7–1.10, invariant distributions, equilibrium, reversal and ergodic averages:** invariant measures, positive recurrence, return-time identity, convergence by coupling, detailed balance/time reversal and long-run averages. **Hard point:** construct and compare couplings on a common probability space, then derive total-variation convergence without assuming a finite state space. AFP’s stationary-distribution work and Prove2Me’s finite-chain mission are related components, not Norris’s countable-state proofs. [AFP stochastic matrices](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Stochastic_Matrices.html); [Prove2Me finite-chain mission](https://prove2.me/collections/markov-chains-and-mixing-times)
- **Chapter 1.11–1.12, recurrence relations and n! asymptotics:** implement the specialized solvers and Stirling bounds needed by the random-walk arguments. **Hard point:** keep asymptotic constants and limit directions identical to the text. [Norris appendices](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 2.1–2.3, Q-matrices, continuous-time processes and exponentials:** Q-matrices/exponentials, right-continuous jump processes, memoryless exponential holding times, and minima of independent exponentials. **Hard point:** semigroup/matrix-exponential API and total existence of independent exponential families; the exponential PDF alone is only a small prerequisite. [Exponential distribution API](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Distributions/Exponential.html)
- **Chapter 2.4–2.5, Poisson and birth processes:** construct counting paths, stationary independent increments, thinning/superposition, birth rates and first explosion. **Hard point:** path-space measurability and identifying several equivalent definitions rather than merely defining a Poisson random variable. [Norris Chapter 2 TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 2.6–2.7, jump chain/holding times and explosion:** construct the minimal process from a jump chain and independent holding rates; characterize explosion through jump times and birth-process criteria. **Hard point:** this is the central continuous-time construction. Hölzl’s Isabelle jump-and-hold formalization supplies a comparison architecture, but Norris requires the full countable/minimal theory and its exact statements. [Hölzl paper](https://www.cs.vu.nl/~jhl890/pub/hoelzl2017markovprocesses.pdf)
- **Chapter 2.8–2.10, forward/backward equations, non-minimal chains and matrix appendix:** derive both equations from the construction, treat unbounded rates correctly, describe processes beyond the explosion time, and prove the matrix-exponential lemmas. **Hard point:** non-minimal chains make “the Q-matrix determines everything” false in general; hypotheses and path conventions need hostile review. [Norris Chapter 2](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 3, continuous-time chains II:** transport class structure, hitting, recurrence, invariant measures, convergence, time reversal and ergodic averages from the jump chain to real time. **Hard point:** holding-time integrals, explosion-aware statements, and the distinction between jump-chain recurrence and continuous-time recurrence. [Norris Chapter 3 TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 4.1–4.2, martingales and potential theory:** reuse the Williams martingale layer; define harmonic/superharmonic functions, potentials and Green’s functions for chains. **Hard point:** signed infinite sums and boundary/recurrence assumptions, not the basic martingale definition. [Norris Chapter 4 summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/markov-chains/A3F966B10633A32C8F06F37158031739/listing)
- **Chapter 4.3, electrical networks:** flows, conductances, Kirchhoff laws, effective resistance, energy, variational principles and the recurrence connection. **Hard point:** almost an independent graph/potential-theory library; high downstream value but a separate work package. [Norris Chapter 4 TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 4.4, Brownian motion:** connect the elementary introduction to the constructed Brownian process and basic path properties. **Hard point:** use the existing Brownian construction but prove Norris’s exact statements; BrownianMotion’s paper explicitly leaves some strong-Markov/martingale properties to future work at publication time. [BrownianMotion paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20118v2); [Mathlib Brownian basics](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/BrownianMotion/Basic.html)
- **Chapter 5.1, biological models:** formalize branching/epidemic/genetic examples and their hitting/stationary calculations. **Hard point:** each model needs explicit finite/countable state and rate definitions; examples must not silently become new axioms. [CUP Chapter 5 summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/markov-chains/A3F966B10633A32C8F06F37158031739/listing)
- **Chapter 5.2, queues and queueing networks:** M/M/1 and network stationary distributions, arrival/departure processes and performance quantities. **Hard point:** product-form stationary laws and infinite linear systems; reuse, rather than duplicate, the CTMC kernel semantics. [Norris Chapter 5 TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 5.3, resource management:** inventory/replacement/reward models and optimal long-run behaviour. **Hard point:** model-specific reward expectation and almost-sure finiteness; these are separate proof obligations, not illustrative comments. [Norris Chapter 5 TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 5.4, Markov decision processes:** policies, expected discounted/average rewards, optimality equations and optimal stationary policies. **Hard point:** a new dynamic-programming layer over the chain semantics; the AFP includes MDPs but does not provide Lean code or Norris’s exact chapter. [AFP Markov Models](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Markov_Models.html)
- **Chapter 5.5, MCMC:** Metropolis–Hastings/Gibbs constructions, detailed balance and convergence to target laws. **Hard point:** correctness of the generated transition kernel and target distribution before invoking Chapter 1’s convergence theorem; this is one of the highest-value reusable outputs. [Norris Chapter 5 TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
- **Chapter 6, probability and measure appendix:** countable sets/sums, measure facts, expectation, monotone convergence/Fubini, stopping/strong Markov and uniqueness/independence. **Hard point:** mostly bridge work, but it should be done early so the main text has no hidden informal prerequisites. [Norris appendix TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)
