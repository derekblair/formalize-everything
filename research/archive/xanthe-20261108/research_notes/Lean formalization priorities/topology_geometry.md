# Topology and geometry book candidates for a Lean 4 complete-coverage programme

## Which books offer high downstream value with prerequisites mostly present?

### Takeaway

The best two candidates for a potential top-three slot are **Hatcher, _Algebraic Topology_** (largest downstream payoff, but a large/multi-year project) and **Milnor, _Topology from the Differentiable Viewpoint_** (small enough for a genuine single-book project, with several hard infrastructure packages). **Munkres, _Topology_**, is third: it is foundational and mostly ready in point-set topology, but its algebraic-topology half is not, and a substantial non-Lean formalization of Chapters 2–8 already exists. Lee is exceptionally valuable but too large for the first complete-coverage attempt; Guillemin–Pollack and do Carmo are attractive staged projects rather than prerequisites-mostly-present books.

The decisive distinction is between **core Mathlib**, **external Lean theorem libraries**, and **whole-book formalizations**. Current core Mathlib already has substantial algebraic-topology and manifold infrastructure. External Lean projects now supply selected Sard/transversality/Morse and advanced geometry results, but none of the six books below was verified here as completely formalized in Lean. Negative whole-book claims are explicitly marked as unverified searches, not proofs of nonexistence.

### Cited Findings

**Counting and acceptance convention used below.** A node means a main-text definition, theorem, proposition, lemma, or corollary; examples, exercises, problems, and bibliographic material are excluded unless explicitly noted. Ranges are analyst estimates, not publisher counts, and are calibrated against the supplied Rudin PMA ≈330-node yardstick. The strict target is every selected node in book order, zero `sorry`/`admit`/custom axioms, faithful statements, standard Lean axioms only, and hostile statement review. In particular, `theorem_wanted` is not an acceptable finished proof; ATLAS separates deferred `WantedExt` declarations from its proved `MathlibExt` library ([MathlibExt policy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/README.md), [WantedExt policy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/WantedExt/README.md)).

#### 1) Allen Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology* — first choice for downstream value; large/multi-year

- **Exact text and canonical/teaching status.** Use Hatcher's *Algebraic Topology*, Cambridge University Press, 2002, preferably the author's corrected online edition and a recorded revision date. Hatcher's own page identifies the 2002 paperback and free electronic version, and warns that post-2015 reprintings changed some theorem numbering ([author book page](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html)). A contemporary Cambridge review calls it a carefully written, detailed, self-contained introduction to homotopy groups, homology, and cohomology ([review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/9F3482890F390477D49007403D5E701F/S0013091503214620a.pdf/hatcher-aalgebraic-topology-cambridge-university-press-2002-556-pp-0-521-79540-0-softback-pound2095-0-521-79160-x-hardback-pound60.pdf)); it is also used as the main text in university algebraic-topology courses ([Kansas Math 821](https://jeremymartinmath.github.io/courses/math821-S14/index.html)).
- **TOC and scope.** Chapter 0 is geometric notions; Chapter 1 is the fundamental group and covering spaces; Chapter 2 is homology; Chapter 3 is cohomology; Chapter 4 is homotopy theory; there is an appendix on CW topology and the compact-open topology. Each main chapter has additional topics, which must not silently be dropped from a whole-book target ([author chapter list](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATchapters.html), [Library of Congress detailed TOC](https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/cam022/00065166.html)).
- **Node/effort estimate.** A text scan of the author PDF found 244 unique numbered theorem/proposition/lemma/corollary labels, before unnumbered definitions and other main-text nodes. With inline definitions and the additional topics, a defensible estimate is **400–550 nodes** (roughly 250–350 for only the core sections), hence **large single-book to multi-year**, not a PMA-sized weekend project. This is an estimate based on [the author PDF](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf), not a certified inventory; Hatcher himself says the additional topics amount to nearly half the book ([preface](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf)).
- **Lean status.** No complete or near-complete whole-book Lean formalization was found. Prove2Me has individually proved Hatcher items, including the disk Brouwer theorem ([Theorem 1.9](https://prove2.me/theorems/ad651f40-9e18-4e53-af8f-bbd8968a3bcd)), van Kampen surjectivity ([Theorem 1.20 first part](https://prove2.me/theorems/714a9c26-c893-4a99-ac95-b7d4e6e2d0e8)), the lifting criterion ([Proposition 1.33](https://prove2.me/theorems/3fe20d40-c21b-4244-922b-87ba49a1bbd1)), and a deck-group result ([Proposition 1.39(b)](https://prove2.me/theorems/452e9e92-ce8b-4c6c-8447-c0fcf55e631b)); these are selected theorem missions, not Chapters 0–4 in order. ATLAS's archived `AlgebraicTopologyI` is an MIT OCW notes project with 110/171 targets formalized, not Hatcher, and ATLAS explicitly says none of its 26 books is fully formalized ([ATLAS v1 statistics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/README.md), [paper limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1)). Lean Pool's Hatcher-derived entries include fundamental groups of finite connected graphs and Whitehead's theorem for CW complexes, again selected results rather than the book ([Lean Pool table](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25199v1)). No disqualifying complete Lean whole-book project was established.
- **Other proof assistants.** HOL Light has a documented singular-homology development, not a Hatcher whole-book formalization ([BIRS cohomology review](https://www.birs.ca/files//workshops/Juniper/23w5124/report23w5124.pdf)). Isabelle AFP has the fundamental group of the circle and a classical Seifert–van Kampen development ([circle](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Fundamental_Group_Circle.html), [Seifert–van Kampen](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Seifert-Van-Kampen.html)); these cover selected Hatcher topics, not the whole text. Coq HoTT covers substantial *synthetic* homotopy theory, but that is a different foundational formulation, not a classical Hatcher book formalization ([HoTT library paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04591)). The BIRS review also records a synthetic simplicial/persistent-homology development in Coq, not a classical Hatcher whole-book project ([BIRS review](https://www.birs.ca/files//workshops/Juniper/23w5124/report23w5124.pdf)). No complete non-Lean Hatcher book was found; see the dated absence protocol below.
- **Mathlib readiness.** Useful current modules include `Mathlib.Topology.CWComplex` (relative/classical CW structures), `Mathlib.AlgebraicTopology.FundamentalGroupoid` and `FundamentalGroup`, `Mathlib.Topology.Covering`, `Mathlib.Topology.Homotopy.HomotopyGroup`, `Mathlib.AlgebraicTopology.SingularHomology` (basic, degree zero, homotopy invariance), and `Mathlib.AlgebraicTopology.EilenbergSteenrod` (an interface for homology theories) ([CW docs](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/CWComplex/Classical/Basic.html), [fundamental groupoid](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/FundamentalGroupoid/Basic.html), [covering maps](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Covering/Basic.html), [homotopy groups](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Homotopy/HomotopyGroup.html), [singular homology](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/SingularHomology/Basic.html), [homotopy invariance](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/SingularHomology/HomotopyInvariance.html), [Eilenberg–Steenrod interface](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/EilenbergSteenrod.html)). The current homotopy-group file itself lists missing sphere computations and path-induced homomorphisms as TODOs ([docs](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Homotopy/HomotopyGroup.html)).
- **Biggest actual gaps.** Relative singular homology, excision, Mayer–Vietoris, cellular-homology comparison, singular cohomology and cup/cap products, universal coefficients and Künneth formulas, Poincaré duality, Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems, and the fibration/Postnikov/obstruction/spectra material are not supplied merely by the existing basic singular-homology files. Tau Ceti's roadmap independently enumerates almost exactly these missing pieces as work still to build ([roadmap, stages 1–8](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)); it is a roadmap, not evidence of completion.
- **Downstream value.** This is the strongest investment in reusable algebraic-topology infrastructure: it would serve manifold topology, geometric group theory, algebraic geometry, persistent/computational topology, and later differential-topology books. The value judgment follows from the book's unusually broad core-plus-additional-topics scope ([TOC](https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/cam022/00065166.html)) and the explicitly reusable map-level goals in the [Tau Ceti roadmap](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md).
- **Risks.** The book is much larger than its four core chapters suggest; definitions are often inline; naturality, signs, coefficient generality, CW-versus-∆-complex conventions, and orientation must be reviewed hostily. The author's correction/renumbering notice makes a fixed source revision essential ([author page](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html)). Existing selected proofs cannot be used as evidence of complete coverage.

**Chapter-by-chapter formalization sketch (top-3 use).**

1. **Chapter 0, geometric notions.** Formalize homotopy/homotopy type, deformation retracts, cell complexes, suspensions/cones, mapping cylinders, and the homotopy extension property. Reuse `Topology.CWComplex` where faithful, but write an explicit bridge for Hatcher's ∆-complexes and quotient constructions. Hard points are quotient-topology coherence, attaching maps, and HEP—not a supposed absence of basic homotopy definitions ([Hatcher Ch. 0 TOC](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATchapters.html), [Mathlib CW](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/CWComplex/Classical/Basic.html)).
2. **Chapter 1, fundamental group.** Do paths and homotopy, induced maps, the circle computation, van Kampen and applications, covering spaces/lifting/classification/deck actions, then graphs/free groups and `K(G,1)` topics. Existing groupoid/covering APIs help; the hard points are the circle's exact generator, the general van Kampen colimit and its based corollaries, universal-cover existence, and subgroup classification. Prove2Me's circle, van Kampen-surjectivity, lifting, and deck results are useful checkpoints but do not discharge the chapter ([Hatcher Ch. 1](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch1.pdf), [Prove2Me examples](https://prove2.me/theorems/714a9c26-c893-4a99-ac95-b7d4e6e2d0e8)).
3. **Chapter 2, homology.** Build ∆/simplicial and singular chains, homotopy invariance, relative complexes, exact sequences and excision, then degree, cellular homology, Mayer–Vietoris, coefficients, the Eilenberg–Steenrod viewpoint, and the additional `H_1`/classical-applications/simplicial-approximation topics. Basic singular homology and homotopy invariance are present; barycentric subdivision, relative excision, cellular comparison, and natural induced maps are the first major new field-scale work packages ([Hatcher Ch. 2](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch2.pdf), [Mathlib singular homology](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/SingularHomology/Basic.html), [Tau Ceti Stages 2–4](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)).
4. **Chapter 3, cohomology.** Add cochains, universal coefficients, cup products and the cohomology ring, external products/Künneth, orientations and Poincaré duality, then the additional universal-coefficient, general-Künneth, H-space/Hopf-algebra, `SO(n)`, Bockstein, limits/Ext, transfer, and local-coefficient topics. The hard points are not just defining `Hom`: Ext/Universal coefficients, Alexander–Whitney signs, fundamental classes, local systems, and duality require a coherent chain-level architecture ([Hatcher Ch. 3](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch3.pdf), [Tau Ceti Stages 5–6](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)).
5. **Chapter 4, homotopy theory.** Extend cubical homotopy groups to relative groups and induced maps, prove Whitehead/CW approximation, excision and Hurewicz, then fiber bundles/stable groups, the cohomology connection, fibrations, Postnikov towers, obstruction theory, and the additional topics through spectra and Steenrod operations. The current file's own TODO list confirms that even basic sphere calculations and path-induced maps remain open in core Mathlib ([Mathlib homotopy groups](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Homotopy/HomotopyGroup.html)); relative Hurewicz/Whitehead and fibration infrastructure are the dominant hard points ([Hatcher Ch. 4](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch4.pdf), [Tau Ceti Stage 8](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)).
6. **Appendix.** Formalize CW topology, compact-open topology, HEP, and simplicial CW structures, checking that the chosen definitions coincide with Chapter 0 and the cellular-homology implementation. The main risk is creating incompatible parallel CW APIs rather than one faithful reusable interface ([author chapter list](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATchapters.html)).

#### 2) John W. Milnor, *Topology from the Differentiable Viewpoint* — best compact complete-book target

- **Exact text and canonical/teaching status.** Use the revised 1997 Princeton University Press edition, based on notes by David W. Weaver (the original was 1965). Princeton describes the book as a clear, succinct introduction covering smooth manifolds, tangent spaces, oriented manifolds, vector fields, degree, the Pontryagin construction, Sard's theorem, and the Hopf theorem ([publisher](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691048338/topology-from-the-differentiable-viewpoint)). A current University of Tennessee preliminary-exam syllabus assigns Milnor Chapters 1–6 alongside Guillemin–Pollack, direct evidence of continued teaching use ([UTK syllabus](https://math.utk.edu/graduate/preliminary-examinations/topology/)).
- **TOC and scope.** The revised TOC has: §1 smooth manifolds/maps, tangent spaces/derivatives, regular values, and the fundamental theorem of algebra; §2 Sard–Brown, manifolds with boundary, and Brouwer fixed point; §3 proof of Sard; §4 degree mod 2 and smooth homotopy/isotopy; §5 oriented manifolds and Brouwer degree; §6 vector fields and Euler number; §7 framed cobordism, the Pontryagin construction, and the Hopf theorem; §8 exercises; and an appendix classifying 1-manifolds ([revised TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf)).
- **Node/effort estimate.** The book is about 80 pages. A scan of the public 1965 text found 40 explicit theorem/definition-like declaration lines, with many additional unnumbered named theorem blocks; allowing those and the appendix gives **80–130 main-text nodes**. This is a **compact single-book project**, but not an easy one: Sard, orientation/degree, Euler/Poincaré–Hopf, and framed cobordism are substantial work packages. Estimate uses the [public text](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnortop.pdf) and [revised TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf), not an exact revised-edition census.
- **Lean status.** No complete Lean book was found. The 2025 JMM program includes a manifolds-in-Lean talk that mentions possible results from Milnor, which is evidence of interest rather than coverage ([AMS record](https://meetings.ams.org/math/jmm2025/meetingapp.cgi/Paper/44013)). Current ATLAS `MathlibExt` supplies selected Sard, parametric Sard, Thom transversality, Morse, and Riemannian-metric results, not a Milnor-ordered development ([Sard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/Sard.lean), [Thom transversality](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/ThomTransversality.lean), [Morse existence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/MorseExistence.lean)). `SardMoreira` says its main theorem is formalized but some corollaries are not, so it is not a whole-book or even complete-Sard-package solution ([project README](https://github.com/urkud/SardMoreira)).
- **Other proof assistants.** Isabelle's Smooth Manifolds AFP entry covers partitions of unity, tangent/cotangent spaces, path integrals, spheres, and projective spaces, not Milnor's book ([AFP](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Smooth_Manifolds.html)). No complete Coq/Isabelle/HOL/Mizar whole-book version was found in the dated searches; this is an unverified negative, not a claim that no such project exists.
- **Mathlib readiness.** Core has charted manifolds, `ContMDiff`/`mfderiv`, tangent bundles, immersions/submersions, partitions of unity, compact Whitney embedding, integral-curve existence/uniqueness, and a beginning unoriented-bordism definition ([manifold directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold), [immersion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Immersion.html), [partition of unity](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/PartitionOfUnity.html), [Whitney](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html), [bordism](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html)). The compact Whitney file explicitly says the weak Whitney theorem still requires Sard; the bordism file says bordisms/groups remain future work ([Whitney TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html), [Bordism TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html)).
- **Biggest actual gaps.** Core Mathlib has no path-named Sard/transversality/orientation/degree/Stokes modules at the audited revision, and lacks the full mod-2 and oriented degree theories, Euler-number/Poincaré–Hopf API, framed-cobordism/Pontryagin machinery, and Hopf theorem. The current core path check is [Mathlib at `d888143`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold). External ATLAS `MathlibExt` now materially improves Sard/transversality/Morse readiness, but it must be audited and integrated rather than treated as core ([ATLAS proved-library policy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/README.md), [Sard source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/Sard.lean)).
- **Downstream value.** Milnor supplies the smallest canonical route to regular values, degree, transversality-adjacent differential topology, Euler numbers, and framed cobordism. It is a high-yield prerequisite for Guillemin–Pollack, Lee, and later manifold projects; the publisher's scope and the UTK syllabus support its role as a teaching bridge ([Princeton](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691048338/topology-from-the-differentiable-viewpoint), [UTK](https://math.utk.edu/graduate/preliminary-examinations/topology/)).
- **Risks.** Milnor's concrete embedded-manifold presentation must be reconciled with Mathlib's abstract model-with-corners representation without weakening statements. Orientation, the exact regularity hypotheses of Sard, degree-sign conventions, cobordism data, and the appendix/exercise boundary are likely hostile-review flashpoints. A short page count does not remove those hard points.

**Chapter-by-chapter formalization sketch (top-3 use).**

1. **§1, smooth manifolds and smooth maps.** Implement embedded smooth manifolds, tangent spaces and derivatives, regular values, and the fundamental theorem of algebra. Reuse `ContMDiff`, `mfderiv`, and tangent-bundle APIs, but prove the equivalence with Milnor's concrete definitions. Hard point: chart/subtype coercions and exact regular-value statements ([TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf), [tangent bundle](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/Tangent.html)).
2. **§2, Sard–Brown, boundary, and Brouwer.** Add manifolds with boundary and the Sard–Brown statement, then derive the smooth Brouwer fixed-point theorem. Boundary charts and a faithful “almost every”/measure-zero formulation are the hard points; do not confuse the later general Brouwer theorem with this smooth route ([Milnor TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf)).
3. **§3, proof of Sard's theorem.** Build the measure-zero/critical-value induction and its finite-dimensional analytic estimates. Core Whitney's TODO confirms this is a real prerequisite hole; ATLAS/SardMoreira can supply candidate lemmas but require independent statement and axiom review ([Whitney TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html), [SardMoreira status](https://github.com/urkud/SardMoreira), [ATLAS Sard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/Sard.lean)).
4. **§4, degree modulo 2.** Define smooth homotopy/isotopy and the mod-2 degree, prove homotopy invariance, and connect regular-value counting to the earlier Sard package. Hard points are finite-count proofs, boundary conventions, and avoiding an axiomatized degree black box ([Milnor TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf)).
5. **§5, oriented manifolds and Brouwer degree.** Add orientation and integer-valued Brouwer degree, then relate it to mod-2 degree and homotopy. Core has no orientation module at the audited path snapshot; orientation signs and boundary behavior are the main review risks ([core manifold tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold)).
6. **§6, vector fields and Euler number.** Formalize vector fields, zeros, indices, Euler number, and the relevant Poincaré–Hopf/Hopf consequences. Existing vector-field/Lie-bracket APIs help only with the basic language; the finite-zero/index and global topology work remains ([Mathlib vector fields](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorField/LieBracket.html)).
7. **§7, framed cobordism and Pontryagin construction.** Define framed cobordism, the Pontryagin correspondence, and prove the Hopf theorem. `Bordism.lean` currently defines only singular manifolds and explicitly leaves bordisms and bordism groups to future PRs, so this is a new development rather than a library lookup ([Bordism TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html)).
8. **§8 and appendix.** Decide explicitly whether exercises are outside the programme's main-text contract; if included, inventory them separately. Formalize the classification of compact 1-manifolds and check that its smooth/topological hypotheses match the rest of the book. The appendix is small in page count but easy to accidentally omit from a “complete” claim ([revised TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf)).

#### 3) James R. Munkres, *Topology*, second edition — broad foundation, but a split ready/hard book

- **Exact text and canonical/teaching status.** Use *Topology*, 2nd ed., James R. Munkres, Prentice Hall, 2000, xvi+537 pages (Pearson's current Classic Version is a reprint, not a new mathematical edition). Pearson describes it as a single instructor resource bridging general and algebraic topology, with each part suitable for a semester ([Pearson TOC and overview](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/topology-classic-version/P200000006299/9780137848669)); bibliographic details for the original second edition are also recorded by [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Topology.html?id=XjoZAQAAIAAJ).
- **TOC and scope.** Chapters are: 1 Set Theory and Logic; 2 Topological Spaces and Continuous Functions; 3 Connectedness and Compactness; 4 Countability and Separation Axioms; 5 The Tychonoff Theorem; 6 Metrization Theorems and Paracompactness; 7 Complete Metric Spaces and Function Spaces; 8 Baire Spaces and Dimension Theory; 9 The Fundamental Group; 10 Separation Theorems in the Plane; 11 The Seifert–van Kampen Theorem; 12 Classification of Surfaces; 13 Classification of Covering Spaces; 14 Applications to Group Theory ([Pearson](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/topology-classic-version/P200000006299/9780137848669)).
- **Node/effort estimate.** The Isabelle source-inventory paper reports 76 definitions, 109 theorems, 34 lemmas, and 13 corollaries in Chapters 2–8 (232 non-example nodes), plus 115 examples; exercises are excluded ([source inventory](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1)). Extrapolating that documented density to Chapters 1 and 9–14 gives **430–560 main-text nodes**, a **large staged single-book project**. This is an estimate, not a full-book machine count; including examples/exercises would increase it.
- **Lean status.** No complete Lean whole-book formalization was found. ProofNet's `Munkres.lean` is only a 329-line benchmark file of translated exercises, and its issue tracker documents numerous faithfulness defects, so it is not coverage and is a warning for hostile review ([file](https://github.com/zhangir-azerbayev/ProofNet/blob/main/benchmark/benchmark_to_publish/formal/Munkres.lean), [issue](https://github.com/zhangir-azerbayev/ProofNet/issues/21)). ATLAS v1's 26-book list has no Munkres entry ([list/statistics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/README.md)). No complete Lean coverage disqualification was established.
- **Other proof assistants and the explicit Lean opening.** A 2026 paper reports an Isabelle/HOL autoformalization of all 39 sections of Munkres Chapters 2–8, with 85,472 lines and 806 fully proved formal results/zero `sorry`; Chapter 1 is said to be covered by `Complex_Main` ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1), [code repository](https://github.com/JUrban/isa_top_autoform1)). This is **not the whole 14-chapter book and not Lean**. The same paper's manual review reports incomplete statements, weakened definitions, and non-idiomatic formulations, so even its “complete” claim is not automatically sufficient for this programme's faithful-statement standard ([review section](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1)). Urban's earlier large Munkres project used **Megalodon**, not Lean ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03298v1)). Thus a Lean opening remains explicitly for Chapters 1–14, but duplicating Chapters 2–8 lowers novelty.
- **Mathlib readiness.** Point-set topology is unusually well supported: `isCompact_univ_pi`/Tychonoff ([compactness](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Compactness/Compact.html)), Urysohn metrization ([Urysohn](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Metrizable/Urysohn.html)), Stone–Čech ([StoneCech](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Compactification/StoneCech.html)), Arzela–Ascoli ([Ascoli](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Ascoli.html)), Baire and small inductive dimension ([inductive dimension](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/InductiveDimension/Classes.html)), and Tietze/Urysohn APIs ([Tietze](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Topology/TietzeExtension.lean), [Urysohn's lemma](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Topology/UrysohnsLemma.lean)). Do not report Tychonoff, basic covering maps, or CW complexes as holes.
- **Biggest actual gaps.** The current path inventory has no dedicated Nagata–Smirnov/Smirnov metrization file, and the small-inductive-dimension file explicitly leaves large inductive dimension as TODO ([tree check](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Topology), [dimension TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/InductiveDimension/Classes.html)). Chapters 9–14 inherit the algebraic-topology gaps listed for Hatcher: general van Kampen, plane separation, surface classification, covering-space classification, and group-theoretic applications.
- **Downstream value.** Munkres offers a coherent foundation for later topology, analysis, manifolds, and algebraic topology, with the added benefit that its point-set half can stress-test existing Mathlib APIs against a canonical source ([Pearson](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/topology-classic-version/P200000006299/9780137848669)).
- **Risks.** The project has two very different difficulty regimes: Chapters 2–8 are largely reconciliation and gap-filling, while Chapters 9–14 require new algebraic topology. Existing Isabelle/ProofNet statement defects show why “proved” is not synonymous with “faithful.” The exact treatment of examples and exercises must be fixed before claiming a node count.

#### 4) John M. Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*, second edition — highest manifold payoff, but multi-year

- **Exact text and canonical/teaching status.** Use John M. Lee, *Introduction to Smooth Manifolds*, 2nd ed., Graduate Texts in Mathematics 218, Springer, 2012 (some citations use the 2013 printing), xvi+708 pages. Springer calls it an introductory graduate-level textbook and Lee's author page says he regularly teaches graduate topology/geometry courses ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4419-9982-5), [author page](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~lee/Books/ISM/)).
- **TOC and scope.** The second-edition front matter lists 22 chapters: Smooth Manifolds; Smooth Maps; Tangent Vectors; Submersions, Immersions, and Embeddings; Submanifolds; Sard's Theorem; Lie Groups; Vector Fields; Integral Curves and Flows; Vector Bundles; The Cotangent Bundle; Tensors; Riemannian Metrics; Differential Forms; Orientations; Integration on Manifolds; De Rham Cohomology; The de Rham Theorem; Distributions and Foliations; The Exponential Map; Quotient Manifolds; Symplectic Manifolds; plus four review appendices ([author front matter](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~lee/Books/ISM/front-matter.pdf)).
- **Node/effort estimate.** A 22-chapter, 708-page graduate text with detailed proofs and many numbered statements is plausibly **500–700 main-text nodes** (excluding problems), hence **multi-year/large single-book**. This is a planning estimate from the [author TOC](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~lee/Books/ISM/front-matter.pdf); a scan of the publicly available first-edition PDF found 490 typed theorem/proposition/lemma/corollary/example labels, but that is only a lower-order cross-check and is **not** a second-edition count ([first-edition comparison PDF](https://math.mit.edu/~hrm/palestine/lee-smooth-manifolds.pdf)).
- **Lean status.** No complete or near-complete Lee-ordered Lean book was found. The old `lean-differential-topology` repository describes itself only as something that “may become” a smooth-manifold formalization ([repository](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/lean-differential-topology)). Bordg–Cavalleri report case studies in Lie groups, vector bundles, and Lie algebras, not Lee's 22 chapters ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2108.00484)). ATLAS v1 has no Lee book entry ([ATLAS list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/README.md)); the current qinz1yang library is Ricci-flow/geometric-analysis infrastructure, not Lee in order ([repository](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry)).
- **Other proof assistants.** Isabelle/HOL has basic smooth manifolds and a separate Lie-algebra development, not complete Lee coverage ([Smooth Manifolds AFP](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Smooth_Manifolds.html), [Lie algebra paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.19211v1)). No complete non-Lean Lee whole book was found in the dated searches.
- **Mathlib readiness.** Strong starting modules include charted spaces, `ContMDiff`/`MFDeriv`, tangent bundles, immersions/submersions, submanifolds, partitions of unity, Lie groups and `GroupLieAlgebra`, vector fields/Lie brackets, integral curves, vector bundles, Riemannian bundles, and Levi–Civita connections ([manifold tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold), [submersion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Submersion.html), [Lie group](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Algebra/LieGroup.html), [GroupLieAlgebra](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/GroupLieAlgebra.html), [integral curves](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/IntegralCurve/ExistUnique.html), [Levi–Civita](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/LeviCivita.html)).
- **Biggest actual gaps.** Core still needs a coherent manifold cotangent/differential-form/orientation/integration/Stokes/de-Rham stack, transversality, foliations/Frobenius, quotient-manifold theory, symplectic/contact structures, and some global-flow/boundary results. The normed-space differential-form file explicitly says manifold forms are “not defined yet”; partition-of-unity documentation lists integration of differential forms as future work; integral-curve documentation leaves boundary curves as TODO ([differential forms TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/DifferentialForm/Basic.html), [partition TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/PartitionOfUnity.html), [integral-curve TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/IntegralCurve/ExistUnique.html)). ATLAS's external Sard/transversality/Morse modules help with Chapter 6 but do not supply the entire stack ([ATLAS modules](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/tree/52908d9ef815f49572bc720c6991c7cbc25e52e0/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold)).
- **Important assumed-hole correction.** Lee's second edition deliberately introduces Riemannian metrics but **omits connections, geodesics, and curvature** as later topics; those should not be charged as prerequisites for this particular book ([author preface](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~lee/Books/ISM/front-matter.pdf)). Curvature is a hole for Riemannian-geometry books, not evidence that Lee ISM is infeasible.
- **Downstream value.** Lee would provide the common language for differential geometry, Riemannian geometry, PDE, Lie theory, and symplectic geometry, and its de Rham/homology bridge would be especially reusable ([Springer scope](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4419-9982-5)).
- **Risks.** Size and dependency breadth are the obvious risks. More subtle risks are equivalent tangent-space definitions, boundary/corner conventions, orientation and integration signs, the distinction between smooth singular and continuous singular homology, and avoiding a weakened de Rham theorem. The 22-chapter second edition, not the 20-chapter first edition, must be the source of record.

#### 5) Victor Guillemin and Alan Pollack, *Differential Topology* — valuable transversality/intersection project

- **Exact text and canonical/teaching status.** Use *Differential Topology*, Victor Guillemin and Alan Pollack, Prentice-Hall, 1974 (AMS Chelsea reprint available). AMS calls it a standard text, suitable for introductory graduate or advanced undergraduate courses, and emphasizes its transversality-based treatment ([AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/chel-370-h)); UTK assigns Chapters 1–3 in its differential-topology preliminary syllabus ([UTK](https://math.utk.edu/graduate/preliminary-examinations/topology/)).
- **TOC and scope.** Chapter 1 Manifolds and Smooth Maps; Chapter 2 Transversality and Intersection; Chapter 3 Oriented Intersection Theory; Chapter 4 Integration on Manifolds; appendices on measure zero/Sard and classification of compact one-manifolds ([AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/chel-370-h), [detailed TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/ilmenau/toc/014131080.PDF)).
- **Node/effort estimate.** A raw scan of the public 1974 PDF found 124 theorem/proposition/lemma/corollary/definition/example declaration lines; adding unnumbered definitions and named theorem blocks while excluding exercises gives **160–240 main-text nodes**. The many guided exercises could make the effective target **250–400 nodes** if included. Main-text effort is **staged single-book with hard infrastructure**, not a small Milnor-style project ([public PDF](https://www.cimat.mx/~gil/docencia/2020/topologia_diferencial/%5BGuillemin,Pollack%5DDifferential_Topology%281974%29.pdf)).
- **Lean status.** No complete Guillemin–Pollack Lean book was found. ATLAS's selected Sard, parametric Sard, Thom transversality, and Morse files are adjacent prerequisites, not a book-ordered formalization ([ATLAS Sard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/Sard.lean), [parametric Sard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/ParametricSard.lean), [Thom](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/ThomTransversality.lean)). The old Lean differential-topology repository is only an intention ([repository](https://github.com/PatrickMassot/lean-differential-topology)).
- **Other proof assistants.** Isabelle's basic smooth-manifold entry does not cover Guillemin–Pollack's intersection/orientation/integration programme ([AFP](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Smooth_Manifolds.html)). No complete non-Lean whole-book version was found; confidence in this negative is medium-low because no global registry is exhaustive.
- **Mathlib readiness.** Core manifolds, local normal forms for immersions/submersions, tangent bundles, partitions of unity, compact Whitney embedding, and beginning bordism are useful ([immersion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Immersion.html), [submersion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Submersion.html), [Whitney](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html), [Bordism](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html)). ATLAS supplies selected Sard/transversality/Morse infrastructure under a no-`sorry` policy ([MathlibExt policy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/README.md)).
- **Biggest actual gaps.** The hard missing parts are transversality as a general API, mod-2 and oriented intersection numbers, degree and winding-number infrastructure, Lefschetz fixed-point theory, Poincaré–Hopf, Hopf degree, differential forms/integration/Stokes, and Gauss–Bonnet. Existing bordism and Whitney files explicitly describe future work rather than finished theories ([Bordism TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html), [Whitney TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html)).
- **Downstream value.** The book would consolidate a reusable transversality/intersection/degree library that Milnor only introduces and Lee later uses in other forms. AMS specifically highlights Lefschetz, Poincaré–Hopf, Stokes, and de Rham exercises, making it a high-value but non-routine target ([AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/chel-370-h)).
- **Risks.** Major results are deliberately placed in guided exercises, including Jordan–Brouwer separation and de Rham cohomology; excluding exercises without an explicit contract would make “complete coverage” misleading. Oriented intersection signs, tubular neighborhoods, integration, and the exact smoothness hypotheses are the hard points.

#### 6) Manfredo P. do Carmo, *Differential Geometry of Curves and Surfaces*, revised and updated second edition — canonical but analytically demanding

- **Exact text and canonical/teaching status.** Use Manfredo P. do Carmo, *Differential Geometry of Curves and Surfaces*, Revised & Updated Second Edition, Dover, 2016, 528 pages. The bookseller description calls it one of the most widely used texts in its field and says it is suitable for advanced undergraduates and graduate students ([edition/status](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780486806990)); the author PDF confirms the revised-second-edition bibliographic note ([public PDF](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~huangty0407/DGNotes/Do%20Carmo%20-%20Differential%20Geometry%20of%20Curves%20and%20Surfaces.pdf)).
- **TOC and scope.** Five chapters: 1 Curves; 2 Regular Surfaces; 3 The Geometry of the Gauss Map; 4 The Intrinsic Geometry of Surfaces; 5 Global Differential Geometry. Subtopics include first/second fundamental forms, Gauss/compatibility equations, parallel transport/geodesics, Gauss–Bonnet, exponential map, completeness/Hopf–Rinow, variations, Jacobi fields, Hadamard theorems, Fáry–Milnor, and Hilbert's theorem ([TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/ilmenau/toc/027531538.PDF)).
- **Node/effort estimate.** A raw scan of the 2016 PDF found 393 declaration-like lines including examples and exercise material; filtering those conservatively gives **250–380 main-text nodes**. This is a **large staged book to multi-year** because the final chapter requires global analysis, not merely definitions ([public PDF](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~huangty0407/DGNotes/Do%20Carmo%20-%20Differential%20Geometry%20of%20Curves%20and%20Surfaces.pdf)).
- **Lean status.** No complete do Carmo book was found. A Lean formalization of the classical isoperimetric inequality cites do Carmo but is a selected theorem project ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14663v1)). The qinz1yang library has extensive differential-geometry infrastructure but is organized around Ricci flow and geometric analysis, not do Carmo's five chapters ([repository](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry)).
- **Other proof assistants.** Isabelle's Smooth Manifolds AFP entry covers only basic smooth-manifold material, not do Carmo's local/global surface theory ([AFP](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Smooth_Manifolds.html)). No complete non-Lean whole-book version was found in the dated searches.
- **Mathlib readiness.** Euclidean calculus, inner products/cross products, abstract manifolds, tangent bundles, partitions of unity, Riemannian bundles and Riemannian distance, and Levi–Civita connections provide a base ([Riemannian basic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian/Basic.html), [Levi–Civita](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/LeviCivita.html)). ATLAS also has a proved Riemannian-metric-existence module ([source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/RiemannianMetricExistence.lean)).
- **Biggest actual gaps.** There is no surface-specific first/second-fundamental-form/Gauss-map/shape-operator stack in core, and the audited core Riemannian directory contains only `Basic.lean` and `PathELength.lean`; no curvature/geodesic/Hopf–Rinow/Gauss–Bonnet API was found by path ([core Riemannian tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian)). ATLAS `WantedExt` still defers Hopf–Rinow and Fáry–Milnor ([Hopf–Rinow](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/WantedExt/Geometry/HopfRinowWanted.lean), [Fáry–Milnor](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/WantedExt/Geometry/FaryMilnorWanted.lean)); these are precisely the sort of global results needed later in do Carmo.
- **Downstream value.** The book would verify the classical local-to-global surface toolkit and feed Riemannian geometry, geometric analysis, and later comparison theorems ([TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/ilmenau/toc/027531538.PDF)).
- **Risks.** The first four chapters require careful extrinsic/intrinsic reconciliation; the fifth requires completeness, variational calculus, Jacobi fields, conjugate points, covering arguments, and global curve/surface theorems. These are actual analytic gaps, not a claim that “Mathlib has no manifolds.” The large exercise corpus and multiple equivalent definitions also need a scope decision.

### Inferences

- **Best immediate complete-coverage bet:** Milnor. Its 80–130-node scale makes a complete ordered inventory and hostile review plausible, while its hard packages are precisely reusable gaps (Sard, degree, orientation, cobordism). The evidence does not justify calling it easy or prerequisites-complete; it just makes the total scope manageable ([TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf)).
- **Best strategic infrastructure bet:** Hatcher. It should be selected if the programme values downstream algebraic-topology capacity over a quick finish. The existence of a detailed Tau Ceti roadmap is evidence of a feasible decomposition, not evidence that the hard work has already been done ([roadmap](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)).
- **Best broad but potentially duplicative bet:** Munkres. Keep it only with the explicit qualification that Lean coverage of the entire book is missing; the Isabelle Chapters 2–8 project is non-Lean and incomplete relative to the 14-chapter target ([Isabelle paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1), [Pearson TOC](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/topology-classic-version/P200000006299/9780137848669)).
- **Do not rank by page count alone.** Lee ISM has fewer pages than Hatcher's full additional-topics scope but a much larger manifold/de-Rham/foliation/symplectic dependency graph; Guillemin–Pollack is shorter but its guided exercises hide major results; do Carmo's final chapter is much harder than its early curve/surface computations ([Lee front matter](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~lee/Books/ISM/front-matter.pdf), [AMS G&P](https://bookstore.ams.org/chel-370-h), [do Carmo TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/ilmenau/toc/027531538.PDF)).
- **Effort classes are judgments, not measured person-years.** They combine the node ranges with the number of missing field-scale packages. No reliable public effort measurement was found for completing these exact books under the strict zero-`sorry`, faithful-statement contract; the Isabelle and ATLAS papers concern different scopes and quality gates ([Isabelle paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1), [ATLAS limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1)).

### Gaps

- No candidate received a full independent Lean build or axiom audit in this research. Statements about current external projects are source/repository claims, not local verification. In particular, ATLAS `MathlibExt` has a no-`sorry` policy, while `WantedExt` deliberately contains deferred statements ([policies](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/README.md), [WantedExt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/WantedExt/README.md)).
- Node ranges are approximate and have medium-to-low confidence. Hatcher's 244-label scan, Munkres's documented Chapters 2–8 inventory, and the raw scans for Milnor/Guillemin–Pollack/do Carmo support the ranges but do not replace a hand-curated main-text inventory ([Hatcher PDF](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf), [Munkres inventory](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1), [Milnor text](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnortop.pdf), [G&P PDF](https://www.cimat.mx/~gil/docencia/2020/topologia_diferencial/%5BGuillemin,Pollack%5DDifferential_Topology%281974%29.pdf), [do Carmo PDF](http://home.ustc.edu.cn/~huangty0407/DGNotes/Do%20Carmo%20-%20Differential%20Geometry%20of%20Curves%20and%20Surfaces.pdf)).
- The programme must decide whether examples and guided exercises count as nodes. This matters especially for Guillemin–Pollack, whose AMS description says exercises lead students through Jordan–Brouwer separation and de Rham cohomology ([AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/chel-370-h)).
- For every candidate except the specifically documented Isabelle/Megalodon Munkres work, complete/near-complete **non-Lean** status is an unverified negative with medium-to-low confidence, not a demonstrated absence. See the third section's search log.

## Where are the actual holes in current Mathlib (e.g. algebraic topology, manifolds, Riemannian geometry) versus assumed holes?

### Takeaway

The actual holes are narrower at the foundation and more specific at the frontier than a stock “Mathlib lacks topology/manifolds/Riemannian geometry” claim suggests. General topology, covering maps, CW complexes, fundamental groupoids, basic singular homology, smooth manifolds, partitions of unity, Riemannian distance, and Levi–Civita are present. The largest core gaps are relative/exact/cellular/cohomological algebraic topology, manifold forms/integration/de Rham theory, orientation/intersection/degree, and curvature/geodesic/global Riemannian geometry. External Lean libraries already fill some of these gaps, especially Sard/transversality/Morse and selected advanced geometry, but they are not equivalent to core coverage or whole-book coverage.

### Cited Findings

**Audited core snapshot.** The live GitHub recursive-tree check was run against Mathlib commit `d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1` (22 October 2026); it was not truncated. The following findings therefore concern that exact revision, with direct paths linked below ([repository at revision](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1)).

- **General topology is mostly ready, not blank.** `isCompact_univ_pi` proves Tychonoff; Urysohn metrization, Stone–Čech, Arzela–Ascoli, Tietze, Baire, and small inductive dimension have dedicated modules ([Tychonoff](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Compactness/Compact.html), [Urysohn metrization](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Metrizable/Urysohn.html), [Stone–Čech](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Compactification/StoneCech.html), [Ascoli](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/UniformSpace/Ascoli.html), [small inductive dimension](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/InductiveDimension/Classes.html)). Actual point-set gaps found were dedicated Nagata–Smirnov/Smirnov metrization and large inductive dimension; the latter is explicitly TODO ([dimension TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/InductiveDimension/Classes.html), [topology tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Topology)). Path absence is not a semantic proof that no declaration with another name exists, so this is a high-confidence module-level finding but a medium-confidence declaration-level negative.
- **Algebraic topology has real foundations.** `FundamentalGroupoid` defines paths quotiented by homotopy and induced functors; covering maps and deck transformations exist; `HomotopyGroup` defines cubical `π_n`, group instances, and commutativity for `n>1`; classical/relative CW complexes exist ([fundamental groupoid](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/FundamentalGroupoid/Basic.html), [covering](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Covering/Basic.html), [deck](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Covering/Deck.html), [homotopy groups](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/Homotopy/HomotopyGroup.html), [CW](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Topology/CWComplex/Classical/Basic.html)).
- **Singular homology is present but shallow relative to Hatcher.** The current directory has only `Basic`, `HomologyZero`, `HomotopyInvariance`, and `HomotopyInvarianceTopCat`; the documentation describes the singular chain complex, degree-zero example, and homotopy invariance ([directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/SingularHomology), [Basic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/SingularHomology/Basic.html), [HomotopyInvariance](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/SingularHomology/HomotopyInvariance.html)). `EilenbergSteenrod.lean` introduces the homology-theory interface, not a complete proof of Hatcher's excision/cellular/cohomology programme ([interface](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/AlgebraicTopology/EilenbergSteenrod.html)).
- **The actual algebraic-topology holes are explicit in a current roadmap.** Tau Ceti starts from the existing singular chains, fundamental groupoid, topological pairs, cubical homotopy groups, and classical CW complexes, and lists as work to build: van Kampen, relative homology, excision, cellular comparison, cohomology/products/duality, Euler characteristic, Hurewicz, and Whitehead ([roadmap scope](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)). This is strong evidence against both “there is no algebraic topology” and “Hatcher is mostly ready.”
- **Smooth manifolds are substantial.** Core has charted spaces, smooth maps and derivatives, tangent bundles, immersions/submersions, partitions of unity, Lie groups/algebras, vector fields/Lie brackets, integral curves, and vector/Riemannian bundles ([manifold tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold), [immersion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Immersion.html), [partition](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/PartitionOfUnity.html), [Lie algebra](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/GroupLieAlgebra.html), [integral curves](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/IntegralCurve/ExistUnique.html)).
- **Some frequently cited manifold holes are no longer correct as blanket statements.** Compact Whitney embedding is proved in core, and Levi–Civita connection existence/uniqueness is proved in core ([Whitney](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html), [Levi–Civita](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/LeviCivita.html)). The remaining Whitney TODO is the weak noncompact/σ-compact theorem, which the file says requires Sard ([Whitney TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html)).
- **Actual core manifold holes have precise TODO evidence.** Normed-space differential forms say manifold forms are not defined yet; partitions of unity identify manifold-form integration as future work; integral curves leave boundary curves open; bordism defines singular manifolds but leaves bordisms and bordism groups to future PRs ([forms](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/DifferentialForm/Basic.html), [partition](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/PartitionOfUnity.html), [integral curves](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/IntegralCurve/ExistUnique.html), [bordism](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html)). The current path check also found no dedicated orientation, transversality, Sard, Stokes, foliation, symplectic, or manifold-de-Rham path ([core manifold tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold)).
- **Riemannian geometry is present at the metric/connection layer, not the full curvature syllabus.** `RiemannianBundle`, `IsRiemannianManifold`, Riemannian distance, and Levi–Civita are documented ([Riemannian basic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian/Basic.html), [Riemannian bundle](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/Riemannian.html), [Levi–Civita](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/LeviCivita.html)). At the audited revision the `Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian` directory contains only `Basic.lean` and `PathELength.lean`; no curvature/geodesic file was found ([directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian)). ATLAS separately keeps Hopf–Rinow as a deferred `theorem_wanted` ([wanted statement](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/WantedExt/Geometry/HopfRinowWanted.lean)).
- **External ATLAS changes the readiness calculation, but not the core fact.** The current ATLAS `MathlibExt` policy requires no `sorry`, `sorryAx`, `admit`, custom axioms, or `native_decide`; its manifold files include Sard, parametric Sard, Thom transversality, Morse lemma/existence, Riemannian-metric existence, collars, and tubular neighborhoods ([policy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/README.md), [current module directory](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/tree/52908d9ef815f49572bc720c6991c7cbc25e52e0/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold), [Sard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/Sard.lean), [Thom](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/ThomTransversality.lean)). These are reusable theorem packages, not evidence that Hatcher/Lee/Milnor/G&P/do Carmo has been completed.
- **Advanced external geometry is also selected infrastructure, not a textbook.** qinz1yang's active repository describes itself as focused on Ricci flow, lists Riemannian tensor calculus, de Rham cohomology, Morse results, Hopf–Rinow and extensive topology, and gives a standard-axiom verification protocol ([repository](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry), [release metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/qinz1yang/differential-geometry/releases/latest)). It may be a valuable upstream source for later books, but it is not a Hatcher/Lee/do Carmo whole-book formalization and its claims still need integration review.

### Inferences

- **For Hatcher and Munkres Chapters 9–14, the bottleneck is exact/natural algebraic topology, not basic definitions.** A sensible programme should reuse the existing groupoid/CW/singular-chain interfaces and spend effort on relative homology, excision, products, duality, and homotopy calculations rather than rebuilding basic topology ([Tau Ceti roadmap](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)).
- **For Milnor and Guillemin–Pollack, external Sard/transversality/Morse results make the project more feasible than a core-only audit suggests.** The remaining distinctive holes are degree, orientation, intersection, Euler/Poincaré–Hopf, framed cobordism, and integration; these should be scheduled as separate work packages ([ATLAS modules](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/tree/52908d9ef815f49572bc720c6991c7cbc25e52e0/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold), [Bordism TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html)).
- **For Lee ISM, the central missing stack is forms → orientation → integration/Stokes → de Rham, with foliations/quotients/symplectic afterward.** Curvature should not be used as a generic reason to reject Lee ISM, because the author explicitly defers it ([Lee preface](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~lee/Books/ISM/front-matter.pdf)).
- **For do Carmo and Lee Riemannian, curvature/geodesics/global analysis are genuine holes.** These books should not be marketed as “mostly present” merely because `IsRiemannianManifold` and Levi–Civita exist ([core Riemannian directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian), [Hopf–Rinow wanted](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/WantedExt/Geometry/HopfRinowWanted.lean)).

### Gaps

- A recursive path search cannot prove that a differently named declaration is absent. The strong claims above are “no dedicated module/path at this revision” plus documentation TODOs; declaration-level absence remains unverified until a source-wide identifier search or `#check` audit is run ([Mathlib revision](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1)).
- External repositories move quickly and have different pins. The ATLAS evidence is at commit `52908d9ef815f49572bc720c6991c7cbc25e52e0`; qinz1yang metadata was checked at `fd2f07fba4c6c102ba626c59153ad98da8f0d442`. No attempt was made to unify these into one Lean environment ([ATLAS revision](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/tree/52908d9ef815f49572bc720c6991c7cbc25e52e0), [qinz1yang repository](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry)).
- The current Tau Ceti document is a specification of desired work, not a completion report. It must not be cited as saying the missing theorems are already proved ([roadmap](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicTopology/README.md)).

## What existing Lean/Coq/Isabelle/HOL projects already cover substantial portions?

### Takeaway

There is substantial adjacent coverage, but it is mostly **selected theorems, infrastructure, or a different proof assistant**, not whole-book formalization. The only near-complete book-sized result directly found was Munkres Chapters 2–8 in Isabelle/HOL, and it is both non-Lean and short of the full 14-chapter book. Hatcher has the richest Lean theorem-level activity; Milnor/G&P benefit from recent Sard/transversality/Morse libraries; Lee and do Carmo have manifold/geometric-analysis infrastructure but no verified whole-book project found here.

### Cited Findings

- **ATLAS book-scale evidence.** ATLAS v1 contains 26 book directories and 2,855/4,007 target statements formalized (71.3%), but its own README says it is ongoing and its paper explicitly says none of the books is fully formalized ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/README.md), [paper §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1)). Its `AlgebraicTopologyI` and `DifferentialGeometry` entries are MIT OCW notes, not Hatcher or do Carmo; the directory listing is public ([ATLAS directories](https://api.github.com/repos/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/contents/v1/Atlas?ref=main)). The current root is being reorganized for v2 and the archived v1 projects are not part of the root build ([root README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean)).
- **Prove2Me Hatcher is selected theorem coverage.** Public pages show individual Hatcher statements with human review, including Brouwer, van Kampen surjectivity, lifting, and deck-group results ([Brouwer](https://prove2.me/theorems/ad651f40-9e18-4e53-af8f-bbd8968a3bcd), [van Kampen](https://prove2.me/theorems/714a9c26-c893-4a99-ac95-b7d4e6e2d0e8), [lifting](https://prove2.me/theorems/3fe20d40-c21b-4244-922b-87ba49a1bbd1), [deck](https://prove2.me/theorems/452e9e92-ce8b-4c6c-8447-c0fcf55e631b)). Prove2Me describes missions as one paper or textbook each, but a mission existing is not evidence that every chapter is done ([platform paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28433v2)). The public API returned HTTP 401 to the unauthenticated mission/theorem queries on 22 October 2026, so coverage beyond public indexed pages remains unverified.
- **Lean Pool and Lax are not whole-book evidence.** Lean Pool reports 211 pooled projects and lists selected results such as Hatcher-derived finite-graph fundamental groups and Whitehead's theorem for CW complexes ([paper/table](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25199v1)). Lax's public homepage reports 110 submissions and shows its current submission stream, but no whole-book candidate was established from that page ([Lax](https://laxarchive.org/)). Both are useful places to look for reusable theorem packages, not proof that these six books are done.
- **Formal Conjectures is a statement benchmark, not a book-completion registry.** Its own description says it collects formalized statements of conjectures in Lean 4, with optional formal-proof annotations ([about](https://firsching.ch/formal-conjectures/about/), [repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures)). It should not be confused with a zero-`sorry` complete textbook proof library.
- **Munkres has a real non-Lean near-complete subset.** The Isabelle/HOL paper reports all 39 sections of Chapters 2–8, 199 definitions and 806 lemmas/theorems/corollaries, zero `sorry` ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1)); the associated code is public ([repository](https://github.com/JUrban/isa_top_autoform1)). It does not cover Chapters 9–14, and its own review flags incomplete statements. Urban's separate 130k-line project used Megalodon ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03298v1)). This is the clearest case where complete non-Lean *partial* coverage leaves an explicit Lean opening rather than disqualifying the book.
- **Other assistant coverage is meaningful but fragmentary.** Isabelle AFP covers the circle and classical Seifert–van Kampen ([circle](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Fundamental_Group_Circle.html), [Seifert](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Seifert-Van-Kampen.html)) and basic smooth manifolds ([Smooth Manifolds](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Smooth_Manifolds.html)); HOL Light has singular homology ([BIRS review](https://www.birs.ca/files//workshops/Juniper/23w5124/report23w5124.pdf)); Coq HoTT has synthetic homotopy theory ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04591)). None is evidence of complete Hatcher, Lee, Milnor, Guillemin–Pollack, or do Carmo coverage.
- **Recent Lean differential-topology packages are useful but not books.** `SardMoreira` explicitly says some corollaries remain unfinished ([README](https://github.com/urkud/SardMoreira)); ATLAS `MathlibExt` supplies selected Sard/transversality/Morse modules under a no-`sorry` policy ([policy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/README.md)); qinz1yang supplies selected advanced geometry and topology, not a textbook ([repository](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry)).
- **Faithfulness warnings are directly relevant.** ProofNet's Munkres issue lists missing hypotheses, reversed inclusions, and weakened statements ([issue](https://github.com/zhangir-azerbayev/ProofNet/issues/21)); the Munkres Isabelle paper reports incomplete statements in its own manual review ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1)); ATLAS's paper says its evaluation includes faithfulness and proof-integrity checks but none of its books is fully formalized ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1)). These projects should be mined for lemmas, not accepted uncritically as complete-book precedents.

### Inferences

- **Disqualification result:** none of the six shortlisted books was shown to have complete Lean coverage. Munkres is retained only because the near-complete result is Isabelle/HOL Chapters 2–8 and the full 14-chapter Lean target remains open; this is exactly the non-Lean-partial exception, not an exception based on a selected theorem ([Isabelle scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1), [Munkres full TOC](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/topology-classic-version/P200000006299/9780137848669)).
- **No selected-theorem project should reduce a claimed book node count.** Hatcher's Prove2Me pages, Lean Pool entries, ATLAS's MIT notes, SardMoreira, and qinz1yang are evidence of reusable infrastructure only. A completion audit must map every book node to a declaration and check order, source version, statement faithfulness, and transitive axioms.
- **A practical dependency strategy emerges:** start with Milnor's compact ordered target while extracting reusable Sard/transversality components; in parallel build Hatcher Chapters 0–2 as the first algebraic-topology stage, then decide whether Munkres's duplication is worth its teaching/canonical value. This is an inference from the scopes and current gaps, not a claim of measured productivity ([Milnor TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf), [Hatcher TOC](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATchapters.html), [ATLAS Sard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/Sard.lean)).

### Gaps

**Dated absence/search protocol (all negatives below are unverified, not assertions of nonexistence).** Searches were conducted on 21–22 October 2026. The exact queries included each book title with `Lean formalization`, `Lean whole book`, `Coq formalization`, `Isabelle formalization`, `HOL formalization`, and `complete formalization`; title-specific variants included `Hatcher Algebraic Topology Lean`, `Munkres Topology Lean`, `Milnor Topology Differentiable Viewpoint Lean`, `Guillemin Pollack Differential Topology Lean`, `do Carmo Differential Geometry Lean`, and `Lee Introduction Smooth Manifolds Lean`. Sites checked were publisher/author pages, Mathlib docs and GitHub recursive trees, the Lean community project registry, ATLAS and its v1 book list, Prove2Me public pages, Lean Pool/Lax, Formal Conjectures, Isabelle AFP, and general GitHub/web results ([Lean project registry](https://leanprover-community.github.io/lean_projects.html), [ATLAS list](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/README.md), [AFP topology index](https://isa-afp.org/topics/mathematics/topology/), [Formal Conjectures](https://firsching.ch/formal-conjectures/about/)).

- **Hatcher, Lee ISM, Milnor, Guillemin–Pollack, and do Carmo:** no complete or near-complete whole-book Lean/Coq/Isabelle/HOL project was found in those searches. Confidence: **medium** for Lean, **medium-low** for all non-Lean systems because there is no exhaustive global index. Selected projects cited above are the reason this is not a claim of no substantial coverage.
- **Munkres:** the positive Isabelle Chapters 2–8 result is verified by the paper and repository; no complete 14-chapter Lean project was found. Confidence: **medium-high** for the limited positive scope, **medium** for the negative whole-book Lean search ([Isabelle paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07455v1), [Pearson full TOC](https://www.pearson.com/en-us/subject-catalog/p/topology-classic-version/P200000006299/9780137848669)).
- **Bott–Tu and Lee Riemannian:** no whole-book Lean or non-Lean version was found. Confidence: **medium-low**; the positive evidence found was selected infrastructure and Ricci-flow work, not book-order coverage ([Bott–Tu](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-3951-0), [Lee Riemannian](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-91755-9), [qinz1yang](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry)).
- **Search limitations:** several `site:leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs` and `site:laxarchive.org` WebSearch requests returned HTTP 500, and unauthenticated `prove2.me/api/v1/missions` and `/theorems` returned HTTP 401. I compensated for Mathlib with direct documentation and GitHub API tree checks, but did not claim equivalent exhaustive coverage for Lax or Prove2Me ([Mathlib revision](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1), [Lax](https://laxarchive.org/), [Prove2Me paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28433v2)).
- **Verification still to do before a final go/no-go decision:** pin one source edition per book; hand-count its nodes including the exercise policy; run a repository-wide identifier search and local builds for any claimed near-complete project; inspect `#print axioms` transitively; and perform hostile read-back of statements. Without those steps, the correct conclusion is “strong candidates with no complete project found,” not “proven unformalized.”
