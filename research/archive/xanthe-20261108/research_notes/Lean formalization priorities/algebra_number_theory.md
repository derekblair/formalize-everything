# Algebra and number theory books for Lean 4 complete coverage

## Which canonical texts are complete-coverage-sized and build on current Mathlib rather than requiring a new field?

### Takeaway

**Ranked recommendation:** (1) Atiyah–Macdonald, *Introduction to Commutative Algebra*; (2) Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*; (3) Serre, *Local Fields*; (4) Washington, *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*; (5) Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*. Only the first two are credible single-book starts on present evidence; the last three are multi-year programmes with substantial missing or external prerequisites.

No complete or near-complete **ordered, full-book** Lean formalization was verified for any of the five. This is an **unverified negative**, not a claim that none exists: confidence is medium-to-high for the Lean audit and low-to-medium for non-Lean assistants. Large endpoint projects—local/global class field theory, regular-prime FLT, elliptic-curve group law, modular forms—must not be mistaken for book coverage.

### Cited Findings

#### Scope and evidence conventions

- **Web-verified facts** below are tied to publisher, project, paper, or current Mathlib URLs. **Node ranges, effort classes, rankings, downstream assessments, and implementation sketches are analyst inferences**, not claims made by those sources. The current Mathlib documentation identifies itself as built with Lean 4.35.0: [Mathlib docs](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/).
- **Training-knowledge use is explicitly segregated.** Mathematical interpretation of chapter titles, likely proof routes, and hard-point diagnosis in the implementation sketches comes from analyst knowledge and is labeled inference; it is not independently web-verified unless a direct source is attached, and it is not used as evidence for any complete-coverage negative.
- The calibration supplied for this research is Rudin PMA ≈330 main-text definition/theorem nodes. Estimates here cover **main-text definitions and stated results, including unnumbered ones and appendices**, but not exercises, bibliography, or index. An exercises-inclusive interpretation would need a new census; it is not silently included in these ranges.
- Estimated ranges use publisher page counts, chapter density, and comparison with the supplied PMA calibration, widened to avoid false precision. They are **medium/low-confidence planning estimates**, not inventories. Strict acceptance should mean stable source-node IDs, every definition and theorem in order, faithful statements, zero `sorry`/`admit`, no added axioms, only Lean's standard `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` axioms, and independent statement review.

#### 1. M. F. Atiyah and I. G. Macdonald, *Introduction to Commutative Algebra* — strongest candidate

- **Exact text and teaching status (web-verified).** Target the 1969 Addison-Wesley first edition, ix+128 pages; Macdonald's bibliography records this exact bibliographic form and distinguishes the 2016 Westview economy reprint: [I. G. Macdonald bibliography](https://ig-macdonald.github.io/). The book's introduction says it grew from lectures to third-year Oxford undergraduates and is a rapid introduction after elementary algebra: [publicly hosted text](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/atiyahmacdonald.pdf); [Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Introduction_To_Commutative_Algebra.html?hl=&id=3V8PAQAAMAAJ).
- **TOC (web-verified).** Eleven chapters: 1 Rings and Ideals; 2 Modules; 3 Rings and Modules of Fractions; 4 Primary Decomposition; 5 Integral Dependence and Valuations; 6 Chain Conditions; 7 Noetherian Rings; 8 Artin Rings; 9 Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains; 10 Completions; 11 Dimension Theory. The same [text and contents](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/atiyahmacdonald.pdf) gives the section structure, including Hilbert functions, Noetherian local dimension, regular local rings, and transcendental dimension in Chapter 11.
- **Scope/effort assessment (inference).** **180–240 nodes (0.55–0.75 PMA); single-book project.** This is the cleanest complete-coverage size in the set. Its terse presentation increases statement-extraction work but not the mathematical breadth.
- **Lean coverage (web-verified positive; negative remains provisional).** Mathlib references individual Atiyah–Macdonald results rather than presenting an ordered book development. For example, `Mathlib.RingTheory.Lasker` explicitly identifies its two uniqueness results as Theorems 4.5 and 4.10 in Atiyah–Macdonald and gives Lasker–Noether decomposition: [Lasker](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.html). A 2023 community discussion estimated that most of the book except primary decomposition and dimension was present, but that snapshot is now outdated and was never a book-coverage audit: [Zulip, December 2023](https://leanprover-community.github.io/archive/stream/116395-maths/topic/commutative.20algebra.20status.html). GitHub repository API query `"Atiyah Macdonald" lean` returned **0 repositories** on 2026-10-21: [query](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=%22Atiyah%20Macdonald%22%20lean). **No complete/near-complete Lean book formalization verified; medium confidence in this negative.**
- **Other proof assistants (unverified negative).** Searches for the exact title with `formalization`, `Coq`, `Isabelle`, and `Lean` on 2026-10-21 found theorem-level commutative-algebra work but no credible full-book or near-full-book development in Coq/Rocq, Isabelle/HOL, HOL Light, Mizar, or Metamath. Confidence: **low-to-medium**, because library search is not a mathematical census. There is therefore no verified non-Lean completion to use as a reason either to disqualify or to assume a port is available.
- **Current Mathlib readiness (web-verified).** This is exceptionally strong:
  - primary ideals and both uniqueness theorems plus `IsLasker.exists_isMinimalPrimaryDecomposition`: [Lasker](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.html);
  - Noetherian polynomial and multivariate-polynomial Krull-dimension formulas, `Polynomial.ringKrullDim_of_isNoetherianRing` and `MvPolynomial.ringKrullDim_of_isNoetherianRing`: [KrullDimension.Polynomial](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/KrullDimension/Polynomial.html);
  - Krull's principal-ideal and height theorems: [Ideal.KrullsHeightTheorem](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/KrullsHeightTheorem.html);
  - exactness of adic completion on finite modules over a Noetherian ring, `AdicCompletion.map_exact`: [AdicCompletion.Exactness](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Exactness.html);
  - Artinian/Noetherian equivalence in Krull dimension at most zero and Hopkins–Levitzki: [HopkinsLevitzki](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/HopkinsLevitzki.html);
  - going-up for integral algebras: [Ideal.GoingUp](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/GoingUp.html).
- **Documented residual gap (web-verified).** `Mathlib.RingTheory.Polynomial.HilbertPoly` constructs coefficient/Hilbert polynomials but its TODO explicitly asks for Hilbert polynomials of finitely generated graded modules over Noetherian rings: [Polynomial.HilbertPoly](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/HilbertPoly.html). That makes Chapter 11's Hilbert–Samuel theory, rather than Chapters 1–10, the principal new-work spike.
- **Downstream value (inference).** A faithful ordered development would become a reviewed API map for localization, primary decomposition, integrality, Dedekind domains, completion, and dimension—the commutative-algebra base used by algebraic geometry and algebraic number theory. The value is coherence and statement fidelity, not merely re-proving famous endpoints.
- **Risks.** Library definitions may be more general or packaged differently (arbitrary localization types versus explicit fractions; algebraic adic completion versus uniform-space completion; `ENat`-valued dimension). Chapter 11 can become a prerequisite project if the local-dimension characterizations are not all present. These are engineering risks supported by the modules above, not evidence of missing elementary algebra.

#### 2. Jean-Pierre Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic* — strongest number-theory start, with two hard spikes

- **Exact text and teaching status (web-verified).** Target the English first edition, GTM 7, Springer, copyright 1973, IX+119 pages; Springer's bibliographic page explicitly says edition 1, while its hardcover listing is 1978: [publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4). The publisher describes its origin in lectures to second-year students at the École Normale Supérieure and its two goals: Hasse–Minkowski/classification of rational quadratic forms and analytic methods culminating in Dirichlet and modular forms: [same source](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4).
- **TOC (web-verified).** Seven chapters: 1 Finite Fields; 2 p-Adic Fields; 3 Hilbert Symbol; 4 Quadratic Forms over Qp and over Q; 5 Integral Quadratic Forms with Discriminant ±1; 6 The Theorem on Arithmetic Progressions; 7 Modular Forms: [publisher TOC](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4), with individual records for [Chapter 4](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4_4), [Chapter 5](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4_5), and [Chapter 7](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4_7).
- **Scope/effort assessment (inference).** **150–210 nodes (0.45–0.65 PMA); single-book, but high variance.** Its small page count is deceptive: Chapters 3–5 contain the main new theory, while Chapters 6–7 have unusually good library support.
- **Lean coverage (web-verified positive).** `are4c4/SerreNumberTheoryBlueprint` describes itself as a Lean 4 formalization of this book: [repository](https://github.com/are4c4/SerreNumberTheoryBlueprint). Its actual `Formalization` tree contains only `Chapter01`, and that directory has exactly four files—finite fields, multiplicative group, power sums, and Chevalley–Warning: [Formalization tree](https://github.com/are4c4/SerreNumberTheoryBlueprint/tree/main/SerreNumberTheory/Formalization), [Chapter01 tree](https://github.com/are4c4/SerreNumberTheoryBlueprint/tree/main/SerreNumberTheory/Formalization/Chapter01). Thus it is an **active partial overlap, not near-complete coverage**. GitHub API query `"Course in Arithmetic" lean` returned this one repository on 2026-10-21: [query](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=%22Course%20in%20Arithmetic%22%20lean). No other complete Lean book project was verified; **medium-to-high confidence**, subject to the usual absence caveat.
- **Other proof assistants (unverified negative).** Exact-title searches with `Coq`, `Isabelle`, and `formalization` on 2026-10-21 found no credible complete/near-complete non-Lean book development. Confidence: **low-to-medium**. The partial Lean project and Mathlib results are not evidence of non-Lean coverage.
- **Current Mathlib readiness (web-verified).**
  - Finite-field Chevalley–Warning is proved as `char_dvd_card_solutions_of_sum_lt`, with finitary, `Fintype`, unary, and binary versions: [FieldTheory.ChevalleyWarning](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/ChevalleyWarning.html).
  - Dirichlet's theorem is proved via Dirichlet L-functions as `Nat.infinite_setOfPred_prime_and_eq_mod` and `Nat.forall_exists_prime_gt_and_eq_mod`: [LSeries.PrimesInAP](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/PrimesInAP.html). Dirichlet L-functions have meromorphic continuation and functional equations: [LSeries.DirichletContinuation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/DirichletContinuation.html).
  - Level-one modular forms have dimension formulas and a Sturm bound: [LevelOne.DimensionFormula](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/DimensionFormula.html); every level-one form is a polynomial in E4 and E6, and the module explicitly cites Serre VII.3.2: [LevelOne.GradedRing](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/GradedRing.html).
  - Quadratic-form basics, isometries, real signatures, and Sylvester uniqueness exist: [QuadraticForm.Signature](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/QuadraticForm/Signature.html).
- **Gap signal and warning (web-verified).** A current Mathlib master-tree name audit on 2026-10-21 found no path containing `HasseMinkowski` or `HilbertSymbol`; this is only a name-level gap signal, not a proof of absence. A web-facing LeanGenius page is **not** an acceptable substitute: it explicitly declares `axiom hasse_minkowski_alt`, other axioms, `True` placeholders, and placeholder local solvability despite displaying a “verified” label: [LeanGenius, Hilbert 11](https://leangenius.org/proof/hilbert-11). Under the requested standard this is non-strict and cannot count as Hasse–Minkowski or book coverage.
- **Downstream value (inference).** The book joins finite fields, p-adics, quadratic reciprocity/forms, Dirichlet L-functions, and modular forms in one short reviewed dependency chain. Chapters 3–5 would fill a particularly valuable local–global and integral-quadratic-form gap rather than duplicate existing modular-form endpoints.
- **Risks.** The active Chapter 1 project creates duplication/coordination risk. Hilbert-symbol product formulas, p=2 computations, Hasse–Minkowski, and integral lattices are the hard mathematical core. Classical definitions must be aligned with Mathlib's scalar-extension, quadratic-form isometry, slash-action, cusp, and q-expansion definitions without weakening statements.

#### 3. Jean-Pierre Serre, *Local Fields* — excellent target, but not a current-Mathlib single-book start

- **Exact text and status (web-verified).** Target the English first edition, GTM 67, Springer, 1979, VIII+241 pages: [publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-5673-9). Springer describes the goal as local class field theory from the cohomological viewpoint, with preliminary parts on local fields and group cohomology; this is an advanced graduate/reference text, not an elementary course: [same source](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-5673-9).
- **TOC (web-verified).** Fourteen numbered chapters plus introduction and the Ramification appendix: Introduction; Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains; Completion; Discriminant and Different; Ramification Groups; The Norm; Artin Representation; Basic Facts; Cohomology of Finite Groups; Theorems of Tate and Nakayama; Galois Cohomology; Class Formations; Brauer Group of a Local Field; Local Class Field Theory; Local Symbols and Existence Theorem; Ramification: [publisher book/TOC](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-5673-9) and [appendix record](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4757-5673-9_16).
- **Scope/effort assessment (inference).** **320–450 nodes (0.95–1.35 PMA); multi-year on Mathlib alone.** A shorter project is conceivable only after a deliberate, hostile-reviewed reuse of the large external class-field-theory development below; that is a dependency decision, not current Mathlib readiness.
- **Lean coverage: basic local fields (web-verified).** The CPP 2024 paper formalizes discrete valuations, DVRs, completions, extensions, and basic local fields—about 8,000 lines in Lean 3—not Serre's book: [de Frutos-Fernández and Nuccio](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01998v2). It explicitly calls this a first step toward local class field theory and says a systematic comparison of algebraic and uniform completion was then missing. It is prerequisite work, not full-book coverage.
- **Lean coverage: major external endpoint work (web-verified).** `n-yamaguchi-0729/ClassFieldTheory` now claims finite abelian local reciprocity, local existence/classification, and global reciprocity/existence, with CI proof and axiom audits: [repository](https://github.com/n-yamaguchi-0729/ClassFieldTheory). My source inspection at commit `fbd320cd8b1b06487c58227e92eb135794267709` (2026-10-21) counted 3,136 Lean files/477,075 lines and found no `sorry` or explicit `axiom` in `Nyll`; I did **not** rerun its build or independently review statement fidelity: [commit](https://github.com/n-yamaguchi-0729/ClassFieldTheory/commit/fbd320cd8b1b06487c58227e92eb135794267709). Its own API contract warns that it checks compiled presence and kind, **not theorem statements**: [main-declarations.json](https://github.com/n-yamaguchi-0729/ClassFieldTheory/blob/main/.github/verification/main-declarations.json). This is a major reusable overlap, but not an ordered Serre book formalization and not evidence of near-complete book coverage.
- **Other proof assistants (unverified negative with historical positive).** The 2024 paper says its basic local-field work was, to the authors' knowledge, the first in any proof assistant: [paper, introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01998v2). Exact-title Coq/Isabelle searches on 2026-10-21 found no complete book development; confidence **low-to-medium** for the current negative. GitHub query `"Local Fields" Serre lean` returned only the isolated MassFormula project on 2026-10-21: [query](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=%22Local%20Fields%22%20Serre%20lean), [MassFormula](https://github.com/0stellensatz/MassFormula).
- **Current Mathlib readiness and documented gaps (web-verified).** Useful modules include the different ideal and its ramification properties: [DedekindDomain.Different](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/DedekindDomain/Different.html); decomposition and inertia fields: [RamificationInertia.HilbertTheory](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/RamificationInertia/HilbertTheory.html); finite-cyclic group cohomology: [GroupCohomology.FiniteCyclic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Homological/GroupCohomology/FiniteCyclic.html); and a nonarchimedean-local-field instance for Qp: [Padics.LocalField](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/LocalField.html). Against that, `RingTheory.Valuation.RamificationGroup` explicitly says “TODO: Define higher ramification groups in lower numbering”: [RamificationGroup](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/RamificationGroup.html), and `Algebra.BrauerGroup.Defs` lists the abelian-group and functor proofs as TODOs: [BrauerGroup.Defs](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/BrauerGroup/Defs.html).
- **Downstream value (inference).** This would supply reviewed ramification, cohomology, Brauer-group, and local-reciprocity infrastructure for global class field theory, Galois representations, and arithmetic geometry.
- **Risks.** Higher/lower ramification numbering, Herbrand theory, Artin representations, class formations, and faithful comparison with the external library are the hard points. Importing a 477k-line external development could replace one problem with a large statement-audit and maintenance problem.

#### 4. Lawrence C. Washington, *Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields*, 2nd ed. — high value, multi-year

- **Exact text and teaching status (web-verified).** Target the 1997 second edition, GTM 83, Springer, XIV+490 pages: [publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-1934-7). Springer explicitly says it can serve as a second course in algebraic number theory and covers p-adic L-functions, class numbers, cyclotomic units, FLT, Iwasawa theory, and the second edition's Main Conjecture material: [same source](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-1934-7).
- **TOC (web-verified).** Sixteen chapters: Fermat’s Last Theorem; Basic Results; Dirichlet Characters; Dirichlet L-series and Class Number Formulas; p-adic L-functions and Bernoulli Numbers; Stickelberger’s Theorem; Iwasawa’s Construction of p-adic L-functions; Cyclotomic Units; The Second Case of Fermat’s Last Theorem; Galois Groups Acting on Ideal Class Groups; Cyclotomic Fields of Class Number One; Measures and Distributions; Iwasawa’s Theory of Zp-extensions; The Kronecker–Weber Theorem; The Main Conjecture and Annihilation of Class Groups; Miscellany: [publisher TOC](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-1934-7).
- **Scope/effort assessment (inference).** **550–750 nodes (1.65–2.25 PMA); multi-year.** The Main Conjecture and Iwasawa chapters make this unlike a compact textbook assembly project.
- **Lean coverage (web-verified positives).** `flt-regular` targets FLT for regular primes and its README points to `Mathlib.NumberTheory.Cyclotomic.*`; it is a theorem project, not a Washington book project: [repository](https://github.com/leanprover-community/flt-regular). The published 2025 account proves the regular case completely, including Kummer's lemma: [Annals of Formalized Mathematics](https://afm.episciences.org/16046). Separately, `p-adic-L-functions` formalizes Kubota–Leopoldt p-adic L-functions in **Lean 3**, following constructions in Washington Chapter 12; the paper calls this first work in any theorem prover, not a book formalization: [paper and software link](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14491v1). `acmepjz/lean-iwasawa` describes itself as tentative: [repository](https://github.com/acmepjz/lean-iwasawa). None supplies ordered 16-chapter coverage. GitHub API query `"Introduction to Cyclotomic Fields" lean` returned **0 repositories** on 2026-10-21: [query](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=%22Introduction%20to%20Cyclotomic%20Fields%22%20lean). **No complete/near-complete book verified; medium confidence.**
- **Other proof assistants (unverified negative).** Exact-title Coq/Isabelle searches on 2026-10-21 found no complete/near-complete book development; confidence **low-to-medium**. The 2023 p-adic-L-function paper's “first in any theorem prover” statement is positive historical evidence for that isolated topic, not for the whole book: [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.14491v1).
- **Current Mathlib readiness (web-verified).** Dirichlet characters and Gauss sums: [DirichletCharacter.GaussSum](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/DirichletCharacter/GaussSum.html); Dirichlet L-function continuation/functional equation: [DirichletContinuation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/DirichletContinuation.html); cyclotomic discriminants: [Cyclotomic.Discriminant](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Cyclotomic/Discriminant.html); the Dedekind zeta residue/class-number formula: [NumberField.DedekindZeta](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/NumberField/DedekindZeta.html); and the Amice transform relating measures and power series: [Padics.Measure.AmiceTransform](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Padics/Measure/AmiceTransform.html). `RingTheory.RootsOfUnity.CyclotomicUnits` currently collects elementary associated-unit identities, not Washington's full cyclotomic-unit theory: [CyclotomicUnits](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/RootsOfUnity/CyclotomicUnits.html).
- **Gap signal (web-verified/local audit).** The current master-tree path audit found no `Iwasawa` number-theory module (only an unrelated group-action file), `Stickelberger`, or `PadicLFunction` path. This is not a declaration-level proof of absence, but it confirms that no obvious named infrastructure exists at those paths. The p-adic L-function project cited above is Lean 3, so a Lean 4 port/reconciliation must be checked rather than assumed.
- **Downstream value (inference).** The book would connect cyclotomic fields, class numbers, p-adic L-functions, Euler systems, and Iwasawa modules, with immediate reuse in regular-prime FLT and modern algebraic number theory.
- **Risks.** Measures/distributions, Iwasawa's construction, class-number-one computations, the Main Conjecture/Euler-system arguments, and bookkeeping at p=2 are research-scale formalization blocks. Existing famous endpoints reduce duplication but do not establish chapter order or full statements.

#### 5. Joseph H. Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*, 2nd ed. — canonical but largest technical commitment

- **Exact text and teaching status (web-verified).** Target the 2009 second edition, GTM 106, Springer, XX+513 pages: [publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-09494-6). Springer calls it a thorough introduction and prints reviews describing it as a standard reference and popular introductory text; it is used as the textbook in graduate courses, e.g. the University of Waterloo reading course: [Waterloo CO 690](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~djao/co690.2007/).
- **TOC (web-verified).** Eleven chapters: Algebraic Varieties; Algebraic Curves; The Geometry of Elliptic Curves; The Formal Group of an Elliptic Curve; Elliptic Curves over Finite Fields; Elliptic Curves over C; Elliptic Curves over Local Fields; Elliptic Curves over Global Fields; Integral Points on Elliptic Curves; Computing the Mordell–Weil Group; Algorithmic Aspects of Elliptic Curves. The publisher also lists three appendices: characteristics 2 and 3, group cohomology, and an overview of advanced topics: [publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-387-09494-6).
- **Scope/effort assessment (inference).** **600–850 nodes (1.8–2.6 PMA), including appendices; multi-year.** The algebraic-geometry foundation, local/global arithmetic, algorithms, and appendix survey are separate workstreams.
- **Lean coverage (web-verified positives).** Angdinata and Xu formalized nonsingular points of Weierstrass curves and the group law in arbitrary characteristic: [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10640). That is a major theorem cluster, not Silverman coverage. GitHub API query `"Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves" lean` returned **0 repositories** on 2026-10-21: [query](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=%22Arithmetic%20of%20Elliptic%20Curves%22%20lean). **No complete/near-complete ordered book verified; medium confidence.**
- **Other proof assistants (web-verified partial; negative provisional).** The 2022 Mordell–Weil project report records Coq developments that stopped at the group law and itself documents an ongoing Lean attempt, not a completed Mordell–Weil theorem or book: [project report](https://multramate.github.io/projects/mw/main.pdf). Exact-title Coq/Isabelle searches on 2026-10-21 found no complete/near-complete non-Lean book; confidence **low-to-medium**.
- **Current Mathlib readiness and documented gaps (web-verified).** In addition to the modules above, `RingTheory.DedekindDomain.SelmerGroup` defines K(S,n) but lists the maps, exactness, and global finiteness proofs as TODOs: [SelmerGroup](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/DedekindDomain/SelmerGroup.html). `NumberTheory.Height.Northcott` lists projectivization instances as TODOs: [Height.Northcott](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Height/Northcott.html). ### Inferences

#### Top candidate A — Atiyah–Macdonald chapter-by-chapter implementation sketch

This is an **implementation design**, not a claim that these chapters are already formalized. Module names above are web-verified; the sequencing and hard-point analysis are inference. Run a Chapter 11 pilot first to test feasibility, then deliver source nodes in book order.

1. **Rings and Ideals.** Map ring homomorphisms, ideals, quotients, prime/maximal ideals, nilradical/Jacobson radical, ideal operations, extension/contraction, and Chinese remainder to the existing `Ideal`, `RingHom`, quotient, and radical APIs. **Hard points:** preserve the zero-ring convention; distinguish ideal extension from the image of a set; avoid creating a parallel elementary API.
2. **Modules.** Cover submodules, quotient modules, finite generation, exact sequences, tensor products, restriction/extension of scalars, algebras, and tensor exactness using `Module`, `Submodule`, `LinearMap`, and `TensorProduct`. **Hard points:** universe management, equality of bundled maps, and faithful right-exactness statements.
3. **Rings and Modules of Fractions.** Use `IsLocalization`, `Localization`, `IsFractionRing`, and the localization/module APIs for local properties and extended/contracted ideals. **Hard points:** transport between an explicit quotient construction and an arbitrary localization; keep denominators and extension/contraction equalities definitionally manageable.
4. **Primary Decomposition.** Use `Submodule.IsPrimary`, associated primes, `IsLasker`, minimal decompositions, and the two uniqueness theorems in [Lasker](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.html). **Hard points:** colon submodules versus ideal quotients, radical/annihilator correspondence, irredundant indexing, and proof that library “minimal” matches the book.
5. **Integral Dependence and Valuations.** Use `IsIntegral`, `integralClosure`, integrally closed domains, going-up/down, and valuation-ring APIs. **Hard points:** exact going-down hypotheses, normalizations, and choice-heavy existence statements.
6. **Chain Conditions.** Reuse `IsNoetherian`, `IsArtinian`, finite-length and composition-series machinery. **Hard points:** align ring-versus-module typeclasses and avoid duplicating definitions with subtly different simplification behavior.
7. **Noetherian Rings.** Assemble Hilbert basis, Noetherian-module results, primary decomposition, and the chapter's Nullstellensatz consequences. **Hard points:** select the library's equivalent formulations and document every source-node correspondence rather than claiming the chapter from topic coverage.
8. **Artin Rings.** Use `IsArtinianRing`, Hopkins–Levitzki, and `isArtinianRing_iff_isNoetherianRing_krullDimLE_zero` from [HopkinsLevitzki](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/HopkinsLevitzki.html). **Hard points:** commutative versus noncommutative statements and decomposition into local Artinian factors.
9. **Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains.** Use `IsDiscreteValuationRing`, `IsDedekindDomain`, `FractionalIdeal`, factorization, and adic valuations. **Hard points:** equivalent definitions, valuation normalization, and conversion between ordinary and fractional ideals.
10. **Completions.** Cover topologies, filtrations, graded modules, associated graded rings, and adic completion, using `AdicCompletion.map_exact` from [AdicCompletion.Exactness](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Exactness.html). **Hard points:** reconcile algebraic inverse-limit completion with uniform-space completion; prove natural comparison isomorphisms and the required Artin–Rees/graded statements rather than changing the book's statements.
11. **Dimension Theory.** Combine `ringKrullDim`, `Ideal.height`, [Krull's height theorem](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/KrullsHeightTheorem.html), [polynomial dimension](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/KrullDimension/Polynomial.html), regular-local-ring APIs, and [Polynomial.hilbertPoly](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/HilbertPoly.html). **Hard points:** the documented missing graded-module Hilbert polynomial layer and the equivalences among the book's local-dimension characterizations. This chapter is the schedule-critical new fieldlet; if the pilot fails, do not relabel Chapters 1–10 as complete-book coverage.

#### Top candidate B — Serre, *A Course in Arithmetic*, chapter-by-chapter implementation sketch

Audit/reuse the existing Chapter 1 project where statements are faithful, and pilot Chapters 3–5 before promising the book. Then proceed in order with the same strict node and axiom review.

1. **Finite Fields.** Assemble finite-field existence/uniqueness, cyclic multiplicative groups, power sums, quadratic characters/reciprocity, and [Chevalley–Warning](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/ChevalleyWarning.html). **Hard points:** coherent q/cardinality notation, character codomains, and reconciliation with the four existing Chapter 1 files.
2. **p-Adic Fields.** Use `Padic`, `PadicInt`, valuation, completion, inverse-limit, and Hensel APIs for the algebraic/topological development and square/unit computations. **Hard points:** inverse limits versus Cauchy completion, topology/algebra comparison, and the exceptional p=2 calculations.
3. **Hilbert Symbol.** Define the real and p-adic symbols, establish bilinearity/nondegeneracy and the global product formula from the preceding chapters. **Hard points:** this is the first major new API; places, finite support, normalization, and p=2 must be designed before automation. No `HasseMinkowski`/`HilbertSymbol` path was found in the name audit, so budget it as new work rather than assuming it away.
4. **Quadratic Forms over Qp and over Q.** Build rank/discriminant/Hasse invariants on `QuadraticForm` and `Isometry`, prove the local classifications, then Hasse–Minkowski and the rational classification. **Hard points:** scalar extension and faithful local–global statements; this is the principal mathematical spike. The axiomatized [LeanGenius artifact](https://leangenius.org/proof/hilbert-11) must not be imported as evidence or as a proof.
5. **Integral Quadratic Forms with Discriminant ±1.** Develop integral lattices, unimodular/even forms, signatures, and the arithmetic consequences needed for later theta identities. **Hard points:** integral-versus-rational equivalence, basis-change bookkeeping, explicit examples, and definite/indefinite classification. Do not replace the book's integral statements with easier rational-form statements.
6. **The Theorem on Arithmetic Progressions.** Reuse Dirichlet characters, L-functions, and [PrimesInAP](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/PrimesInAP.html). **Hard points:** Serre's Dirichlet-density formulation is richer than merely an infinite-set corollary; retain the density and nonvanishing statements in the book's order.
7. **Modular Forms.** Map classical level-one definitions to `ModularForm`, slash actions, cusps, q-expansions, Eisenstein series, the discriminant, and theta functions; use the existing [dimension formula](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/DimensionFormula.html) and [E4/E6 graded-ring results](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/GradedRing.html). **Hard points:** exact transformation/cusp conventions, q-expansion coefficients, and theta identities. Existing endpoint theorems reduce proof work but still require a source-by-source node inventory.

## Which attractive books are already covered by projects such as Lean central/symposium repositories, Coq, Isabelle, or others?

### Takeaway

None of the recommended five is verified as already covered by a complete Lean or non-Lean book project. The important attractive **reject** is Ireland–Rosen: despite excellent topical support, Chapter 20 is explicitly a survey without proofs, so strict every-theorem coverage would import research-level obligations rather than permit a Chapters 1–19 “complete” label.
