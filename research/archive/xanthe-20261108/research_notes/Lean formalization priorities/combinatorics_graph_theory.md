# Combinatorics and graph theory books for Lean 4 complete coverage

## Which books are canonical and right-sized, and which are multi-volume or too advanced?

### Takeaway

The strongest candidates are **Wilf, _generatingfunctionology_ (3rd ed.)** and **Bollobás, _Graph Theory: An Introductory Course_ (1st ed.)**: compact, canonical, useful beyond their own chapters, and not already completed as books in any proof assistant found in this audit. _Concrete Mathematics_ is the best broad discrete-mathematics follow-up; Alon–Spencer is higher-value but substantially harder; Bóna is broad and teachable but less focused. Diestel is **not** a sensible next complete-coverage book under the strict “every theorem, zero `sorry`s” rule because its Graph Minors chapter explicitly supplies only an overview of a proof taking “well over 500 pages.”

### Cited Findings

**Calibration used below.** The assignment supplies Rudin PMA ≈330 main-text definition/theorem nodes as mid-size and requires every definition and theorem in order, zero `sorry`s, standard axioms only, faithful statements, and hostile review. Estimates here use the same main-text convention: include definitions, lemmas, propositions, theorems, and corollaries, including unnumbered ones; exclude exercises, hints/solutions, indices, and bibliographies. A worked example counts only when it introduces a definition or proves a result, not merely because it displays a formula. Effort classes are judgments, not publisher claims:

- **M:** a single-book programme, roughly 0.3–0.8 PMA-equivalent nodes, without a known book-sized external prerequisite.
- **L:** a single-book but multi-stage/team programme, roughly 0.8–1.7 PMA or several missing subfields.
- **XL:** multi-year, or dependent on another multi-year development; over 1.7 PMA or a monumental external theorem.

Mechanical scans were used only as lower-bound anchors, not as completed inventories. OCR can split or miss labels, and unnumbered main-text nodes require human judgment.

#### 1. Herbert S. Wilf, _generatingfunctionology_, 3rd edition (A K Peters/CRC Press, 2005), ISBN 9781568812793 — **recommended first, M**

- **Canonical/teaching status and exact scope.** The publisher identifies the third edition and its five-chapter spine: (1) Introductory Ideas and Examples, (2) Series, (3) Cards, Decks, and Hands: The Exponential Formula, (4) Applications of Generating Functions, and (5) Analytic and Asymptotic Methods. It calls generating functions a bridge between discrete mathematics and continuous analysis and prints George Andrews’s statement that he would use it when teaching. [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/generatingfunctionology-Third-Edition/Wilf/p/book/9781568812793) The third-edition section TOC adds “Waiting for a String” and “Blocks of 1” in Chapter 4 and places Chapter 5 at page 181. [Third-edition TOC](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html); [Google Books TOC](https://books.google.com.mt/books?id=bVFuBwAAQBAJ&source=gbs_navlinks_s) The author-hosted second-edition preface records testing at a University of Pennsylvania senior course and a classroom test by Bruce Sagan; it also supplies the detailed mathematical provenance of the same five-chapter method. [Wilf, author-hosted PDF](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/gfology2.pdf)
- **Node estimate: 110–160 (0.33–0.48 PMA), medium confidence.** My text scan of the accessible third-edition text found 37 `Theorem`, 7 `Lemma`, 4 `Proposition`, 6 `Corollary`, and 13 `Definition` unique numeric labels, 67 total. The range adds unnumbered definitions and proved main-text statements while excluding exercises, solutions, and the software appendix’s prose. [Source text scanned](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html) This is a small-to-mid-size book, but Chapter 5 makes the effort M rather than S.
- **Mathlib readiness and largest gaps.** Ready building blocks include `PowerSeries` arithmetic, coefficients, substitution, derivatives, inverses, exponentials/logarithms, and multivariate series; `Nat.Partition.genFun`; Catalan and Schröder series; and the pentagonal-number theorem. [PowerSeries directory, pinned](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries); [`Nat.Partition.genFun`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Partition/GenFun.lean); [`PowerSeries.catalanSeries`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries/Catalan.lean); [pentagonal series](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Pentagonal/PowerSeries.html) A substantial adjacent Lean development of Grinberg supplies further infinite products, limits, Laurent series, partitions, and weighted-set generating functions, but it is a different book. [Grinberg formalization](https://github.com/facebookresearch/algebraic-combinatorics) The largest missing pieces are a general exponential-families/species interface, WZ/creative-telescoping machinery, a dedicated Lagrange-inversion theorem, and Hayman-style singularity asymptotics; the pinned path/content audit found no dedicated declarations for these, which is a provisional absence claim, not a logical proof of absence. [Pinned Mathlib tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1)
- **Downstream value and risks.** This is the highest-reuse enumeration project: it supports recurrences, labelled and unlabelled counting, partitions, permutations, graph enumeration, discrete probability, and analytic combinatorics, and would feed directly into _Concrete Mathematics_, Bóna, and selected Stanley material. [Wilf’s own scope statement](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/gfology2.pdf) The critical path is Chapter 5, especially Lagrange inversion and Hayman’s method; Chapter 4’s WZ pairs and cycle index are separate research-level infrastructure spikes. A faithful treatment must also reconcile formal-series identities with analytic convergence rather than silently identifying the two. [Third-edition Chapter 5 TOC](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html)

#### 2. Béla Bollobás, _Graph Theory: An Introductory Course_, 1st edition, GTM 63 (Springer, 1979; electronic reissue 2012), X+180 pp. — **recommended second, L**

- **Canonical/teaching status and exact scope.** Springer identifies the first edition, author, series, 180 pages, and eight chapters: Fundamentals; Electrical Networks; Flows, Connectivity and Matching; Extremal Problems; Colouring; Ramsey Theory; Random Graphs; Graphs and Groups. Its quoted London Mathematical Society review calls the book a “watershed” and predicts a permanent place in the literature. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7) The individual publisher chapter records confirm the same scope and give the central results: [Fundamentals](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_1), [Electrical Networks](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_2), [Flows/Connectivity/Matching](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_3), [Extremal Problems](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_4), [Colouring](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_5), [Ramsey](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_6), [Random Graphs](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_7), [Graphs and Groups](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_8). It is also recommended in Warwick’s MA241 bibliography. [Warwick notes](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/maths/people/staff/keith_ball/combinatorics_notes.pdf)
- **Node estimate: 200–300 (0.6–0.9 PMA), medium-low confidence.** The publisher’s notes show a dense numbered-result presentation, including high-water references to Theorem 14 in Fundamentals, Theorem 20 in Extremal Problems, Theorem 15 in Ramsey Theory, Theorem 14 in Random Graphs, and Theorem 9 in Graphs and Groups; lemmas, corollaries, and the unusually numerous definitions must be added. [Fundamentals](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_1); [Extremal](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_4); [Ramsey](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_6); [Random Graphs](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_7); [Graphs and Groups](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_8) Its 180 pages do **not** make it easy: the eight chapters span Kuratowski, electrical networks, Menger/Hall/Tutte, Erdős–Stone, Brooks/Vizing, Hales–Jewett/van der Waerden/Hindman, random-graph thresholds, and Pólya enumeration.
- **Mathlib readiness and largest gaps.** Particularly strong matches are `SimpleGraph`, the newer explicit-edge `Graph` multigraph, walks/trails/acyclic graphs, `SimpleGraph.tutte`, Hall’s matching theorems, Turán, `eventually_completeEquipartiteGraph_isContained_of_minDegree` (Erdős–Stone), `szemeredi_regularity`, Hales–Jewett/van der Waerden, Hindman, `binomialRandom`, Cayley graphs, and Schreier graphs. [`Graph` multigraph](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Graph/Basic.lean); [Tutte](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Tutte.lean); [Hall](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Hall.lean); [Erdős–Stone](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Extremal/ErdosStoneSimonovits.lean); [Hales–Jewett](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/HalesJewett.lean); [binomial random graphs](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Defs.lean); [Schreier graphs](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Quiver/Schreier.lean) The main gaps are topological planarity and its combinatorial bridge, a general max-flow/min-cut library, Brooks’s theorem, the original finite/infinite Ramsey API, random-graph thresholds/zero-one laws, and Pólya cycle-index enumeration. External Lean projects cover Menger, Vizing, and combinatorial planarity only partially; see the coverage section below.
- **Downstream value and risks.** This would create a reusable core for later Diestel/Bondy–Murty work while avoiding Diestel’s graph-minor-theorem blocker. It also links graphs to linear algebra, probability, and group actions. [Publisher chapter scope](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7) The hard points are not evenly distributed: topological embeddings and Kuratowski; electrical-network geometric certificates; general flows; the four-colour theorem if included as a stated background result; forbidden-subgraph Ramsey theorems; random-graph asymptotics; and Pólya enumeration. In particular, an external Lean planarity library explicitly does **not** formalize the equivalence between its rotation-system planarity and topological embeddings, so it cannot simply be cited as closing Bollobás’s topological statements. [Planarity library limitations](https://github.com/jtraverso/planar-list-colouring)

#### 3. Ronald L. Graham, Donald E. Knuth, and Oren Patashnik, _Concrete Mathematics: A Foundation for Computer Science_, 2nd edition (Addison-Wesley, 1994), xiii+657 pp. — **recommended third, L**

- **Canonical/teaching status and exact scope.** Knuth’s author page gives the exact authors, second edition, date, ISBN 0-201-55802-5, and 657 pages; it says the book grew from the Stanford course he taught from 1970–1989 and contains more than 500 exercises with answers. [Knuth’s book page](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/gkp.html) Its nine chapters are Recurrent Problems; Sums; Integer Functions; Number Theory; Binomial Coefficients; Special Numbers; Generating Functions; Discrete Probability; Asymptotics. [Publisher TOC](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/concrete-mathematics-a/9780134389974/)
- **Node estimate: 350–550 (1.1–1.7 PMA), medium-low confidence.** The range reflects nine long, formula-dense chapters and includes unnumbered identities that are proved as main-text results, but excludes the 500+ exercises and their answers; it does not treat every displayed equation as a separate theorem. [Author page and exercise count](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/gkp.html); [TOC](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/concrete-mathematics-a/9780134389974/) A manual inventory is essential because this book has fewer conventionally labelled `Theorem` environments than the other candidates.
- **Mathlib readiness and largest gaps.** Finite sums and big operators, floor functions, elementary number theory, binomial coefficients, Stirling/Bell/Catalan/partition combinatorics, inclusion–exclusion, formal power series, discrete probability, asymptotics, Stirling’s formula, and Akra–Bazzi provide broad coverage. [Enumerative directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative); [PowerSeries](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries); [asymptotics](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Analysis/Asymptotics); [Stirling’s formula](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Stirling.lean); [Akra–Bazzi](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Computability/AkraBazzi/AkraBazzi.lean) The main gaps are Gosper–Zeilberger mechanical summation, Iverson-bracket conventions, generalized binomial coefficients over all intended upper indices, a named Euler–Maclaurin API, and systematic finite-difference/summation machinery. These are scan-based provisional negatives, not claims that no equivalent theorem could exist under another name. [Pinned tree audit](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1)
- **Downstream value and risks.** Few books would reuse so much basic infrastructure across algorithm analysis, enumeration, number theory, generating functions, probability, and asymptotics; the author page explicitly positions the book as the mathematical basis for advanced programming and algorithm analysis. [Knuth](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/gkp.html) The risks are scope discipline and faithfulness: informal “rules,” conventions, and identities must not disappear merely because they are unnumbered, and the second edition’s Gosper–Zeilberger material is a substantial new formalization field rather than a routine application of existing `ring` tactics. [Second-edition mechanical-summation note](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/gkp.html)

#### 4. Noga Alon and Joel H. Spencer, _The Probabilistic Method_, 4th edition (Wiley, 2016), ISBN 978-1-119-06195-3, 396 pp. — **recommended fourth, L (upper end)**

- **Canonical/teaching status and exact scope.** Wiley calls it the leading reference on probabilistic methods in combinatorics and suitable for upper-undergraduate/graduate students and researchers. [Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Probabilistic+Method%2C+4th+Edition-p-9781119061953) Noga Alon’s Spring 2026 Princeton MAT577/MAT478 course lists this fourth edition first and says most course topics appear in it. [Princeton course](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/prob.html) Part I has nine method chapters (Basic Method; Linearity of Expectation; Alterations; Second Moment; Local Lemma; Correlation Inequalities; Martingales and Tight Concentration; Poisson Paradigm; Quasirandomness); Part II has eight topic chapters (Random Graphs; Erdős–Rényi Phase Transition; Circuit Complexity; Discrepancy; Geometry; Codes, Games, and Entropy; Derandomization; Graph Property Testing), plus large-deviations and Erdős appendices and separate “Probabilistic Lens” results. [Full fourth-edition TOC](https://mathematicaster.org/teaching/lcs22/alon_probcomb.pdf)
- **Node estimate: 280–400 (0.85–1.2 PMA), medium confidence.** My scan of the fourth-edition PDF found 142 `Theorem`, 29 `Lemma`, 10 `Proposition`, and 34 `Corollary` unique numeric labels, 215 theorem-like nodes before unnumbered definitions and the lens material. The range adds those definitions and main-text unnumbered results while excluding exercises. [Source PDF scanned](https://mathematicaster.org/teaching/lcs22/alon_probcomb.pdf)
- **Mathlib readiness and largest gaps.** Strong prerequisites include independence, expectation/variance, martingales, Hoeffding and Azuma–Hoeffding via `measure_sum_ge_le_of_iIndepFun` and `measure_sum_ge_le_of_HasCondSubgaussianMGF`, `SimpleGraph.binomialRandom`, four-functions/FKG/Holley inequalities, regularity, triangle removal, and extremal graph theory. [Variance](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability/Moments/Variance.lean); [sub-Gaussian concentration](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability/Moments/SubGaussian.lean); [random graphs](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Defs.lean); [four functions/FKG](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SetFamily/FourFunctions.lean); [regularity](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Regularity/Lemma.lean) The largest gaps are the Lovász local lemma in Lean, Janson and Talagrand inequalities, Kim–Vu concentration, graphons, hypergraph containers, branching-process phase transitions, VC-dimension infrastructure, circuit complexity, and graph property testing. No dedicated Mathlib module for these was found in the pinned scan; they remain provisional negatives. [Pinned tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1)
- **Downstream value and risks.** This is the best foundation for probabilistic/extremal combinatorics and theoretical CS, and Isabelle already demonstrates that the local-lemma methodology is formalizable. [Isabelle Lovász local lemma](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Lovasz_Local.html) But it has the most heterogeneous prerequisite frontier of the shortlist: probability, analysis, spectral graph theory, information theory, algorithms, complexity, geometry, graph limits, and property testing all appear in one 396-page book. [TOC](https://mathematicaster.org/teaching/lcs22/alon_probcomb.pdf) Treat it as a team programme after Wilf/Bollobás infrastructure exists, not as a compact introduction to formalization.

#### 5. Miklós Bóna, _A Walk Through Combinatorics: An Introduction to Enumeration, Graph Theory, and Selected Other Topics_, 5th edition (World Scientific, 2023), 636 pp. — **recommended fifth, L (borderline XL)**

- **Teaching status and exact scope.** The author confirms that the fifth edition is available. [Bóna’s book list](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/bona/books/) The fifth-edition description identifies 2023, 636 pages, more than 100 new Instant Check exercises, and suitability for undergraduate introductory combinatorics. [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/A_Walk_Through_Combinatorics_An_Introduc.html?id=worVEAAAQBAJ) Its four parts contain 20 chapters: two basic-methods chapters; six enumeration chapters; four graph-theory chapters; and eight “Horizons” chapters on Ramsey theory, permutation patterns, probabilistic method, posets/lattices, designs/codes, unlabelled structures, algorithms, and computational complexity. [Fifth-edition TOC](https://dokumen.pub/a-walk-through-combinatorics-an-introduction-to-enumeration-graph-theory-and-selected-other-topics-5nbsped-9811277842-9789811277849-9789811277856-9789811277863.html); [third-edition TOC for the stable chapter architecture](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf03/UFBI_978-981-4335-23-2_01.pdf) Binghamton used the third edition repeatedly in combinatorics courses, supporting teaching status rather than merely popularity. [Course history](http://people.math.binghamton.edu/zaslav/Oldcourses/)
- **Node estimate: 300–450 (0.9–1.4 PMA), medium confidence.** My scan of the fifth-edition text found 106 `Theorem`, 28 `Lemma`, 28 `Proposition`, 19 `Corollary`, and 62 `Definition` unique numeric labels, 243 total. The range adds unnumbered main-text results while excluding the extensive exercise/solution apparatus. [Source text scanned](https://dokumen.pub/a-walk-through-combinatorics-an-introduction-to-enumeration-graph-theory-and-selected-other-topics-5nbsped-9811277842-9789811277849-9789811277856-9789811277863.html)
- **Mathlib readiness and largest gaps.** Basic counting, induction, binomial/multinomial results, partitions, permutations, inclusion–exclusion, generating functions, elementary graphs, Hall/Tutte, basic probability, posets/lattices, and Turing machines are present; the pinned enumerative, graph, probability, and computability directories document that breadth. [Enumerative](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative); [SimpleGraph](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph); [Computability](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Computability) Gaps include permutation-pattern/stack-sorting infrastructure, block designs and coding theory in the forms used, Pólya enumeration, general graph algorithms, and a full P/NP/NP-complete/Cook–Levin development. The computability scan found polynomial-time predicates but no dedicated NP-completeness module, a provisional negative. [Turing-machine computability](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Computability/TuringMachine/Computable.lean)
- **Downstream value and risks.** It would provide an unusually coherent undergraduate library and a teaching-aligned route from basic counting to graphs and complexity. [Publisher description](https://books.google.com/books/about/A_Walk_Through_Combinatorics_An_Introduc.html?id=worVEAAAQBAJ) The risk is breadth without corresponding reuse per chapter: the last eight chapters open eight new areas, and the complexity chapters create a second formalization programme on their own. It also overlaps Wilf, Bollobás, and Alon–Spencer, so it is less distinctive than its node count suggests. [TOC](https://dokumen.pub/a-walk-through-combinatorics-an-introduction-to-enumeration-graph-theory-and-selected-other-topics-5nbsped-9811277842-9789811277849-9789811277856-9789811277863.html)

#### Attractive rejects

- **Reinhard Diestel, _Graph Theory_, 6th edition, GTM 173 (Springer, 2025), XX+455 pp. — reject for now, XL.** This is unambiguously canonical: the author/publisher call it the standard textbook and provide a 12-chapter free preview. [Author page](https://diestel-graph-theory.com/); [sixth-edition TOC](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/home/diestel/books/graph.theory/preview/Contents.pdf) A rough main-text estimate is 320–450 nodes before any external prerequisites (medium-low confidence). The decisive issue is Chapter 12: it says the graph minor theorem’s proof takes “well over 500 pages” and that the chapter conveys only a rough impression of it. Under the strict every-theorem rule, this is an additional multi-year prerequisite, not an optional bibliographical remark. [Official Chapter 12](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/home/diestel/books/graph.theory/preview/Ch12.pdf) The sixth edition also adds regularity applications and Szemerédi’s theorem, tangles, and a large infinite-graph chapter, increasing rather than reducing risk. [Chapter 7](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/home/diestel/books/graph.theory/preview/Ch7.pdf); [TOC](https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/home/diestel/books/graph.theory/preview/Contents.pdf) Existing Menger/minor/treewidth work is partial and does not solve this blocker. [Coq graph library](https://hal.science/hal-02127698/document); [Prove2Me Menger mission](https://prove2.me/theorems/627d82b4-76e4-4da5-8269-6df695702902)
- **J. A. Bondy and U. S. R. Murty, _Graph Theory_, 1st edition, GTM 244 (Springer, 2008), XII+663 pp. — reject as a first graph book, XL.** Springer confirms edition/status and describes the dual textbook/research-introduction role. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-84628-970-5) The 21 chapters cover graphs through algorithms, complexity, connectivity, planarity, four colours, probabilistic method, matchings, Hamilton cycles, directed packings, electrical networks, and integer flows. [Full TOC](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf/z01_978-1-84628-969-9_01.pdf) A rough 450–700-node estimate and the number of independent advanced topics make this multi-year and duplicative of Bollobás plus Diestel rather than right-sized; no complete whole-book formalization was found in the audit (medium-confidence non-discovery, not a disqualification).
- **Richard P. Stanley, _Enumerative Combinatorics_, Volume 1, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 2012), and Volume 2, 2nd ed. (Cambridge, 2023) — reject as a programme-sized/multi-volume and too advanced target, XL.** Cambridge calls the two-volume work the standard guide and describes Volume 2’s generating functions, algebraic/D-finite/noncommutative series, symmetric functions, RSK, and Fomin appendix, with over 400 exercises in Volume 2 alone. [Cambridge Volume 2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/enumerative-combinatorics/360F1EEA6B91AE359EE489AC4145EF49) Volume 1’s second edition adds ten sections and more than 350 exercises. [Volume 1 preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139200561_A24436694/preview-9781139200561_A24436694.pdf) Even Volume 1 alone is a large graduate project; the full two-volume target is roughly estimated at 800–1200 main-text nodes (low confidence) and requires algebraic combinatorics far beyond Wilf. Existing work is explicitly sectional, e.g. Rocq’s Stanley EC1 §§1.4/1.6 project and Coq-Combi’s symmetric-function corpus, not whole-book coverage. [mathcomp-eulerian](https://github.com/LLM4Rocq/mathcomp-eulerian); [Coq-Combi](https://github.com/math-comp/Coq-Combi)
- **J. H. van Lint and R. M. Wilson, _A Course in Combinatorics_, 2nd edition (Cambridge, 2001), xiv+602 pp. — reject for breadth/specialized prerequisites, XL.** The primary preview gives 38 chapters spanning graphs, extremal set theory, flows, enumeration, partitions, Latin squares, Hadamard matrices, designs/codes, strongly regular graphs, geometries, association schemes, surface embeddings, Pólya theory, and Baranyai’s theorem. [Publisher preview/TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780511668777_A23679336/preview-9780511668777_A23679336.pdf) It is canonical and currently taught—Nanjing’s Spring 2026 course assigns the second edition—but the authors themselves say every chapter was presented in class without ever managing all chapters in one year. [Nanjing course](https://tcs.nju.edu.cn/wiki/index.php?title=%E7%BB%84%E5%90%88%E6%95%B0%E5%AD%A6_%28Spring_2026%29); [authors’ preface](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780511668777_A23679336/preview-9780511668777_A23679336.pdf) A rough 450–700-node estimate and the number of unrelated design/coding/geometric libraries make it less reusable per unit effort than the shortlist; no complete whole-book formalization was found (medium-confidence non-discovery).

### Inferences

- **Recommended order:** Wilf → Bollobás → _Concrete Mathematics_ → Alon–Spencer → Bóna. Wilf has the best node/reuse ratio; Bollobás is the best graph foundation; _Concrete Mathematics_ generalizes the same summation/generating-function payoff; Alon–Spencer should wait for probability and graph infrastructure; Bóna is worthwhile but least focused. These judgments follow from the cited TOCs and the pinned readiness audit, not from book reputation alone.
- **Do not substitute “famous and compact” for the strict completion contract.** Diestel is only 455 pages, but its own text forces the 500+ page graph-minor proof into scope if every stated theorem must be proved. Bollobás avoids that particular blocker, although its background four-colour and topological-planarity statements still require an explicit inventory decision and proof plan.
- **No serious candidate is disqualified on current evidence.** None was found with complete or near-complete whole-book Lean coverage; none was found with complete non-Lean whole-book coverage either. The partial projects below reduce work but must not be promoted to book completion in the report.

### Gaps

- All node ranges are planning estimates, not finished node inventories. Wilf, Alon–Spencer, and Bóna have mechanical lower-bound anchors; Bollobás and _Concrete Mathematics_ especially need manual annotation before scheduling. Exercises are excluded only because the supplied PMA calibration is main-text; including exercise theorems would require a new, larger inventory rather than silent scope expansion or contraction.
- Publisher metadata conflict on Wilf’s page count: Routledge displays 256 pages while Google Books displays 192, although its TOC reaches the back cover at 243. The edition and five-chapter scope are secure; the page-count discrepancy is not used to derive the node range. [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/generatingfunctionology-Third-Edition/Wilf/p/book/9781568812793); [Google Books](https://books.google.com.mt/books?id=bVFuBwAAQBAJ&source=gbs_navlinks_s)
- “No complete project found” is always a bounded web/repository audit, not a universal negative. Private projects, misnamed repositories, and very new work may escape title searches.

## What graph/combinatorics infrastructure is actually present or absent in Mathlib?

### Takeaway

Current Mathlib is much stronger than a graph-theory skeptic might expect: finite simple graphs, matchings, extremal results, regularity, enumerative combinatorics, formal power series, finite probability, concentration, and even binomial random graphs are present. The genuine blockers are representation-sensitive planarity/minors/flows, several colouring and Pólya APIs, analytic generating-function asymptotics, and the more specialized probabilistic/complexity machinery. Absence statements below are **scan-backed but provisional**, never inferred merely from an unfamiliar module name.

### Cited Findings

**Audit pin and method.** I made a sparse checkout of Mathlib master at commit `9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1` (2026-10-21), inspected the full Git tree, and searched relevant source contents for graph, enumeration, power-series, probability, and computability terms. [Exact repository tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1) This is stronger than a filename-only check for the searched directories, but it is not a proof that an equivalent declaration is absent everywhere under a different name.

**Present: graph objects and core theory.**

- `SimpleGraph V` models an irreflexive symmetric adjacency relation and supplies neighbor sets, common neighbors, incidence sets, and a complete lattice of spanning subgraphs. [`SimpleGraph.Basic`](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Basic.html) A separate `Graph α β` now models multigraphs with explicit vertex/edge sets and loops; its docstring explicitly anticipates future minor orders, so it should not be mistaken for a finished replacement for all `SimpleGraph` APIs. [`Graph.Basic`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Graph/Basic.lean)
- The `SimpleGraph` directory contains walks and walks-as-paths, connectivity, acyclic graphs/trees, bipartite graphs, cliques, colouring, Hamiltonian paths, adjacency/incidence/Laplacian matrices, matchings, and ends. [Directory, pinned](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph)
- Matching is unusually ready: `exists_isMatching_of_forall_ncard_le` and `exists_isPerfectMatching_of_forall_ncard_le` give Hall’s theorem, while `SimpleGraph.tutte` characterizes perfect matchings by absence of Tutte violators. [`Hall.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Hall.lean); [`Tutte.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Tutte.lean)
- Extremal graph theory includes `turanGraph` and the full Turán characterization, `eventually_completeEquipartiteGraph_isContained_of_minDegree` for Erdős–Stone, Turán density, and triangle removal. [`Turan.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Extremal/Turan.lean); [`ErdosStoneSimonovits.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Extremal/ErdosStoneSimonovits.lean); [`Triangle/Removal.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Triangle/Removal.lean)
- `szemeredi_regularity` produces an equipartition with a bounded number of parts and `P.IsUniform G ε`; the module explicitly lists the diagonal/degree/partition-regularity variants as TODO, so it is not every formulation used by every textbook. [`Regularity/Lemma.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Regularity/Lemma.lean); [documentation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Regularity/Lemma.html)
- Ramsey-adjacent infrastructure includes Hales–Jewett (`Line.exists_mono_in_high_dimension`), a generalized van der Waerden statement (`exists_mono_homothetic_copy`), and strong/weak Hindman theorems (`FS_partition_regular`, `exists_FS_of_finite_cover`). [`HalesJewett.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/HalesJewett.lean); [`Hindman.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Hindman.lean)
- Algebraic graph connections include `SimpleGraph.mulCayley`/`addCayley` and the labelled `SchreierGraph` quiver with reachability results. [`Cayley.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Cayley.lean); [`Quiver/Schreier.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Quiver/Schreier.lean)

**Present: enumerative combinatorics and generating functions.**

- The `RingTheory/PowerSeries` directory has basic arithmetic, derivatives, inverses, substitution, expansion, exponentials, logarithms, binomial and Catalan series, topology, and Weierstrass preparation; `MvPowerSeries` supplies the multivariate counterpart. [`PowerSeries`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries); [`MvPowerSeries`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/MvPowerSeries)
- The enumerative directory includes Catalan, Schröder, Stirling, Bell, compositions, inclusion–exclusion, partitions, pentagonal identities, Young diagrams, and incidence algebras. [Directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative) Specific useful declarations include `PowerSeries.catalanSeries` and its recurrence, `Nat.Partition.genFun` and `hasProd_genFun`, and `PowerSeries.pentagonalSeries` with the two-sided pentagonal identity. [`Catalan.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries/Catalan.lean); [`Partition/GenFun.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Partition/GenFun.lean); [pentagonal documentation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Pentagonal/PowerSeries.html)
- The Grinberg project is external to Mathlib but highly relevant: its current README reports 340 target theorems across 45 chapters, with formal power series, infinite products, partitions, permutations, determinants, and symmetric functions. It is not evidence of Wilf/Bóna/_Concrete Mathematics_ completion and must be reviewed independently before reuse. [Repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/algebraic-combinatorics)

**Present: probability and adjacent methods.**

- `SimpleGraph.binomialRandom V p` constructs the binomial random-graph measure and proves it is a probability measure; the singleton formula computes the probability of each finite graph. [`BinomialRandomGraph/Defs.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Defs.lean)
- Independence, conditional probability, expectation/variance, martingales, binomial distributions, and Borel–Cantelli are available. [Probability directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability)
- `Kernel.HasSubgaussianMGF`, `HasCondSubgaussianMGF`, and `HasSubgaussianMGF` support Hoeffding’s lemma/inequality and Azuma–Hoeffding through `measure_sum_ge_le_of_iIndepFun` and `measure_sum_ge_le_of_HasCondSubgaussianMGF`. [`Moments/SubGaussian.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability/Moments/SubGaussian.lean)
- `four_functions_theorem`, `holley`, and `fkg` cover the Ahlswede–Daykin/FKG correlation backbone used by Alon–Spencer. [`SetFamily/FourFunctions.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SetFamily/FourFunctions.lean)

**Not found in the pinned scan (all provisional negatives).**

- No dedicated `SimpleGraph` development of topological planarity, graph minors/tree decompositions/treewidth, Menger, Vizing, Brooks, Pólya cycle indices, or general network max-flow/min-cut was found. The colouring file still lists planar graphs and chromatic polynomials as TODO. [`Coloring/Vertex.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Coloring/Vertex.lean) This does **not** negate the external Menger/Vizing/planarity projects described below.
- No dedicated combinatorial-species/exponential-formula, Lagrange-inversion, Hayman-asymptotics, WZ/Gosper–Zeilberger, or named Euler–Maclaurin module was found. The full tree has no matching path for `species`, `zeilberger`, `hayman`, `gosper`, or `eulermaclaurin`; relevant power-series sources were also content-searched. [Pinned tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1) Confidence is medium for species/WZ/Hayman, lower for Euler–Maclaurin and differently named Lagrange results.
- No dedicated Lovász-local-lemma, Talagrand-inequality, graphon, hypergraph-container, or graph-property-testing development was found; nor was a full Cook–Levin/NP-complete development. These are the main Alon–Spencer/Bóna frontier gaps. [Pinned tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1) Polynomial-time Turing-machine predicates do exist, so it would be wrong to say computational complexity is wholly absent. [`TuringMachine/Computable.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Computability/TuringMachine/Computable.lean)

### Inferences

- **Wilf can start immediately**, but Chapter 5 must be scheduled as an analysis project from day one. **Bollobás can start with Chapters I–IV and VII**, while planarity, colouring, Ramsey, and Pólya gaps are attacked in parallel. **Alon–Spencer should not start as a single linear crawl**: its local-lemma, graphon/container, concentration, and complexity gaps require independent workstreams.
- **Representation review is as important as theorem review.** `SimpleGraph`’s relation-based graphs, `Graph`’s explicit-edge multigraphs, `Quiver`’s labelled directed graphs, and external rotation-system planarity are not interchangeable by wishful thinking. A hostile review should require explicit equivalence proofs or a documented reason that a book statement uses exactly the chosen representation.
- **External results do not become Mathlib readiness merely by existing.** They need version reconciliation, statement comparison, axiom reports, and dependency audits before being counted as completed nodes.

### Gaps

- I did not run a full Mathlib build or `#print axioms` on every cited declaration; declarations and docstrings were source-verified at the pinned commit. A downstream project must still check standard-axiom dependencies and actual statement fidelity.
- Declaration-level absence cannot be proved by the performed scan. In particular, generic names can hide Lagrange inversion, Ramsey, flows, or Euler–Maclaurin results. Every “not found” statement above should be rechecked with Mathlib’s semantic search at implementation time.
- Current Mathlib is moving quickly. The 2026 presence of Erdős–Stone, Tutte, and binomial random graphs means older claims about missing graph infrastructure are stale; the pinned commit, not training memory, is the basis for this report.

### Takeaway

The evidence instead shows a rich but fragmented collection of selected chapters, named theorems, course notes, and adjacent books. Complete non-Lean results such as the Four Colour Theorem do not disqualify a candidate; they also do not establish coverage of the book in which that result is mentioned.

### Inferences

## Top-two chapter-by-chapter formalization sketches and hard points

### Takeaway

Wilf is the cleanest complete-book programme: five chapters with a tractable algebraic core and a difficult analytic finale. Bollobás is the best graph programme: eight dense chapters whose early core is reusable but whose planarity, Ramsey, random-graph, and Pólya material needs independent spikes. Both plans include every main-text definition/theorem in source order; statements given without proof in the book are inventory items, not permission for `sorry`.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Wilf, _generatingfunctionology_, 3rd ed. — five-chapter plan

Source of chapter/section order: [third-edition TOC](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html); author’s detailed second-edition text for mathematical cross-checking: [Wilf PDF](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/gfology2.pdf). Start with a source-indexed manifest and preserve the third edition’s added sections rather than silently formalizing the second edition.

1. **Chapter 1, Introductory Ideas and Examples (§§1.1–1.7).** Formalize the recurrence-solving method, ordinary and two-variable generating functions, coefficient extraction, binomial coefficients, set partitions, and Stirling-number examples in the book’s order. Use `PowerSeries`, `MvPowerSeries`, finite sums, binomial coefficients, and `Combinatorics/Enumerative/Stirling`; reuse the Grinberg Fibonacci/recurrence examples only after statement comparison. [PowerSeries](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries); [Grinberg recurrence blueprint](https://faabian.github.io/algebraic-combinatorics/blueprint/index.html) **Hard points:** the three-term boundary-value problem and cubic-spline application require explicit finite linear systems and root cases; two-sided summation conventions and generalized binomial coefficients need a single reviewed convention, not ad hoc reindexing. [Chapter 1 text](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/gfology2.pdf)
2. **Chapter 2, Series (§§2.1–2.7).** Build ordinary and exponential generating-function calculi, then analytic power series and formal Dirichlet series. Use `PowerSeries.Derivative`, `Inverse`, `Substitution`, `Exp`, `Log`, `Binomial`, the multivariate API, and Mathlib’s complex-analysis/asymptotics libraries. [PowerSeries directory](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries); [Dirichlet L-series-related modules](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries) **Hard points:** formal equality versus analytic convergence, exponential-versus-ordinary coefficient conventions, composition/inversion hypotheses, and multiplicativity of formal Dirichlet series. A general formal Dirichlet-series ring was not found and should be scheduled rather than assumed from Dirichlet L-functions.
3. **Chapter 3, Cards, Decks, and Hands: The Exponential Formula (§§3.1–3.19).** Introduce exponential families and the main counting theorems, then instantiate permutations/cycles, set partitions, restricted permutations, involutions, 2-regular graphs, connected graphs, labelled bipartite graphs, labelled trees, binomial-type polynomials, unlabelled cards/hands, money changing, integer partitions, and rooted trees/forests. Use finite types, `Equiv.Perm`, set partitions, `Nat.Partition.genFun`, power-series composition/exp/log, and weighted-set ideas from Grinberg. [`Partition/GenFun.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Partition/GenFun.lean); [Grinberg weighted sets](https://github.com/facebookresearch/algebraic-combinatorics) **Hard points:** there is no dedicated species/exponential-family interface in the pinned scan; labelled-versus-unlabelled equality must be proved through actual equivalences of finite structures, and the connected-versus-all logarithm must track nonzero constant coefficients correctly. Rooted-tree and connected-graph counts are excellent integration tests for the entire API.
4. **Chapter 4, Applications of Generating Functions (§§4.1–4.13).** Formalize averages, the sieve, Snake Oil, WZ pairs, unimodality/convexity, congruences, the symmetric-group cycle index, permutation square roots, polyominoes, exact covering sequences, then the third edition’s waiting-for-string and blocks-of-1 sections. Use inclusion–exclusion, formal series, finite probability, group actions, and the existing partition/permutation libraries. [`InclusionExclusion.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/InclusionExclusion.lean); [Probability](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability) **Hard points:** WZ/creative telescoping has no general engine in the pinned scan; cycle-index/Pólya infrastructure is also absent; unimodality requires real-polynomial/root analysis; polyominoes and exact covers create finite-geometry and recursive-type burdens. Do not mistake an LLM-generated WZ proof sketch for a kernel-checked general WZ implementation.
5. **Chapter 5, Analytic and Asymptotic Methods (§§5.1–5.5).** Formalize Lagrange inversion, asymptotics from poles, algebraic singularities, and Hayman’s method, reusing the formal-series identities from Chapters 1–4 and Mathlib’s complex analysis, Gamma/Stirling, and asymptotics libraries. [`Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Stirling.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Stirling.lean); [`Analysis/Asymptotics`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Analysis/Asymptotics) **Hard points:** a dedicated Lagrange-inversion theorem was not found; singularity analysis requires careful analytic continuation and contour/hypothesis management; Hayman’s method is the largest isolated gap and should have an early feasibility prototype before claiming the whole book is on schedule. [Chapter 5 TOC](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html)
6. **Appendix and solutions.** The Maple/Mathematica appendix, exercise solutions, and exercises are not main-text definition/theorem nodes under the supplied PMA calibration, but they must be explicitly marked out of scope in the manifest rather than silently omitted. [Full third-edition TOC](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html)

#### B. Bollobás, _Graph Theory: An Introductory Course_, 1st ed. — eight-chapter plan

Source of chapter order and principal topics: [publisher book record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7) and the eight linked chapter records below. Begin with a representation charter covering `SimpleGraph`, explicit-edge `Graph`, directed graphs, plane/topological graphs, and their equivalence proofs.

1. **Chapter I, Fundamentals.** Formalize graph variants and terminology, paths/cycles/trees, Hamilton cycles and Euler circuits, planar graphs, and the Euler-trail application to algebra. Use `SimpleGraph`, `Graph`, walks/trails/acyclic/Hamiltonian APIs, and adjacency matrices. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_1); [`Graph` multigraph](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Graph/Basic.lean) **Hard points:** multigraphs and loops; topological planarity/Kuratowski rather than merely rotation systems; and the Amitsur–Levitzki application, which belongs to noncommutative algebra rather than elementary graph API. The external Kuratowski/Wagner library can supply mathematical ideas but its own topological-equivalence gap remains. [External planarity limitation](https://github.com/jtraverso/planar-list-colouring)
2. **Chapter II, Electrical Networks.** Formalize conductance/resistance networks, Kirchhoff’s laws, squared rectangles/squares, and the vector spaces/matrices associated with graphs. Use explicit-edge multigraphs, linear algebra, `AdjMatrix`, and `LapMatrix`. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_2); [`LapMatrix.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/LapMatrix.lean) **Hard points:** real-valued currents and potentials, matrix-tree-type identities, and explicit geometric dissection certificates. Numerical examples must be kernel-checked or proved symbolically, not accepted from a picture.
3. **Chapter III, Flows, Connectivity and Matching.** Formalize max-flow/min-cut, Menger, Hall, Tutte, and the chapter’s connectivity variants, retaining the book’s multiple proof routes when they introduce new lemmas. Use `Digraph`/`Graph`, Hall and Tutte in Mathlib, and the external Menger development after code/statement audit. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_3); [`Hall.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Hall.lean); [`Tutte.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Tutte.lean); [Menger mission](https://prove2.me/theorems/627d82b4-76e4-4da5-8269-6df695702902) **Hard points:** no general max-flow/min-cut library was found; Menger’s set/vertex/directed/multigraph forms need proved equivalences; Mader-type connectivity results are not covered by Hall/Tutte.
4. **Chapter IV, Extremal Problems.** Formalize forbidden paths/cycles and Hamilton degree conditions, Turán and Erdős–Stone, and the structure of extremal graphs. Reuse `Extremal/Turan`, `ErdosStoneSimonovits`, `TuranDensity`, and Hamiltonian APIs. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_4); [`Turan.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Extremal/Turan.lean); [`ErdosStoneSimonovits.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Extremal/ErdosStoneSimonovits.lean) **Hard points:** k-closures and Chvátal-type degree sequences, Thomason’s Hamiltonian result, the Bollobás–Erdős extension, and exact asymptotic/extremal hypotheses. Existing Erdős–Stone is a minimum-degree form, so statement-by-statement comparison is mandatory.
5. **Chapter V, Colouring.** Formalize vertex/edge colouring, Brooks and Vizing, maps/surfaces, and all main-text four-colour/background assertions. Use `Coloring`, the verified Lean Misra–Gries development, and the Coq Four Colour proof as a mathematical reference only. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_5); [Lean Vizing](https://github.com/aroheebhoja/vizing); [Coq Four Colour](https://rocq-prover.org/p/coq-fourcolor/1.4.0) **Hard points:** Brooks’s theorem was not found; edge-colouring invariants are substantial even with a standalone implementation; maps/surfaces require topology; and a stated-but-unproved Four Colour Theorem still requires a zero-`sorry` Lean proof under the strict contract. If that is infeasible, the correct response is to mark the whole-book goal blocked, not to delete the statement.
6. **Chapter VI, Ramsey Theory.** Formalize original finite/infinite Ramsey theorems, generalized graph Ramsey results, Euclidean Ramsey results, Hales–Jewett, van der Waerden, Rado, and Hindman in book order. Reuse `HalesJewett`, `Hindman`, compactness/ultrafilter infrastructure, and graph colouring. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_6); [`HalesJewett.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/HalesJewett.lean); [`Hindman.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Hindman.lean) **Hard points:** a general original-Ramsey API was not found; Nešetřil–Rödl forbidden-subgraph Ramsey results, Ramsey categories, Galvin–Prikry/Borel-set arguments, and ultrafilter details are independent advanced workstreams.
7. **Chapter VII, Random Graphs.** Formalize probabilistic Ramsey bounds, high-girth/high-chromatic constructions, random-graph evolution and thresholds, clique behaviour, and Hamilton-cycle phenomena. Use `binomialRandom`, independence, moments/variance, sub-Gaussian concentration, and the Isabelle girth–chromatic proof as a mathematical reference. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_7); [`BinomialRandomGraph/Defs.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Probability/Combinatorics/BinomialRandomGraph/Defs.lean); [Isabelle girth–chromatic](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-32347-8_27) **Hard points:** threshold functions and second-moment asymptotics, Poisson approximation, Pósa’s theorem, and zero-one-law statements mentioned in the chapter. The existing binomial-random-graph definition is only the starting point, not a threshold library.
8. **Chapter VIII, Graphs and Groups.** Formalize Cayley/Schreier diagrams, group presentations insofar as they are mathematical definitions, knot/graph connections, adjacency-matrix applications, Moore graphs, and Pólya enumeration. Use `SimpleGraph.Cayley`, `Quiver.Schreier`, `AdjMatrix`, group actions, and finite permutation infrastructure. [Chapter abstract/notes](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7_8); [`Cayley.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraph/Cayley.lean); [`Quiver/Schreier.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Quiver/Schreier.lean) **Hard points:** informal group-presentation/Todd–Coxeter material needs precise algorithmic statements; knot groups introduce topology; Hoffman–Singleton/Moore-graph arguments need spectral analysis; and no dedicated Pólya cycle-index module was found.

### Inferences

- **Wilf sequencing:** M0 source manifest and conventions → M1 Chapters 1–2 algebraic kernel → M2 Chapter 3 exponential families → M3 Chapter 4 applications, with WZ/cycle-index spikes in parallel → M4 Chapter 5 analytic asymptotics. Run the Lagrange/Hayman feasibility spike before M3 so the final chapter cannot become a surprise multi-year blocker.
- **Bollobás sequencing:** M0 representation charter and topological-planarity spike → M1 Chapters I–III core graphs/flows/matching → M2 Chapter IV extremal → M3 Chapters V–VI colouring/Ramsey → M4 Chapter VII random graphs → M5 Chapter VIII groups/Pólya. Start Four Colour, forbidden-subgraph Ramsey, and Pólya as independent risk tracks rather than waiting for their chapter numbers.
- **Completion gates:** every chapter gets a source-ordered list of definitions/results, proof status, dependencies, axiom report, and statement-fidelity review. A theorem present without proof in the source is marked as required new work; no chapter is “done” by proving only theorems already in Mathlib.

### Gaps

- The sketches do not assert that the difficult missing theorems are routine. Hayman asymptotics, topological-planarity equivalence, general max-flow/min-cut, Four Colour, forbidden-subgraph Ramsey, and Pólya enumeration remain explicit unestimated subprojects.
- A manual decision is needed for informal worked examples, historical remarks containing named assertions, and algorithmic prose: the strict target must distinguish actual definitions/theorems from commentary without weakening the stated coverage contract.
- If exercises are later added, both programmes need fresh inventories and effort classes. The current plans are complete only for the supplied main-text notion of complete coverage.

## Method note and confidence summary

### Takeaway

The recommendations rest on live publisher/author TOCs, a pinned Mathlib source audit, and named formalization projects. They deliberately do **not** rely on training-memory claims that graph theory is “mostly missing” from Mathlib or that a famous book is unformalized merely because no familiar project comes to mind.

### Cited Findings

- Bibliographic and TOC claims were checked on 2026-10-21/22 against primary publisher/author sources wherever available: [Wilf](https://www.routledge.com/generatingfunctionology-Third-Edition/Wilf/p/book/9781568812793), [Bollobás](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-9967-7), [_Concrete Mathematics_](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/gkp.html), [Alon–Spencer](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/The+Probabilistic+Method%2C+4th+Edition-p-9781119061953), [Bóna](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/bona/books/), [Diestel](https://diestel-graph-theory.com/), [Bondy–Murty](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-84628-970-5), [Stanley](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/enumerative-combinatorics/360F1EEA6B91AE359EE489AC4145EF49), and [van Lint–Wilson](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780511668777_A23679336/preview-9780511668777_A23679336.pdf).
