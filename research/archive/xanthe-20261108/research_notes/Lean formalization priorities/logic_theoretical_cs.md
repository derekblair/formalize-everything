# Logic and theoretical computer science books for Lean 4 complete coverage

## Which texts have faithful mathematical content rather than being mostly exercises, code, or prose?

### Takeaway

The strongest overall candidate is Herbert Enderton’s *A Mathematical Introduction to Logic*: five coherent chapters, substantial definitions and proofs, and a realistic—though nontrivial—path through existing Lean logic libraries. Glynn Winskel’s *The Formal Semantics of Programming Languages* is second: it is mathematically rich and still lacks whole-book Lean coverage, but its later domain-theory, recursive-type, and concurrency chapters make it substantially harder than the early IMP chapters suggest. Boolos–Burgess–Jeffrey is third; Sipser, TAPL, and Kleinberg–Tardos remain viable, but have larger scope, more substantial adjacent Lean work, or both.

### Cited Findings

**Counting convention used below.** A “node” means a main-text definition, theorem, lemma, proposition, or corollary that would need a faithful Lean declaration under the programme’s “every definition and theorem, in order” rule. Exercises, chapter-end problems, examples, and proof-only helper lemmas are not counted; definitions hidden in prose and substantive algorithm specifications still need an inventory. Ranges are planning estimates calibrated against the programme-supplied Rudin PMA ≈330-node mid-size text, not claimed counts. They are inferred from chapter/section density and the amount of worked examples, code, and exercises in the publisher/author TOCs cited below; a hostile pre-commitment inventory could move them by roughly 20–30%.

#### 1. Enderton — best balance of coherence, size, and downstream logic value

- **Canonical/teaching status.** Elsevier identifies the second edition, author, year, ISBN, and chapter page ranges; a detailed Logic Matters review calls the first edition a “much-used textbook” and analyzes the second edition’s role in mathematics and philosophy teaching. A university logic course explicitly assigns the second edition. — [Elsevier](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780122384523/a-mathematical-introduction-to-logic); [Logic Matters review](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html); [course lecture](https://cvc4.cs.stanford.edu/logic/slides/lec1.pdf)
- **Faithful mathematical content.** Chapter 1 covers unique readability, induction/recursion, truth and expressive completeness, compactness, and effectiveness; Ch. 2 covers first-order syntax/semantics, a Hilbert-style calculus, Henkin completeness, compactness, Löwenheim–Skolem, interpretations, and infinitesimals; Ch. 3 covers quantifier elimination, Robinson/Presburger arithmetic, representability, arithmetization, recursive enumerability, the arithmetic hierarchy, partial recursive functions, register machines, and both incompleteness theorems; Ch. 4 covers full second-order, Henkin/general, and many-sorted semantics. This is not primarily an exercise or code book. — [detailed chapter analysis](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html)
- **Coverage, readiness, value, and risks.** Only partial book-named Lean work was found, while substantial independent Lean libraries already cover completeness and incompleteness. The project’s value is therefore a faithful, ordered, unified account—not claiming these famous results for the first time. Main risks are translating Enderton’s exact Hilbert calculus and semantics without statement drift, the Ch. 3 quantifier-elimination and arithmetic-hierarchy material, and full versus Henkin second-order semantics in Ch. 4. Detailed evidence is in the next two sections. — [partial Chapter 1 project](https://github.com/t4ccer/enderton-lean); [Foundation logic library](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation)

#### 2. Winskel — best semantics candidate, but not an “easy IMP book”

- **Canonical/teaching status.** MIT Press lists the 1993, 384-page book. Cambridge’s 2023/24 Part II Denotational Semantics notes call it an “excellent introduction” and assign Ch. 5, 8, 9, 10(§§1–2), and 11. — [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262731034/the-formal-semantics-of-programming-languages/); [Cambridge notes](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/2324/DenotSem/notes.pdf)
- **TOC and mathematical content.** Ch. 1 basic set theory; 2 operational semantics; 3 induction; 4 inductive definitions; 5 denotational semantics of IMP; 6 axiomatic semantics of IMP; 7 completeness of Hoare rules; 8 domain theory; 9 recursion equations; 10 techniques for recursion; 11 higher types; 12 information systems; 13 recursive types; 14 nondeterminism and parallelism; Appendix A incompleteness and undecidability. The publisher’s description explicitly includes full proofs of operational/denotational equivalence and Hoare soundness/relative completeness. — [full TOC and description](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C)
- **Coverage, readiness, value, and risks.** Existing Lean projects cover early IMP, PCF/denotational semantics, and Scott information systems separately, not the book. The downstream prize is a joined-up semantics/library stack for CSLib, compiler verification, Hoare logic, and concurrency. The main risk is treating Ch. 1–7 as representative: Ch. 12–14 require information-system domain constructions, recursive domain equations, and nondeterministic/parallel semantics, while Ch. 7 and Appendix A add genuine incompleteness machinery. — [early IMP Lean project](https://github.com/juanjomadrigal/denotational-lean); [PCF Lean project](https://github.com/zeyonaut/pcf-lean); [Scott information-systems project](https://github.com/catskillsresearch/domain_theory)

#### 3. Boolos–Burgess–Jeffrey — superb content, broad and somewhat fragmented

- **Canonical/teaching status.** Cambridge calls the book a classic, identifies the revised 2007 fifth edition, and says it is aimed at intermediate logic students; the preface lays out a semester’s core plus supplementary topics. — [Cambridge book page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/computability-and-logic/440B4178B7CBF1C241694233716AB271); [preface and TOC](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/77527/frontmatter/9780521877527_frontmatter.pdf)
- **Full TOC.** Computability: 1 Enumerability, 2 Diagonalization, 3 Turing Computability, 4 Uncomputability, 5 Abacus Computability, 6 Recursive Functions, 7 Recursive Sets and Relations, 8 Equivalent Definitions of Computability. Basic metalogic: 9 FOL Syntax, 10 FOL Semantics, 11 Undecidability of FOL, 12 Models, 13 Existence of Models, 14 Proofs and Completeness, 15 Arithmetization, 16 Representability of Recursive Functions, 17 Indefinability/Undecidability/Incompleteness, 18 Unprovability of Consistency. Further topics: 19 Normal Forms, 20 Craig Interpolation, 21 Monadic and Dyadic Logic, 22 Second-Order Logic, 23 Arithmetical Definability, 24 Decidability of Arithmetic without Multiplication, 25 Nonstandard Models, 26 Ramsey’s Theorem, 27 Modal Logic and Provability. The preface explicitly emphasizes complete proofs. — [publisher TOC and preface](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/77527/frontmatter/9780521877527_frontmatter.pdf)
- **Coverage, readiness, value, and risks.** Lean’s Foundation library already advertises many central items: completeness, both incompleteness theorems, Church, Tarski, Löb, second-order logic, and provability logic. A book project would unify and audit these against BBJ’s exact statements and fill less-covered topics such as monadic/dyadic logic, Herbrand/interpolation, and Ramsey. The risk is scope: all 27 chapters, including “further topics,” count under strict complete coverage; the book cannot be reduced to its incompleteness core. — [Foundation summary](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation); [publisher TOC](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/77527/frontmatter/9780521877527_frontmatter.pdf)

#### 4. Sipser — canonical and valuable, but extensive adjacent Lean coverage lowers novelty

- **Canonical/teaching status.** Sipser’s MIT page identifies the third edition and its upper-undergraduate/introductory-graduate audience; Princeton’s COS 487 used Ch. 1–4 and 7–8 in full plus selected later sections. — [author’s book page](https://math.mit.edu/~sipser/book.html); [Princeton course](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall08/cos487/)
- **Faithful mathematical content.** The book contains formal automata, grammars, machines, reductions, and complexity theorems, including deterministic context-free languages/LR(k), recursion and incompleteness, hierarchy theorems, circuits, probabilistic computation, alternation, and interactive proofs. It has many examples and proof sketches, but its central definitions and named results are genuine formalization targets. — [third-edition description](https://www.perlego.com/book/801823/introduction-to-the-theory-of-computation-pdf); [author’s description](https://math.mit.edu/~sipser/book.html)
- **Coverage, readiness, value, and risks.** ATLAS has a substantial Lean development for MIT’s related *Theory of Computation* course notes, and Complexitylib now advertises many of the book’s headline complexity results. Neither is Sipser-in-order. The remaining value is a faithful automata-to-complexity spine, especially PDAs/deterministic CFGs and exact Sipser statements; risks are machine/encoding translation, duplicated effort, and Ch. 10’s probabilistic, circuit, alternation, and interactive-proof infrastructure. — [ATLAS statistics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/README.md); [Complexitylib](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib)

#### 5. TAPL — real metatheory, but strict coverage goes well beyond the popular core

- **Canonical/teaching status and content.** MIT Press describes a comprehensive, operational introduction to type systems, including untyped lambda calculus, simple types, reconstruction, universal/existential polymorphism, subtyping, bounded quantification, recursive types, kinds, and type operators. The PL community’s book page calls it a seminal/quintessential introduction. — [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262162098/types-and-programming-languages/); [PL community page and TOC](https://www.pls-lab.org/Types_and_Programming_Languages)
- **Full-scope warning.** The mathematical core is substantial, but the 32 chapters also include ML implementations and case studies. A complete project may not silently convert Ch. 21 (metatheory of recursive types), 26 (bounded quantification), 28 (metatheory of bounded quantification), or 29–31 (kinding and higher-order systems) into “prose chapters.” These are precisely the chapters most likely to be omitted by a core-only formalization. — [chapter TOC](https://www.pls-lab.org/Types_and_Programming_Languages)
- **Coverage, readiness, value, and risks.** A 2026 Lean project advertises 19 core chapter directories and no `sorry`, but explicitly omits 13 chapters, including advanced formal content. Coq Software Foundations PLF is substantial but does not cover TAPL’s full recursive/polymorphic/higher-order arc. CSLib now supplies STLC strong normalization and F-sub type safety. The value is reusable metatheory and executable typecheckers; risks are binder/statement fidelity, duplicated active projects, and the difficulty of bounded and higher-order subtyping. — [TAPL-in-Lean scope](https://github.com/cormacflanagan/tapl-lean); [Coq PLF TOC](https://softwarefoundations.cis.upenn.edu/plf-current/toc.html); [CSLib STLC](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/cslib/main/Cslib/Languages/LambdaCalculus/LocallyNameless/Stlc/StrongNorm.lean); [CSLib F-sub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/cslib/main/Cslib/Languages/LambdaCalculus/LocallyNameless/Fsub/Safety.lean)

#### 6. Kleinberg–Tardos — strong algorithmic content, largest serious commitment

- **Canonical/teaching status and content.** Princeton hosts official accompanying slides mapping every chapter to stable matching, asymptotic analysis, graph algorithms, network flow, NP, PSPACE, approximation, local search, and randomized algorithms. The book is proof-oriented, though it also has extensive worked examples and pseudocode. — [official slides](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~wayne/kleinberg-tardos/); [LOC TOC](https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/ecip056/2005000401.html)
- **Coverage, readiness, value, and risks.** No book-specific complete Lean project was found; adjacent work includes selected CLRS algorithms, Complexitylib, and many one-off Isabelle algorithm verifications. Mathlib supplies asymptotics and Akra–Bazzi, while CSLib supplies `TimeM` and elementary algorithms. The downstream value is a verified algorithm-design library, but strict coverage requires both mathematical correctness and faithful cost models for every algorithm, plus max-flow, approximation, local-search, and randomized infrastructure. — [CLRS-Lean scope](https://tanktechnology.github.io/CLRS-Lean/); [Mathlib Akra–Bazzi](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Computability/AkraBazzi/AkraBazzi.lean); [CSLib `TimeM`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/cslib/main/Cslib/Algorithms/Lean/TimeM.lean)

### Inferences

- **Enderton is the best first project** because its five-chapter dependency chain is coherent and its hard parts are identifiable before starting; the existence of independent completeness/incompleteness libraries reduces proof risk but increases the need for a statement-faithfulness audit rather than a novelty claim. — [Enderton TOC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780122384523/a-mathematical-introduction-to-logic); [Foundation](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation)
- **Winskel is the best second project and the best non-overlapping companion to a logic programme.** Its early chapters offer a low-risk pilot, but a go decision must include a costed Ch. 12–14 plan, not merely a successful IMP demo. — [Winskel TOC](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C)
- **BBJ belongs ahead of Sipser for this strict programme** because its main challenge is broad logical coverage, whereas Sipser also requires reconciling several substantial, differently designed Lean machine/complexity ecosystems. — [BBJ TOC](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/77527/frontmatter/9780521877527_frontmatter.pdf); [Complexitylib](https://github.com/cslib-community/complexitylib)
- **TAPL and Kleinberg–Tardos are not rejected**, but they should not be sold as medium projects. TAPL’s advanced chapters and KT’s algorithm/cost inventory are integral to complete coverage. — [TAPL TOC](https://www.pls-lab.org/Types_and_Programming_Languages); [KT TOC](https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/ecip056/2005000401.html)

### Gaps

- **Node ranges are estimates, not inventories.** No full main-text definition/theorem census was performed. The ranges for TAPL and KT are the least certain because implementation chapters, pseudocode, worked examples, and case studies blur the boundary between a main-text node and exposition. Publisher TOCs anchor the estimates; they do not prove the counts. — [TAPL TOC](https://www.pls-lab.org/Types_and_Programming_Languages); [KT TOC](https://catdir.loc.gov/catdir/toc/ecip056/2005000401.html)
- **Effort classes are comparative judgments, not time-and-motion measurements.** They assume faithful statements, zero `sorry`, standard axioms only, and hostile review. They do not assume that a compiling third-party repository has already passed that standard. — [ATLAS discussion of faithfulness and hidden-axiom failure modes](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1)

## Which canonical books are already comprehensively mechanized elsewhere, and is a Lean version genuinely missing?

### Takeaway

No complete or near-complete **whole-book Lean 4** formalization of any of the six shortlisted titles was found, so none is disqualified on that ground. The important qualifications are that TAPL has a large explicit core-only Lean project, Sipser has substantial adjacent ATLAS/Complexitylib coverage, and Enderton/BBJ share many headline results with Foundation. Coq Software Foundations is not a complete TAPL mechanization; *Concrete Semantics* is comprehensively mechanized in Isabelle and is therefore a lower-priority Lean port, though a Lean opening remains explicit.

## Does current Mathlib/Lean infrastructure support the prerequisites?

### Takeaway

Yes for Enderton and, with explicit external dependencies, for a staged Winskel project; “Mathlib alone” is not the right readiness test for either. Enderton can combine Mathlib model theory/computability with Foundation, while Winskel can combine Mathlib’s ω-CPOs with CSLib, PCF, and Scott-information-systems work. BBJ and Sipser are feasible but need substantial book-specific gap filling; TAPL and KT should be treated as infrastructure-building programmes, not routine Mathlib exercises.

#### Chapter-by-chapter sketch: Enderton (top candidate)

The implementation should keep a source ledger with every numbered and prose definition/result, preserve the book’s statement order, and use library results only after checking their exact hypotheses and semantics.

1. **Ch. 0 — Useful Facts about Sets.** Build the small source-specific vocabulary for sets, relations, functions, sequences, cardinality, and induction using Mathlib’s set/function/cardinal APIs. This is the low-risk ledger/pilot chapter; the main danger is overengineering a new set-theory framework instead of reusing Mathlib. — [publisher scope](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780122384523/a-mathematical-introduction-to-logic)
2. **Ch. 1 — Sentential Logic.** Define well-formed formulas inductively; prove unique readability and the recursion theorem; define truth assignments, satisfaction, semantic consequence, and tautological equivalence; prove expressive adequacy, switching-circuit results, compactness, and effectiveness statements. Use Foundation only where its propositional semantics matches; Enderton’s direct compactness and lack of a formal proof calculus in this chapter must not be “modernized” into a different theorem sequence. — [chapter analysis](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html); [Foundation propositional/completeness summary](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation)
3. **Ch. 2 — First-Order Logic.** Reuse or adapt Mathlib’s `Language`, terms, formulas, structures, and realization; implement free variables/substitution and Enderton’s Hilbert calculus; prove soundness; build the Henkin model and completeness; derive compactness/Löwenheim–Skolem and interpretations; finish with the nonstandard-analysis application. **Hard point:** Foundation’s completeness is LK-based, so either prove a faithful Hilbert↔LK equivalence or reuse only semantics-independent consequences while retaining the book’s proof order. — [Mathlib semantics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Semantics.lean); [Mathlib compactness/LS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Satisfiability.lean); [Foundation LK](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation/master/Foundation/FirstOrder/LK/Completeness.lean)
4. **Ch. 3 — Undecidability.** Formalize successor and successor-plus-order theories, Presburger arithmetic and quantifier elimination; Robinson arithmetic with exponentiation; definability/representability and sequence coding; arithmetization; first/second incompleteness and undecidability; recursive enumerability and the arithmetic hierarchy; partial recursive functions and register machines; applications to set theory and the β-function route. **Hard points:** the quantifier-elimination chain (Mathlib explicitly leaves Presburger theory/QE as TODO), exact representability hypotheses, and register-machine↔partial-recursive equivalence. Foundation and Mathlib supply many landmarks but not the whole ordered chapter. — [detailed Ch. 3 analysis](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html); [Presburger TODO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Arithmetic/Presburger/Basic.lean); [Mathlib halting/Rice](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Computability/Halting.lean); [Foundation incompleteness](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation/master/Foundation/FirstOrder/Incompleteness/Second.lean)
5. **Ch. 4 — Second-Order Logic.** Define second-order languages and full semantics, Skolem functions, many-sorted logic, and Henkin/general structures; prove the book’s comparisons and non-completeness/compactness failures faithfully. Foundation’s `SecondOrder` syntax/semantics/derivations is a starting point. **Hard point:** do not confuse full second-order semantics with Henkin semantics or accidentally inherit first-order completeness/compactness. — [Enderton Ch. 4 analysis](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html); [Foundation second-order summary](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation)

#### Chapter-by-chapter sketch: Winskel (second candidate)

1. **Ch. 1 — Basic set theory.** Reuse Mathlib for sets, relations, functions, and order prerequisites; record source-specific notation without introducing a rival foundations stack. — [Winskel TOC](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C)
2. **Ch. 2 — Introduction to operational semantics.** Define IMP arithmetic/boolean expressions, states, commands, evaluation and big-step execution; prove determinism and command equivalence. Lean inductive relations and CSLib semantics are directly relevant; `denotational-lean` is a partial reference, not coverage. — [partial Lean IMP](https://github.com/juanjomadrigal/denotational-lean); [CSLib semantics](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/tree/main/Cslib/Foundations/Semantics)
3. **Ch. 3 — Some principles of induction.** Formalize structural, rule, course-of-values, and well-founded induction and their equivalences. This is mostly library adaptation, but the exact derivation relations must match Ch. 2 and Ch. 4. — [Mathlib relation/well-founded infrastructure](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/master/Mathlib/Logic/Relation.lean)
4. **Ch. 4 — Inductive definitions.** Define rule sets, derivations, least closed sets/relations, and rule induction; connect to fixed-point formulations needed for later denotational semantics. **Hard point:** keep “least inductively defined relation” and denotational fixed points definitionally separate even when they coincide extensionally. — [Winskel TOC](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C); [Mathlib fixed-point/ω-CPO base](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Order/OmegaCompletePartialOrder.lean)
5. **Ch. 5 — Denotational semantics of IMP.** Interpret expressions and commands using states, partial functions/relations, and the least fixed point for `while`; prove operational–denotational equivalence. `Part` and ω-CPO instances are ready, but the book’s exact denotation convention must govern the development. — [Mathlib `Part`/ω-CPO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Order/OmegaCompletePartialOrder.lean)
6. **Ch. 6 — Axiomatic semantics of IMP.** Define Hoare triples and proof rules; prove soundness and consequences for partial correctness. Coq PLF/Concrete Semantics can guide proof engineering but are not the source statement inventory. — [Coq PLF Hoare chapters](https://softwarefoundations.cis.upenn.edu/plf-current/toc.html); [Concrete Semantics](https://concrete-semantics.org/)
7. **Ch. 7 — Completeness of the Hoare rules.** Formalize weakest preconditions, expressiveness of arithmetic assertions, and relative completeness; coordinate the later incompleteness appendix without circular source dependencies. **Hard point:** arithmetic expressiveness and the precise meaning of “relative” completeness, not routine Hoare-rule soundness. — [publisher description](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C); [Foundation arithmetic/incompleteness](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation)
8. **Ch. 8 — Introduction to domain theory.** Define CPOs/domains, continuous functions, products/function spaces, and Kleene fixed points using `OmegaCompletePartialOrder`, `ContinuousHom`, and `fixedPoints`. Add flat domains and Scott-induction wrappers. This is the first substantial reusable infrastructure milestone. — [Mathlib ω-CPO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Order/OmegaCompletePartialOrder.lean); [Cambridge domain-theory notes](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/2324/DenotSem/notes.pdf)
9. **Ch. 9 — Recursion equations.** Define recursion-equation syntax and call-by-value/call-by-name operational semantics; give denotations and prove correspondence/adequacy. `pcf-lean` is adjacent evidence of feasibility, but the book’s language and theorem order differ. — [PCF Lean contents](https://github.com/zeyonaut/pcf-lean); [Winskel TOC](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C)
10. **Ch. 10 — Techniques for recursion.** Formalize admissible/chain-closed predicates, Scott induction, fixed-point induction, and proof methods for recursive programs. Mathlib’s subtype ω-CPO construction and fixed-point inequalities are useful; the hard work is turning them into the book’s exact induction principles. — [Mathlib subtype/fixed-point results](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Order/OmegaCompletePartialOrder.lean)
11. **Ch. 11 — Languages with higher types.** Treat typed functional languages and eager/lazy lambda calculi, their operational/denotational semantics, approximation, and correspondence results. Reuse PCF approximation/soundness ideas and CSLib STLC metatheory; **hard point:** supporting both evaluation regimes and their exact semantic domains rather than only modern PCF. — [PCF approximation/soundness](https://github.com/zeyonaut/pcf-lean); [CSLib STLC](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/cslib/main/Cslib/Languages/LambdaCalculus/LocallyNameless/Stlc/StrongNorm.lean)
12. **Ch. 12 — Information systems.** Define tokens, consistency, entailment, ideals, and the induced domains; construct product/sum/function spaces and prove their universal/fixed-point properties. Audit the separate Scott 1982 work before reuse; its archived monolith says source transcription verification is unfinished. **This is a go/no-go infrastructure milestone, not an optional appendix.** — [Scott information-systems status](https://github.com/catskillsresearch/domain_theory); [Winskel TOC](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C)
13. **Ch. 13 — Recursive types.** Solve recursive domain equations using information systems; define recursive types and their eager/lazy operational and denotational semantics; prove the correspondence results. **Hardest chapter:** no complete Lean development was found, and it depends on Ch. 12’s exact constructions rather than generic ω-CPO fixed points. — [Winskel TOC/description](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C)
14. **Ch. 14 — Nondeterminism and parallelism.** Model transition systems, nondeterministic and parallel programs, and the book’s specification/verification rules. CSLib’s LTS, simulation, bisimulation, divergence, and execution modules are relevant, but fairness and the exact parallel language remain book-specific hard points. — [CSLib LTS](https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/tree/main/Cslib/Foundations/Semantics/LTS); [Winskel TOC](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Formal_Semantics_of_Programming_Lang.html?id=JzUNn6uUxm0C)
15. **Appendix A — Incompleteness and undecidability.** Define while-program computability, arithmetic representability, and the incompleteness result needed for the semantics discussion; bridge while programs to Mathlib computability and Foundation arithmetic. **Hard point:** proving the machine equivalence and preserving the appendix’s exact semantic assumptions rather than importing a superficially similar incompleteness theorem. — [Mathlib computability](https://api.github.com/repos/leanprover-community/mathlib4/contents/Mathlib/Computability); [Foundation incompleteness](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation/master/Foundation/FirstOrder/Incompleteness/Second.lean)

### Inferences

- **Recommended pilot order:** Enderton Ch. 0–1, then the Ch. 2 Hilbert/Henkin chain; in parallel, a *feasibility-only* Winskel Ch. 8 and Ch. 12 prototype. A successful Winskel Ch. 2 IMP demo is not enough evidence to approve the whole book. — [Enderton analysis](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html); [Mathlib ω-CPO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Order/OmegaCompletePartialOrder.lean); [Scott systems status](https://github.com/catskillsresearch/domain_theory)
- **BBJ is the safest top-3 fallback if Winskel’s Ch. 12 prototype fails.** Its broad further topics are costly, but Foundation already covers a larger share of its landmark results than exists for Winskel’s recursive-type/concurrency arc. — [Foundation](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation); [BBJ TOC](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/77527/frontmatter/9780521877527_frontmatter.pdf)
- **Reuse should be theorem-level and statement-led, not repository-level adoption.** Pin Mathlib, Foundation, CSLib, and Complexitylib commits; write a mapping for every source definition; reject any “equivalent-looking” theorem whose machine model, nonempty-structure convention, numerals, binder representation, or complexity cost model changes the claim. — [Mathlib semantics conventions](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Semantics.lean); [Complexitylib v1 conventions](https://github.com/SamuelSchlesinger/complexitylib-v1); [`TimeM` caveat](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover/cslib/main/Cslib/Algorithms/Lean/TimeM.lean)
