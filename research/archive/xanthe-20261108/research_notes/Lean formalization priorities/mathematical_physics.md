# Mathematical physics books for Lean 4 complete coverage

**Evidence convention.** Bibliographic, contents, project-status, and Mathlib statements below are web-verified. Publisher/project pages were accessed 2026-10-21; catalog scripts and rechecks ran 2026-10-22. Recommendations, node ranges, effort classes, and roadmaps are explicitly marked as estimates/inferences. “Not found” always means “not located in the documented searches,” never a proof of worldwide nonexistence. The node convention is main-text definitions plus numbered or unnumbered mathematical results (theorems, propositions, lemmas, corollaries), excluding exercises, worked examples, bibliography, and index; an inventory that also counts exercise-theorems will need an upward revision. PMA ≈330 nodes is used only as the requested size calibration, not as a candidate.

## Which texts are formalizable mathematics rather than requiring unspecified physical axioms or huge missing fields?

### Takeaway

The strongest overall candidate is **Brian C. Hall, _Quantum Theory for Mathematicians_**: a rigorous, teachable, self-contained-ish 23-chapter text with a measurable 400–550-node scope and very high downstream value, provided the programme first budgets for full spectral theory and geometric quantization. **Arnold’s _Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics_** is the strongest classical-mechanics candidate by canonical value, but its 16 appendices and external proof dependencies make it a multi-year infrastructure programme, not a routine transcription. **Friedli–Velenik** is the best pedagogical statistical-mechanics alternative; **Sachs–Wu** is the best relativity-sized target; **Takhtajan** and **Ruelle** remain valuable but substantially harder openings.

All six are provisional openings: no complete or near-complete whole-book Lean formalization was located, and no complete whole-book non-Lean formalization was located either. The negative evidence is not exhaustive; confidence per book is stated below. Physical laws should be represented as explicit mathematical structures or hypotheses, not as new global Lean axioms. Kernel-checking a mathematical model is not kernel-checking nature.

### Cited Findings

#### Ranked candidate 1 — Brian C. Hall, *Quantum Theory for Mathematicians* (first edition, 2013)

- **Bibliographic identity and status.** Springer GTM 267, xvi+554 pp., DOI `10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5`, hardcover ISBN `978-1-4614-7115-8`, first edition published 19 June 2013. Springer describes a mathematician-facing treatment including bounded and unbounded spectral theorems, Stone–von Neumann, WKB, Lie theory, path integrals, and geometric quantization; it also advertises self-contained mathematics and graduate-course exercises. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5) Edinburgh’s MATH10107 reading list assigns selected chapters, giving independent teaching evidence. [Edinburgh course catalogue](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/25-26/dpt/cxmath10107.htm) **Assessment:** canonical modern graduate text (high confidence, inferred from the publisher, series, reviews, and course adoption).
- **TOC scope.** The 23 chapters are: 1 Experimental Origins; 2 First Approach to Classical Mechanics; 3 First Approach to Quantum Mechanics; 4 Free Schrödinger; 5 Square Well; 6 Perspectives on the Spectral Theorem; 7 Bounded Spectral Theorem—Statements; 8 Bounded Spectral Theorem—Proofs; 9 Unbounded Self-Adjoint Operators; 10 Unbounded Spectral Theorem; 11 Harmonic Oscillator; 12 Uncertainty Principle; 13 Quantization Schemes; 14 Stone–von Neumann; 15 WKB; 16 Lie Groups/Algebras/Representations; 17 Angular Momentum and Spin; 18 Radial Potentials and Hydrogen; 19 Systems/Subsystems and Multiple Particles; 20 Path Integrals; 21 Hamiltonian Mechanics on Manifolds; 22 Geometric Quantization on `R^{2n}`; 23 Geometric Quantization on Manifolds. [Springer book/TOC](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5), [Ch. 20](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5_20), [Ch. 21](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5_21), [Ch. 23](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5_23)
- **Node range and effort (estimate).** A `pdftotext -layout` pass over the publicly accessible PDF, followed by anchored matching of `Definition|Theorem|Proposition|Lemma|Corollary chapter.number`, found **401 unique numbered labels** (449 occurrences including cross-references). OCR misses and unnumbered definitions mean 401 is a lower-bound inventory, not a claim of exact coverage. A defensible planning range is **400–550 nodes = 1.2–1.7 PMA**. [Hall PDF used for the inventory](https://revistamaya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Hall-2013.pdf) **Effort class C:** prerequisite-heavy, multi-stage and plausibly multi-year at the requested hostile-review standard; the node count understates Ch. 7–10 and Ch. 20–23.
- **Lean status.** **No complete or near-complete whole-book Lean formalization located (unverified negative; medium confidence).** PhysLib instead contains selected reusable modules: partial-map unbounded operators and `SpectralMeasure`, quantum harmonic oscillator, hydrogen, uncertainty, and related material; its own `Unbounded.lean` still has TODOs and does not claim Hall’s spectral theorem sequence. [PhysLib unbounded operators](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/physlib/master/Physlib/QuantumMechanics/Operators/Unbounded.lean), [PhysLib spectral measures](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/physlib/master/Physlib/QuantumMechanics/Operators/SpectralTheory/SpectralMeasure.lean), [PhysLib quantum tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/tree/master/Physlib/QuantumMechanics) AxQM is about Nielsen–Chuang, not Hall. [AxQM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05157v1)
- **Other proof assistants.** **No complete or near-complete non-Lean whole-book formalization located (unverified negative; medium confidence).** Coq QuantumLib covers matrices, measurement, discrete probability and quantum-program reasoning; Isabelle Marries Dirac covers finite-dimensional quantum circuits/algorithms. Neither is Hall or a general unbounded-operator textbook. [Coq QuantumLib](https://github.com/inQWIRE/QuantumLib), [Isabelle Marries Dirac](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Isabelle_Marries_Dirac.html)
- **Mathlib readiness.** Useful starting points are `inner_mul_inner_self_le` and the inner-product API, `ContinuousLinearMap.adjoint`, finite-dimensional `LinearMap.IsSymmetric.diagonalization/eigenvectorBasis`, compact self-adjoint spectral results, `Measure.infinitePi`, Lie groups and `GroupLieAlgebra`, `FDRep`, and `IsBrownianReal`. [Inner products](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Basic.html), [adjoints](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Adjoint.html), [spectrum](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.html), [infinite products](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/ProductMeasure.html), [Brownian motion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/BrownianMotion/Basic.html), [Lie groups](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Algebra/LieGroup.html), [FDRep](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/FDRep.html) Crucially, the Mathlib spectrum file itself still says **“TODO: Spectral theory for bounded self-adjoint operators,”** and its unbounded coverage is not Hall’s full sequence. [Mathlib spectrum TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Spectrum.html)
- **Biggest gaps.** Full bounded and unbounded spectral theorems and functional calculi; Stone and Stone–von Neumann; Kato–Rellich; Trotter and Feynman–Kac with an actual Wiener measure on path space; infinite-dimensional tensor/trace machinery; WKB asymptotics; symplectic/manifold differential forms; line bundles, polarizations and geometric quantization. These are inferred directly from Hall’s TOC against the cited Mathlib/PhysLib coverage. Hall Ch. 20 explicitly makes the rigorous object Wiener measure and Feynman–Kac rather than a purely formal Feynman integral. [Hall Ch. 20](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4614-7116-5_20)
- **Downstream value.** A book-faithful development would become infrastructure for operator theory, Schrödinger PDE, quantum information, representation theory, mathematical physics and geometric quantization. This is an inference from the chapter sequence and from PhysLib’s independent reuse of similar components. [PhysLib repository](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib)
- **Risks.** Domain-sensitive unbounded statements; accidental strengthening/weakening of physical postulates; treating Brownian motion as already equivalent to Feynman–Kac; and late-book external dependencies. In particular, Hall’s Ch. 23 text invokes Newlander–Nirenberg in the polarization discussion, so a zero-`sorry`, standard-axioms-only build cannot merely postulate it. [Hall PDF, Ch. 23](https://revistamaya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Hall-2013.pdf) Hostile statement review is essential even where Lean code already exists; AxQM’s discussion gives concrete examples of formally compiling but vacuous physics statements. [AxQM §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05157v1)

#### Ranked candidate 2 — V. I. Arnold, *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics* (second edition, 1989)

- **Bibliographic identity and status.** Springer GTM 60, second edition, translated by K. Vogtmann and A. Weinstein, xvi+520 pp., DOI `10.1007/978-1-4757-2063-1`, hardcover ISBN `978-0-387-96890-2`. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-2063-1) Arnold’s preface says the book grew from a year-and-a-half required course for third- and fourth-year mathematics students at Moscow State; UCLouvain currently recommends it to students preferring mathematical rigour. [Author/publisher PDF](https://www.math.toronto.edu/khesin/biblio/arnold_Math_Methods89.pdf), [UCLouvain LMAT1261](https://uclouvain.be/en-cours-2023-lmat1261) **Assessment:** canonical classical-mechanics text (very high confidence).
- **TOC scope.** Ten main chapters: 1 Experimental Facts; 2 Investigation of Equations of Motion; 3 Variational Principles; 4 Lagrangian Mechanics on Manifolds; 5 Oscillations; 6 Rigid Bodies; 7 Differential Forms; 8 Symplectic Manifolds; 9 Canonical Formalism; 10 Introduction to Perturbation Theory; followed by **16 appendices** on curvature, ideal fluids, algebraic symplectic structures, contact structures, symmetries, normal forms, KAM, Poincaré’s geometric theorem, frequencies/ellipsoids, short-wave asymptotics, Lagrangian singularities, KdV, Poisson structures, elliptic coordinates, and ray systems. [Full TOC in the author/publisher PDF](https://www.math.toronto.edu/khesin/biblio/arnold_Math_Methods89.pdf)
- **Node range and effort (estimate).** **400–650 nodes = 1.2–2.0 PMA**, obtained by scaling the 10-chapter/52-section main text and separately allowing for the 16 dense appendices; no reliable machine inventory was obtained because available scans are image/OCR-poor. **Effort class C+:** multi-year, several missing subfields. The appendices cannot silently be dropped from a strict complete-coverage target.
- **Lean status.** **No complete or near-complete whole-book Lean formalization located (unverified negative; medium confidence).** PhysLib has selected Euler–Lagrange, Hamiltonian, rigid-body, oscillator, scattering and Poisson-bracket modules, not an Arnold sequence. Its `poissonBracket` file explicitly leaves canonical commutation relations and the Jacobi identity as TODOs. [PhysLib classical tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/tree/master/Physlib/ClassicalMechanics), [Poisson bracket](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/physlib/master/Physlib/ClassicalMechanics/HamiltonianMechanics/PoissonBracket.lean) Prove2Me’s completed “Ordinary Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems VII: Attracting Sets and Liouville’s Theorem” targets Teschl and merely lists Arnold as a selected reference; it is not Arnold book coverage. [Public mission catalog, page 3](https://beta.prove2.me/?status=all&page=3)
- **Other proof assistants.** **No complete or near-complete non-Lean whole-book formalization located (unverified negative; medium confidence).** A 2022 Edinburgh project, *Mechanizing Lagrangian Mechanics in Isabelle/HOL*, formalizes a Lagrangian locale, five example systems, conservation laws and Noether’s theorem, explicitly inspired chiefly by Sussman–Wisdom rather than covering Arnold. [Project report](https://project-archive.inf.ed.ac.uk/ug4/20223079/ug4_proj.pdf)
- **Mathlib readiness.** `IsPicardLindelof` and local flows, `ContMDiff`, Lie groups/`GroupLieAlgebra`, Riemannian bundles and `CovariantDerivative.leviCivitaConnection` cover real prerequisites. [Picard–Lindelöf](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/ODE/PicardLindelof.html), [manifolds](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/ContMDiff/Basic.html), [Lie algebra of a Lie group](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/GroupLieAlgebra.html), [Levi–Civita](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/LeviCivita.html) But `extDeriv` is currently for normed spaces and its TODO explicitly says manifold forms are not defined yet. [Differential forms](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/DifferentialForm/Basic.html)
- **Biggest gaps.** Calculus of variations in useful function spaces; manifold differential forms and Stokes; symplectic/Poisson manifolds and Darboux; global Hamiltonian flows; Floquet/parametric resonance; Hamilton–Jacobi; action-angle variables and KAM; contact geometry; singularity/caustic theory; ideal-fluid geometry and KdV. These are inferred from the TOC against the cited modules. The current Mathlib tree has linear-algebra `SymplecticGroup` but no corresponding symplectic-manifold path; this path-level observation is not a declaration-level nonexistence proof. [Mathlib tree API](https://api.github.com/repos/leanprover-community/mathlib4/git/trees/master?recursive=1)
- **Downstream value.** Shared infrastructure for symplectic/contact geometry, ODE and dynamical systems, celestial mechanics, integrable systems, calculus of variations and geometric PDE. The range of downstream fields is also explicit in Arnold’s preface. [Arnold preface](https://www.math.toronto.edu/khesin/biblio/arnold_Math_Methods89.pdf)
- **Risks.** Arnold states that the main chapters try to give explicit proofs while the appendices largely summarize results whose proofs are in cited literature. Therefore strict completion requires completing those external proof gaps under standard axioms, not declaring the results as axioms. [Arnold preface](https://www.math.toronto.edu/khesin/biblio/arnold_Math_Methods89.pdf) Ch. 1’s experimental claims must be converted into explicit Galilean/Newtonian model structures, not proved as empirical facts. **Ranking caveat:** if prerequisite readiness matters more than historical canonicity, move Friedli–Velenik above Arnold.

#### Ranked candidate 3 — Sacha Friedli and Yvan Velenik, *Statistical Mechanics of Lattice Systems: A Concrete Mathematical Introduction* (first edition, 2017)

- **Bibliographic identity and status.** Cambridge University Press, 2017, ISBN `978-1-107-18482-4`, DOI `10.1017/9781316882603`; the authors’ freely accessible revised PDF is dated 22 August 2017. [Author bibliography](https://www.unige.ch/~velenik/papers/smbook_bib.html), [author PDF](https://www.unige.ch/~velenik/smbook/Statistical_Mechanics_of_Lattice_Systems.pdf) The authors explicitly aim at a rigorous mathematical-physics introduction, say early drafts were used in lectures, and Velenik describes the result as student-friendly; EPFL also lists it in a course bibliography. [Author homepage](https://www.unige.ch/~velenik/), [EPFL course booklet](https://www.epfl.ch/schools/ic/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/SSC_LC-2020-2021.pdf) **Assessment:** a modern rigorous teaching text, not as historically canonical as Ruelle; chosen for formalizability and pedagogy.
- **TOC scope.** Ten chapters: 1 Introduction; 2 Curie–Weiss; 3 Ising; 4 Liquid–Vapor Equilibrium; 5 Cluster Expansion; 6 Infinite-Volume Gibbs Measures; 7 Pirogov–Sinai; 8 Gaussian Free Field on `Z^d`; 9 Models with Continuous Symmetry; 10 Reflection Positivity; then notes and extensive mathematical appendices on real/convex/complex analysis, metric spaces, measure/probability, Gaussian fields, entropy, random walks and lattice inequalities, plus exercise solutions. [Full author TOC](https://www.unige.ch/~velenik/smbook/Statistical_Mechanics_of_Lattice_Systems.pdf)
- **Node range and effort (estimate).** **350–600 nodes = 1.1–1.8 PMA**, allowing for the 10 result-dense chapters and mathematical appendices while discounting prose and solutions. **Effort class B/C:** a large single-book project, becoming multi-year if Pirogov–Sinai and reflection positivity infrastructure must be built from scratch. No exact census was obtained.
- **Lean status.** **No complete or near-complete whole-book Lean formalization located (unverified negative; medium-low confidence).** PhysLib’s statistical-mechanics core currently contains only `CanonicalEnsemble`, `MicroCanonicalEnsemble`, and constants, with finite-system results such as the finite fluctuation–dissipation theorem—not Friedli–Velenik’s 10-chapter sequence. [PhysLib statistical tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/tree/master/Physlib/StatisticalMechanics), [finite canonical ensemble](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/physlib/master/Physlib/StatisticalMechanics/CanonicalEnsemble/Finite.lean) ATLAS has a separate large `StatisticalMechanics` development whose root lists measure foundations, FKG/Holley, GKS, FK, percolation and Ising phases; it is not labelled as a Friedli–Velenik or Ruelle book project, so it must be treated as selected-topic infrastructure, not whole-book evidence. [ATLAS statistical root](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/Atlas/StatisticalMechanics/Code.lean)
- **Other proof assistants.** **No complete or near-complete non-Lean whole-book formalization located (unverified negative; low-medium confidence).** Searches found no book-linked Coq, Isabelle/HOL, HOL or Agda development; this is the least certain of the candidate negatives and should be rechecked before adoption.
- **Mathlib readiness.** Particularly useful are `MeasureTheory.ProbabilityMeasure`, `Measure.prod` and Tonelli, `Measure.infinitePi`, `iIndepFun`, Gaussian processes, and binary entropy. [Probability measures](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/ProbabilityMeasure.html), [product measures](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/MeasureTheory/Measure/Prod.html), [infinite products](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/ProductMeasure.html), [independence](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Independence/Basic.html), [Gaussian process paths in the live tree](https://api.github.com/repos/leanprover-community/mathlib4/git/trees/master?recursive=1), [binary entropy](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/BinaryEntropy.html)
- **Biggest gaps.** DLR specifications and conditional distributions; thermodynamic limits and pressure; extremal decompositions and variational principles; cluster/polymer convergence; Pirogov–Sinai contours; general GKS/FKG interfaces (despite selected ATLAS results); continuous-spin Gibbs theory; Mermin–Wagner; reflection positivity/chessboard and infrared bounds; random-walk potential theory. The need is visible from Ch. 3–10 and the appendices. [Author TOC](https://www.unige.ch/~velenik/smbook/Statistical_Mechanics_of_Lattice_Systems.pdf)
- **Downstream value.** Gibbs measures, percolation, phase transitions, probabilistic combinatorics, random walks, ergodic theory and rigorous equilibrium statistical mechanics. ATLAS’s independent statistical-mechanics codebase is direct evidence of reuse potential, although its statements still need faithfulness and axiom audits before adoption. [ATLAS statistical root](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/Atlas/StatisticalMechanics/Code.lean)
- **Risks.** Ch. 1 explicitly says it is less rigorous and motivational than the rest of the book, so the inventory must distinguish formal postulates/results from prose; the Gibbs distribution can be a model hypothesis, not a theorem about nature. [Author introduction](https://www.unige.ch/~velenik/smbook/Statistical_Mechanics_of_Lattice_Systems.pdf) Ch. 7 and Ch. 10 are deep infrastructure bottlenecks, and current ATLAS overlap makes a declaration-by-declaration duplication check mandatory.

#### Ranked candidate 4 — Rainer K. Sachs and Hung-Hsi Wu, *General Relativity for Mathematicians* (first edition, 1977)

- **Bibliographic identity and status.** Springer GTM 48, xii+292 pp., DOI `10.1007/978-1-4612-9903-5`, first edition. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-9903-5) A contemporary AMS review says the authors develop the subject with mathematical precision and that the exercises make it suitable as a text, potentially after a first graduate differential-geometry course; it also notes that the book does not attempt the deeper singularity/black-hole theorems. [J. A. Thorpe review](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/bulletin-of-the-american-mathematical-society/volume-84/issue-6/Review--R-K-Sachs-and-H-Wu-General-relativity/bams/1183541472.pdf)
- **TOC scope.** Ch. 0 Preliminaries; 1 Spacetimes; 2 Observers; 3 Electromagnetism and Matter; 4 Einstein Field Equation; 5 Photons; 6 Cosmology; 7 Further Applications (stationary spacetimes, Schwarzschild, solar system, black holes, gravitational plane waves); 8 Optional Exercises—Relativity; 9 Optional Exercises—Newtonian Analogues. [Full scanned TOC](https://lps.elte.hu/gomori/spacetime-geometry/readings/(Graduate%20Texts%20in%20Mathematics%2048)%20Dr.%20Rainer%20K.%20Sachs,%20Dr.%20Hung-Hsi%20Wu%20(auth.)%20-%20General%20Relativity%20for%20Mathematicians-Springer-Verlag%20New%20York%20(1977).pdf)
- **Node range and effort (estimate).** **250–450 nodes = 0.8–1.4 PMA**, based on 10 short chapters with many explicit definitions/propositions and optional exercise-results. **Effort class C:** multi-year because of the missing semi-Riemannian and global Lorentzian layer, not because of page count.
- **Lean status.** **No complete or near-complete whole-book Lean formalization located (unverified negative; medium-low confidence).** PhysLib covers Minkowski tensors, Lorentz groups, special relativity and causality, not Sachs–Wu’s full curved-spacetime sequence. [PhysLib relativity tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/tree/master/Physlib/Relativity), [Minkowski causality](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/physlib/master/Physlib/Relativity/Causality/Basic.lean) A separate 30-chapter *Mathematical Physics in Lean 4* project includes GR but openly reports **365 axioms**, including analytic scaffolding and physical field equations; it is neither Sachs–Wu coverage nor acceptable under this programme’s standard-axioms-only rule. [Project honesty note](https://github.com/TataSatyaPratheek/mathematical-physics-lean/blob/main/README.md)
- **Other proof assistants.** **No complete or near-complete non-Lean whole-book formalization located (unverified negative; medium-low confidence).** *A Formalization of Elements of Special Relativity in Coq* explicitly calls itself a preliminary portion of special relativity, with selected axioms, causality and no-relative-motion-at-`c`; it is not Sachs–Wu or general relativity. [Harvard/Coq report](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4160f812-a67d-42c4-9261-c159ade7cd86/content)
- **Mathlib readiness.** Smooth manifolds, Riemannian bundles and the new Levi–Civita construction are real starting points; `IsRiemannianManifold` and `CovariantDerivative.IsLeviCivitaConnection` are the specific declarations. [Riemannian manifolds](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Riemannian/Basic.html), [Levi–Civita](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/VectorBundle/CovariantDerivative/LeviCivita.html) These do not supply a Lorentzian causal theory, and manifold differential forms remain a stated TODO. [Differential forms TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Analysis/Calculus/DifferentialForm/Basic.html)
- **Biggest gaps.** Semi-Riemannian/Lorentzian metrics and index; time orientation and causality; covariant derivative/curvature/geodesics at book generality; stress-energy and Maxwell equations; Einstein’s equation; photon gases and lightcone integration; cosmological models; Schwarzschild geometry; global black-hole and wave calculations. This follows from Ch. 0–7 against the cited modules.
- **Downstream value.** Semi-Riemannian geometry, general relativity, relativistic cosmology, black holes, geometric analysis and field equations. The AMS review explicitly positions the book as preparation for deeper physics literature. [Thorpe review](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/bulletin-of-the-american-mathematical-society/volume-84/issue-6/Review--R-K-Sachs-and-H-Wu-General-relativity/bams/1183541472.pdf)
- **Risks.** The authors explicitly reject “proving” general relativity or axiomatizing nature and say central physical postulates are disguised as definitions; a faithful project must preserve that model/theorem distinction. [Sachs–Wu preface and guidelines](https://lps.elte.hu/gomori/spacetime-geometry/readings/(Graduate%20Texts%20in%20Mathematics%2048)%20Dr.%20Rainer%20K.%20Sachs,%20Dr.%20Hung-Hsi%20Wu%20(auth.)%20-%20General%20Relativity%20for%20Mathematicians-Springer-Verlag%20New%20York%20(1977).pdf) Optional exercise-results need a scope decision and separate inventory. The lack of deep singularity theorems makes this substantially more attractive than Wald, not easy.

#### Ranked candidate 5 — Leon A. Takhtajan, *Quantum Mechanics for Mathematicians* (first edition, 2008)

- **Bibliographic identity and status.** AMS Graduate Studies in Mathematics 95, xv+387 pp., hardcover ISBN `978-0-8218-4630-8`. AMS calls it a comprehensive, precise mathematical treatment for second-year graduate students and prints reviews emphasizing definitions, theorems and proofs. [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/view?ProductCode=GSM/95) Columbia’s mathematics course lists it among more mathematical references. [Columbia W4392](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~woit/QM/spring2013.html)
- **TOC scope.** Part I: 1 Classical Mechanics (Lagrangian/Hamiltonian, Noether, symplectic/Poisson); 2 Basic Principles (observables/states/dynamics, quantization, Weyl relations, Stone–von Neumann, deformation); 3 Schrödinger Equation (self-adjointness, Jost/scattering, angular momentum, hydrogen/SO(4), semiclassics); 4 Spin and Identical Particles (SU(2), Pauli, Young diagrams, Schur–Weyl). Part II: 5 Path Integrals (propagators, Gaussian integrals, regularized determinants); 6 Integration in Functional Spaces (Gaussian/Wiener, Feynman–Kac); 7 Fermion Systems (CAR, Clifford, Grassmann, Berezin, graded algebra); 8 Supersymmetry (supermanifolds, localization, supermechanics, Atiyah–Singer). [AMS TOC](https://bookstore.ams.org/view?ProductCode=GSM/95), [detailed ETH TOC](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf/e01_978-0-8218-4630-8_01.pdf)
- **Node range and effort (estimate).** **300–550 nodes = 0.9–1.7 PMA**, with a large uncertainty because the eight chapters are much denser than their page count suggests. **Effort class C+:** multi-year; Part II effectively adds path integration, supergeometry and index theory programmes.
- **Lean status.** **No complete or near-complete whole-book Lean formalization located (unverified negative; medium confidence).** PhysLib and AxQM cover selected quantum topics, not Takhtajan’s sequence; the non-finite-dimensional and supersymmetry parts are especially absent from the located coverage. [PhysLib quantum tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/physlib/tree/master/Physlib/QuantumMechanics), [AxQM scope](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05157v1)
- **Other proof assistants.** **No complete or near-complete non-Lean whole-book formalization located (unverified negative; medium confidence).** Coq QuantumLib and Isabelle Marries Dirac are finite-dimensional quantum-computation libraries, not this book. [QuantumLib](https://github.com/inQWIRE/QuantumLib), [Isabelle Marries Dirac](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Isabelle_Marries_Dirac.html)
- **Mathlib readiness.** Hall’s operator-analysis prerequisites plus `CliffordAlgebra`/`CliffordAlgebra.lift`, `FDRep` and Schur’s lemma, Brownian motion, and infinite probability products provide fragments. [Clifford algebras](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/CliffordAlgebra/Basic.html), [FDRep](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/FDRep.html), [Brownian motion](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/BrownianMotion/Basic.html), [infinite products](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/ProductMeasure.html)
- **Biggest gaps.** Everything hard for Hall plus Jost functions and scattering; Schur–Weyl/Young-diagram infrastructure; regularized determinants; Berezin integration and Grassmann variables; supermanifolds and graded geometry; equivariant localization; and the Atiyah–Singer index formula. The last four chapters make the book substantially harder than Hall despite fewer pages. [Detailed TOC](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf/e01_978-0-8218-4630-8_01.pdf)
- **Downstream value.** Quantum field theory, functional integration, supersymmetry, index theory, integrable systems and representation theory, exactly the bridge advertised by AMS. [AMS](https://bookstore.ams.org/view?ProductCode=GSM/95)
- **Risks.** Treating path-integral notation as a definition without constructing the relevant measure; using finite-dimensional quantum libraries as if they covered unbounded operators; and needing an entire index-theory stack for Ch. 8. This is a poor first quantum book for the programme despite being an excellent long-term target.

#### Ranked candidate 6 — David Ruelle, *Statistical Mechanics: Rigorous Results* (World Scientific reprint, 1999; original 1969)

- **Bibliographic identity and status.** World Scientific 1999 reprint, ISBN `9810238622` / `9789810238629`; Google Books reports 219 pages and identifies the edition as a reprint. [Google Books/publisher description](https://books.google.com/books/about/Statistical_Mechanics.html?id=mNtWfcj_xhwC) **Teaching status:** canonical research monograph rather than the friendliest course text; Friedli–Velenik is the better teaching-first choice.

[Google Books TOC](https://books.google.com/books/about/Statistical_Mechanics.html?id=mNtWfcj_xhwC) The exact subtitles of Ch. 2 and Ch. 3 were not visible and are deliberately not guessed.

### Inferences

#### Recommended order

1. **Hall first**, but as two explicitly staged dependency programmes: Ch. 1–6 and 11–19 can proceed while bounded/unbounded spectral infrastructure is built for Ch. 7–10; Ch. 20–23 should be separate late milestones. Do not declare the entire book “in progress” without a public axiom-and-statement audit for these late chapters.
2. **Arnold second for canonical value; Friedli–Velenik second for readiness.** Arnold is the better intellectual target, Friedli–Velenik the better bounded first statistical-mechanics target. Choosing both simultaneously would duplicate FKG/GKS/Ising effort; audit ATLAS first.
3. **Sachs–Wu before Wald**, and only after a reusable semi-Riemannian/Lorentzian minimum viable library exists.
4. **Takhtajan after Hall**, reusing Hall’s spectral, path-integral and representation stack. **Ruelle after Friedli–Velenik**, reusing DLR, thermodynamic-limit and convex-state infrastructure.

### Inferences

- The most valuable genuinely new kernel-checking work is not another isolated finite-dimensional quantum algorithm: it is **shared infinite-dimensional operator theory**, **manifold symplectic geometry**, **DLR/thermodynamic-limit infrastructure**, and **semi-Riemannian/Lorentzian geometry**. These recur across multiple candidates and would unlock entire chapter sequences.
- Existing selected-topic projects are assets, but each reused theorem needs provenance, exact hypotheses, `#print axioms`, and a book-source mapping. The 365-axiom GR project and ATLAS’s own warning about hidden axioms/weakened statements show why “builds without `sorry`” is insufficient for this programme. [GR honesty note](https://github.com/TataSatyaPratheek/mathematical-physics-lean/blob/main/README.md), [ATLAS evaluation criteria](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1)

### Gaps

- No search can establish that a canonical theorem has never been kernel-checked in any proof assistant. All “not located at book-faithful generality” bullets above remain unverified negatives, with **medium-low worldwide confidence** even where whole-book confidence is higher.
- Prove2Me was not exhaustively crawled, private missions are invisible by design, and GitHub repository search does not inspect all source text. Recheck these before final top-3 selection.
- HOL Light and Agda coverage was checked only through web searches, not through a complete local clone of every library; their negative status is especially provisional.

## Which prerequisites does current Mathlib already have, and which are prohibitive?

### Takeaway

Mathlib is strong on undergraduate/early-graduate analysis, measure/probability, finite-dimensional spectral algebra, ODE, smooth manifolds, Lie groups, Clifford algebras and—at the current live docs—Riemannian/Levi–Civita foundations. It is not yet a drop-in library for full unbounded spectral theory, manifold symplectic geometry, DLR statistical mechanics, or Lorentzian GR. PhysLib fills real gaps but is fragmented and itself contains TODOs. The prohibitive items are not raw node counts but a few deep dependencies: full spectral theory, KAM, Feynman–Kac, Pirogov–Sinai, Atiyah–Singer, and global Lorentzian/GR geometry.

#### Candidate-by-candidate feasibility verdict

- **Hall:** mathematically formalizable with **no unspecified physical axioms** if states/models are explicit; prerequisites are difficult but modular. The full spectral and geometric-quantization walls are real, not a reason to reject outright. **Best overall.**
- **Arnold:** main chapters are formalizable mathematics; empirical content is explicitly separated into models in the text. The appendices’ cited-not-proved results and many missing geometries make complete coverage **prohibitive as a first project but legitimate as a long-term one**. [Arnold preface](https://www.math.toronto.edu/khesin/biblio/arnold_Math_Methods89.pdf)
- **Friedli–Velenik:** rigorous mathematics dominates after the motivational introduction; probability infrastructure is comparatively good. Advanced phase-transition chapters are the limiting factor. **Best statistical-mechanics entry.**
- **Sachs–Wu:** definitions/results are precise, but the authors deliberately keep physical postulates distinct from mathematical deduction. Formalization is possible only as verification of mathematical models, not of nature. Lorentzian prerequisites are the main barrier. [Sachs–Wu guidelines](https://lps.elte.hu/gomori/spacetime-geometry/readings/(Graduate%20Texts%20in%20Mathematics%2048)%20Dr.%20Rainer%20K.%20Sachs,%20Dr.%20Hung-Hsi%20Wu%20(auth.)%20-%20General%20Relativity%20for%20Mathematicians-Springer-Verlag%20New%20York%20(1977).pdf)
- **Takhtajan:** mathematically rigorous, but Part II requires several fields far beyond current Mathlib. **Long-term only.**
- **Ruelle:** rigorous and concise, with no need to formalize physical intuition as axioms; infinite-system/operator-state foundations are the limiting factor. **Long-term second statistical text.**
- **Wald:** also rigorous, unlike Arfken’s primarily computational-methods genre, but its complete scope is currently prohibitive. Rigor alone does not make a book a good complete-coverage candidate. [Wald TOC](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/G/bo5952261.html), [Arfken TOC](https://faculty.fiu.edu/~fiebig/sylb5115.pdf)

### Inferences

#### Minimum prerequisite programmes before final top-3 commitment

1. **Operator analysis:** bounded/unbounded spectral theorem, projection-valued measures, functional calculus, Stone, completed tensor products and trace class. This unlocks Hall and Takhtajan and is reusable outside physics.
2. **Manifold mechanics:** manifold differential forms/integration, symplectic/Poisson structures, global Hamiltonian flows and variational calculus. This unlocks Arnold and Hall Ch. 21–23.
3. **Gibbs/probability:** kernels/specifications, infinite-volume limits, convex/extremal states and correlation inequalities. This unlocks Friedli–Velenik and Ruelle; audit ATLAS before writing new code.
4. **Semi-Riemannian geometry:** indefinite metrics/index, Levi–Civita in that setting, causality, geodesics and curvature. Only then schedule Sachs–Wu; Wald needs much more.
5. **Explicit trust gate for every chapter:** source-to-Lean node map, faithful statement review, zero `sorry`, `#print axioms` limited to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`, no new `axiom` declarations for physical or mathematical results, and no hidden assumptions in typeclass fields. AxQM’s grading names those three permitted axioms, while its paper also illustrates why kernel acceptance alone does not ensure faithful physics statements. [AxQM repository](https://github.com/Axiomatic-AI/AxQM), [AxQM §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05157v1)

### Gaps

- The readiness assessment is declaration- and documentation-based, not a clean build of a proposed book stack. Current Mathlib is moving quickly; exact names and coverage should be pinned to a revision before implementation.
- No exhaustive declaration-level audit of PhysLibAlpha or the rapidly changing ATLAS `StatisticalMechanics` tree was performed. Their existence lowers some gaps only after axiom, faithfulness and provenance checks.
- The largest unresolved scoping question is whether externally cited theorems must be formalized as dependencies even when the book omits their proofs. Under the user’s zero-`sorry`, standard-axioms-only, faithful-statement target, the safe planning assumption is **yes**; this is why Arnold and Hall Ch. 23 receive multi-year classes.
