# Three canonical books should anchor Lean

## 1. Executive summary

**Recommended next tranche, in priority order:**

1. **M. F. Atiyah and I. G. Macdonald, _Introduction to Commutative Algebra_ (1969)** — The best scope/readiness ratio: 11 chapters, an estimated 180–240 nodes, unusually strong Mathlib support, and one clearly identified schedule spike in dimension theory.
2. **Herbert S. Wilf, _generatingfunctionology_ (3rd ed., 2005)** — A compact five-chapter project with high reuse across combinatorics, probability, and number theory; the power-series base is strong, while the exponential formula and analytic asymptotics are meaningful new work.
3. **Herbert B. Enderton, _A Mathematical Introduction to Logic_ (2nd ed., 2001)** — A coherent route from sentential logic through incompleteness and second-order semantics, with major landmarks already available in Mathlib and Foundation but no complete book-ordered Lean development found.

These are **gated recommendations**, not claims of easy transcription: Atiyah–Macdonald requires a Chapter 11 pilot, Wilf an early Lagrange-inversion/Hayman spike, and Enderton a Hilbert/Henkin plus Presburger pilot before a whole-book commitment.

## 2. Scope and acceptance contract

**Edition policy.** Each project must freeze one named edition, one source file or printing, and one dated errata list before making its node ledger. Reprints are not silently treated as new mathematical editions; author-corrected online texts must record a revision date. Later corrections may change a formal statement only through an explicit source-erratum entry, never through silent “repair.” Wilf’s second edition is useful as a cross-check but is not mixed into the third-edition inventory; Hatcher’s online correction and renumbering notice illustrates why this rule matters ([Hatcher’s edition notice](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html)).

**Coverage policy.** The baseline includes every definition, theorem, lemma, proposition, and corollary in the main text, numbered or unnumbered, in source order, plus all mathematical appendices. A worked example counts when it introduces a definition or proves a mathematical result; routine illustrative calculations do not create extra nodes. Chapter-end exercises and their solutions are excluded from the estimates and baseline contract, but that exclusion must be stated in the public manifest. Software manuals and solution appendices are excluded unless they contain mathematical definitions or results not present elsewhere. Starred sections, survey chapters, and statements whose proofs are merely cited remain in scope: the project must supply a proof under the same trust standard rather than deleting the statement or adding an axiom.

**Node and effort meaning.** A node is a source-level definition or result as above, not a Lean declaration, helper lemma, line of code, or exercise. Rudin PMA’s approximately 330 main-text nodes are only a calibration. Ranges below are analyst estimates, often anchored by mechanical label counts; they are not hand-audited censuses. “Compact single-book” means a coherent project with bounded dependency breadth, not a promise of completion within a year; “large single-book” permits several staged workstreams; “multi-year” means several field-scale prerequisites or research-scale endpoints. No reliable person-month measurements exist for these exact strict-coverage projects.

**Acceptance policy.** “Done” requires a source-node ledger, every in-scope node in order, zero `sorry`/`admit`, faithful statements, no custom axioms, transitive axiom reports limited to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`, and independent hostile review of statements and scope. A green build or a repository’s “complete” label alone is insufficient. ATLAS’s selected-target model and Prove2Me’s mission model, for example, do not certify whole-book coverage ([ATLAS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29955v1); [Prove2Me paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28433v2)).

**Absence claims and confidence.** “No complete or near-complete formalization found” always means not found in the dated, documented searches behind this report—not worldwide nonexistence. **High** denotes direct primary-source evidence for a positive fact; **medium-high** denotes a bounded negative supported by exact-title/alias searches, special-purpose registries, and inspection of relevant partial projects; **medium** denotes a useful but less exhaustive negative, often with generic-title or access limitations; **medium-low/low** denotes sparse or rate-limited evidence. Node counts, effort classes, rankings, implementation order, and hard-point diagnoses are analyst inferences, not live-web facts. No shortlisted recommendation relies on a claimed complete non-Lean book; substantial non-Lean work identified below is partial and does not discharge the Lean opening.

## 3. Ranked shortlist

The ranking is **next-project priority under the strict contract**, balancing missing Lean coverage, current readiness, bounded scope, teaching value, and downstream reuse. It is not a ranking of mathematical importance.

### 1. Atiyah–Macdonald, _Introduction to Commutative Algebra_

**Text, field, and scope.** M. F. Atiyah and I. G. Macdonald, **first edition, Addison-Wesley, 1969**; commutative algebra. Eleven chapters from Rings and Ideals through Dimension Theory, estimated **180–240 nodes; compact single-book project**. The book grew from third-year Oxford lectures, and the 2016 Westview issue is a reprint rather than the source edition chosen here ([Macdonald bibliography](https://ig-macdonald.github.io/); [text and TOC](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/atiyahmacdonald.pdf)). **Existing coverage:** no complete or near-complete ordered Lean book was found; the exact GitHub repository query returned zero on 2026-10-21, while Mathlib’s Lasker module explicitly covers selected Theorems 4.5 and 4.10 rather than the book ([query](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=%22Atiyah%20Macdonald%22%20lean); [Lasker](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.html)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium** for Lean, **low–medium** for non-Lean. **Readiness:** primary decomposition, going-up, Krull height/polynomial dimension, Hopkins–Levitzki, and exactness of adic completion are present ([going-up](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/GoingUp.html); [Krull height](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/KrullsHeightTheorem.html); [adic exactness](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Exactness.html)). **Biggest gap:** the graded-module Hilbert-polynomial layer and local-dimension equivalences; `Polynomial.HilbertPoly` has an explicit TODO ([HilbertPoly](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/HilbertPoly.html)). **Case:** a concise, trusted teaching text whose ordered development would audit the API connecting localization, integrality, Dedekind domains, completion, and dimension for algebraic geometry and number theory. **Risks:** library/source representation mismatches and Chapter 11 becoming a separate prerequisite project.

### 2. Wilf, _generatingfunctionology_

**Text, field, and scope.** Herbert S. Wilf, **third edition, A K Peters/CRC, 2005**; enumerative combinatorics and analytic combinatorics. Five chapters—introductory ideas, series, the exponential formula, applications, and analytic/asymptotic methods—plus a Maple/Mathematica appendix; **110–220 nodes; compact single-book project**. The range allows for unnumbered items after a third-edition scan yielded 67 numbered labels; it is not a precise census ([publisher](https://www.routledge.com/generatingfunctionology-Third-Edition/Wilf/p/book/9781568812793); [third-edition TOC](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html)). **Existing coverage:** no complete or near-complete Wilf book was found in the documented search; the relevant Lean repository found was only two files/277 lines with three `sorry`s; a UW talk covers examples, not the book. No named ATLAS/ReasBook/Prove2Me book project was found ([Julian repository](https://github.com/Julian/generatingfunctionology.lean); [UW talk](https://math.washington.edu/events/2024-05-20/generating-functions-lean)). Isabelle work presents selected proofs from Wilf, not five-chapter coverage ([Chaieb record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10817-010-9195-9)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium-high** for Lean, **medium-low** for non-Lean. **Readiness:** substantial `PowerSeries`/`MvPowerSeries`, partitions, Catalan, pentagonal, Stirling, and asymptotics APIs, plus adjacent Grinberg infrastructure ([PowerSeries](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries); [partition generating functions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Partition/GenFun.lean); [Grinberg](https://github.com/facebookresearch/algebraic-combinatorics)). **Biggest gaps:** exponential families/species, formal Dirichlet series, WZ/cycle-index tools, Lagrange inversion, and Hayman/singularity asymptotics; path scans are provisional, not semantic absence proofs ([pinned tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1)). **Case:** class-tested and explicitly presented as a bridge between discrete and continuous mathematics, with reusable enumeration infrastructure rather than another elementary-analysis companion ([author-hosted second-edition preface](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/gfology2.pdf)). **Risks:** Chapter 5, WZ and Pólya side spikes, formal-versus-analytic equality, and edition/appendix drift.

### 3. Enderton, _A Mathematical Introduction to Logic_

**Text, field, and scope.** Herbert B. Enderton, **second edition, Academic Press/Elsevier, 2001**; mathematical logic and computability. Chapters 0–4 cover sets, sentential logic, first-order logic, undecidability, and second-order logic; **230–330 nodes; single-book, medium-high difficulty** ([publisher TOC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780122384523/a-mathematical-introduction-to-logic); [detailed Logic Matters review](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html)). **Existing coverage:** `t4ccer/enderton-lean` and `jrpotter/bookshelf` contain Chapter 1 material only; Foundation is a broad independent library, not Enderton in order. Isabelle’s incompleteness entry is theorem-level, not book coverage ([partial Lean](https://github.com/t4ccer/enderton-lean); [bookshelf](https://github.com/jrpotter/bookshelf); [Foundation](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation); [Isabelle incompleteness](https://www.isa-afp.org/entries/Incompleteness.html)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium-high** for Lean, **medium** for other assistants. **Readiness:** Mathlib supplies first-order semantics, compactness/Löwenheim–Skolem, recursive functions, halting and reducibility; Foundation adds LK completeness and both incompleteness landmarks ([Mathlib semantics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Semantics.lean); [satisfiability](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Satisfiability.lean); [Foundation second incompleteness](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation/master/Foundation/FirstOrder/Incompleteness/Second.lean)). **Biggest gaps:** a faithful Hilbert↔LK bridge, Presburger/quantifier elimination, arithmetic hierarchy and register-machine equivalences, and full versus Henkin/many-sorted second-order semantics; Presburger theory and QE are explicit TODOs ([Presburger](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Arithmetic/Presburger/Basic.lean)). **Case:** a much-used teaching text and an assigned course book whose completion would provide a unified, statement-audited metalogic spine rather than claiming Gödel landmarks anew ([Stanford course](https://cvc4.cs.stanford.edu/logic/slides/lec1.pdf)). **Risks:** calculus/semantics drift, duplication with Foundation, and Chapter 3’s true depth.

### 4. Milnor, _Topology from the Differentiable Viewpoint_

**Text, field, and scope.** John W. Milnor, **revised edition, Princeton University Press, 1997**, based on notes by David W. Weaver; differential topology. Sections 1–8 plus the one-manifold appendix; **80–130 nodes; compact single-book with several hard packages** ([publisher](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691048338/topology-from-the-differentiable-viewpoint); [revised TOC](https://www.gbv.de/dms/goettingen/233916806.pdf)). **Existing coverage:** no Milnor-ordered complete/near-complete Lean book was found; ATLAS `MathlibExt` supplies selected Sard/transversality/Morse modules, and `SardMoreira` explicitly leaves corollaries unfinished. Isabelle Smooth Manifolds is also selected infrastructure ([ATLAS Sard](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/MathlibExt/Geometry/Manifold/Sard.lean); [SardMoreira](https://github.com/urkud/SardMoreira); [AFP](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Smooth_Manifolds.html)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium** for Lean, **medium-low** for non-Lean. **Readiness:** charted manifolds, derivatives, tangent bundles, partitions of unity, compact Whitney embedding, integral curves, and an initial bordism definition exist ([manifold tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/d88814353fcbcc0461c55b2a4585d3f44d903df1/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold); [Whitney](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/WhitneyEmbedding.html)). **Biggest gaps:** full Sard integration, mod-2/oriented degree, Euler-number/Poincaré–Hopf, framed cobordism/Pontryagin, and Hopf; bordism itself is explicitly future work ([Bordism TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Geometry/Manifold/Bordism.html)). **Case:** the smallest canonical route to reusable regular-value, degree, and cobordism infrastructure, with current preliminary-exam teaching evidence ([UTK syllabus](https://math.utk.edu/graduate/preliminary-examinations/topology/)). **Risks:** concrete embedded definitions versus Mathlib’s model-with-corners formulation, orientation signs, and a deceptively difficult appendix/§7.

### 5. Serre, _A Course in Arithmetic_

**Text, field, and scope.** Jean-Pierre Serre, **English first edition, GTM 7, Springer, 1973**; number theory. Seven chapters on finite fields, p-adic fields, Hilbert symbols, quadratic forms, integral quadratic forms, arithmetic progressions, and modular forms; **150–210 nodes; single-book, high variance** ([publisher and TOC](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4684-9884-4)). **Existing coverage:** `SerreNumberTheoryBlueprint` is an active partial project whose formalization tree contains Chapter 1 with four files, not a near-complete seven-chapter book ([repository](https://github.com/are4c4/SerreNumberTheoryBlueprint); [Chapter 1 tree](https://github.com/are4c4/SerreNumberTheoryBlueprint/tree/main/SerreNumberTheory/Formalization/Chapter01)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium-high** for Lean, **low–medium** for non-Lean. **Readiness:** Chevalley–Warning, Dirichlet’s theorem and L-functions, level-one dimension formulas, Sturm bounds, and the E4/E6 graded-ring result are present ([Chevalley–Warning](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/FieldTheory/ChevalleyWarning.html); [primes in progressions](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/PrimesInAP.html); [graded ring](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/ModularForms/LevelOne/GradedRing.html)). **Biggest gaps:** Hilbert-symbol API, p=2 computations, Hasse–Minkowski, and integral lattices; filename searches found no obvious named modules but are not declaration-level proof of absence. The axiomatized LeanGenius Hasse–Minkowski artifact is not usable under this contract ([LeanGenius counterexample](https://leangenius.org/proof/hilbert-11)). **Case:** a short, lecture-derived route joining local–global quadratic forms to Dirichlet and modular forms; Chapters 3–5 would add genuinely valuable infrastructure. **Risks:** coordination with the active project, integral-versus-rational statement weakening, and a difficult central trio of chapters despite strong support at both ends.

### 6. Riehl, _Category Theory in Context_

**Text, field, and scope.** Emily Riehl, **first edition, 2016; use the author-hosted PDF and pin its revision**; category theory. Six chapters on categories, universal properties, limits, adjunctions, monads, and Kan extensions, plus an epilogue on coherence, toposes, and abelian-category embeddings; **250–400 nodes; large single-book to multi-year**, depending on the epilogue and domain examples ([author PDF and TOC](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf)). **Existing coverage:** three relevant Lean repositories are respectively Chapter 1 §§1–4, exercises, and selected Chapters 1–4 with intentional `sorry`s; Lean Pool’s factorization-systems result is likewise selected. Leinster’s completed companion overlaps conceptually but is a different book ([rkirov](https://github.com/rkirov/category-theory-in-context-lean); [masak](https://github.com/masak/category-theory-in-context); [gaxiiiiiiiiiiii/Riehl](https://github.com/gaxiiiiiiiiiiii/Riehl); [Lean Pool index](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25199v1); [Leinster](https://github.com/whoiam1101/basic-category-theory-lean4)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium-high** for Lean; non-Lean searches were not exhaustive. **Readiness:** Mathlib has monads, several monadicity theorems, left/right Kan extensions, pointwise modules, and coherence infrastructure ([monads](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Monad/Basic.html); [monadicity](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Monad/Monadicity.html); [Kan extensions](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/CategoryTheory/Functor/KanExtension/Basic.html)). **Biggest gaps:** exact monadicity variants, pointwise formulas, derived functors, universe/size control, and epilogue topics. **Case:** high downstream reuse across algebra, topology, geometry, logic, and computer science, with new value concentrated in source-specific examples and Chapters 5–6/epilogue. **Risks:** paying for a second category-theory companion where Leinster overlaps, and allowing examples from other fields to become unbudgeted prerequisites.

### 7. Williams, _Probability with Martingales_

**Text, field, and scope.** David Williams, **Cambridge University Press, 1991; the 2012 digital issue is not a new mathematical edition**; probability and discrete-time martingales. Chapter 0, Chapters 1–18, and mathematical appendices; **300–450 nodes; large single-book project** ([CUP](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/probability-with-martingales/B4CFCE0D08930FB46C6E93E775503926); [full TOC](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf)). **Existing coverage:** no ordered complete/near-complete book was found; Mathlib contains many individual landmarks, and Keskin’s Isabelle thesis covers martingale foundations in Banach spaces rather than Williams ([Isabelle thesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.06188); [martingale convergence](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/Convergence.html)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium** for Lean and non-Lean. **Readiness:** conditional expectation, optional stopping, generalized Borel–Cantelli, strong law, CLT, Lévy convergence, and Prokhorov are present ([conditional expectation](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/ConditionalExpectation.html); [optional stopping](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/OptionalStopping.html); [strong law](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/StrongLaw.html); [CLT](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/CentralLimitTheorem.html)). **Biggest gaps:** martingale transforms, three-series, Doob decomposition/angle brackets, Lévy downward, Skorokhod representation, Kakutani products, regular-conditional-probability bridges, and the LIL appendices; ATLAS’s failed/`sorryAx` targets show these are not all disposable prerequisites ([ATLAS report](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/main/v1/Atlas/TheoryOfProbability/report.json)). **Case:** essentially Cambridge third-year lecture notes, still assigned in courses, and a reusable bridge to Markov chains, stochastic calculus, finance, and limit theory ([Williams preface](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781139632263_A24435166/preview-9781139632263_A24435166.pdf); [Stanford STAT310A](https://adembo.su.domains/stat-310a/)). **Risks:** versions and almost-everywhere equivalence, application chapters with separate models, and a large isolated A4 LIL milestone.

### 8. Norris, _Markov Chains_

**Text, field, and scope.** J. R. Norris, **Cambridge University Press, 1997; first paperback 1998**; probability and stochastic processes. Six chapters covering discrete chains, two continuous-time parts, martingales/potential theory/networks, applications, and a measure appendix; **160–230 nodes; large single-book with schedule risk** ([CUP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/markov-chains/A3F966B10633A32C8F06F37158031739/listing); [full text/TOC](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf)). **Existing coverage:** no Norris-ordered complete/near-complete book was found. Isabelle AFP’s Markov Models and Hölzl’s jump-and-hold construction are substantial non-Lean partial coverage, not a Lean version or the book; Prove2Me’s completed Levin–Peres–Wilmer collection is a different source ([AFP Markov Models](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Markov_Models.html); [Hölzl paper](https://www.cs.vu.nl/~jhl890/pub/hoelzl2017markovprocesses.pdf); [Prove2Me collection](https://prove2.me/collections/markov-chains-and-mixing-times)). **Whole-book negative:** no complete or near-complete book was found in the documented search; **confidence: medium**. **Readiness:** finite stochastic matrices, kernel irreducibility, trajectory kernels, hitting times, and exponential distributions exist ([stochastic matrices](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Stochastic.html); [trajectory kernels](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Kernel/IonescuTulcea/Traj.html); [hitting times](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Probability/Process/HittingTime.html)). **Biggest gaps:** countable-state chain semantics, strong Markov/recurrence/invariant measures, Q-matrices and minimal/non-minimal CTMCs, explosion, potential/electrical-network theory, queues, MDPs, and MCMC. The external MarkovProcess library explicitly excludes jump processes ([limitations](https://github.com/scottnarmstrong/MarkovProcess)). **Case:** a repeatedly class-tested Cambridge text and current course reference whose completion would connect chains to queueing, decision processes, and sampling ([Norris preface](https://cape.fcfm.buap.mx/jdzf/cursos/procesos/libros/norris.pdf); [Illinois Math 564](http://psdey.web.illinois.edu/Math564FA24.html)). **Risks:** its small node count hides several new libraries, and finite-matrix shortcuts would betray countable-state statements.

### 9. Bollobás, _Graph Theory: An Introductory Course_

### 10. Hatcher, _Algebraic Topology_

**Text, field, and scope.** Allen Hatcher, **Cambridge University Press, 2002; use the author’s corrected online edition with a recorded revision**; algebraic topology. Chapters 0–4, all additional topics, and the appendix; **400–550 nodes; large single-book to multi-year**. The core-only estimate would be smaller, but Hatcher says additional topics constitute nearly half the book, so dropping them is not complete coverage ([author page](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html); [chapter list](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATchapters.html); [author PDF/preface](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf)). **Existing coverage:** Prove2Me has selected Brouwer, van Kampen, lifting, and deck-group results; ATLAS’s `AlgebraicTopologyI` is a different MIT source with 110/171 selected targets; Lean Pool has selected Hatcher-derived theorems. ## 4. Top 3 in depth

These three win on **overall balance rather than domain quotas**. Atiyah–Macdonald has the strongest readiness evidence; Wilf has the cleanest compact, high-reuse scope; Enderton has a coherent dependency chain with substantial independent logic infrastructure. The plans below cover every chapter of the chosen editions. Out-of-order pilots are risk controls only: the delivered ledger and final presentation must still follow the book.

### 4.1 Atiyah–Macdonald: pilot dimension theory, then assemble in order

**Implementation order.** M0: freeze the 1969 text, create the 11-chapter source ledger, and write representation charters for localization, completion, and Krull dimension. Run the Chapter 11 Hilbert–Samuel/local-dimension pilot immediately. M1: Chapters 1–3. M2: Chapters 4–7. M3: Chapters 8–10. M4: finish Chapter 11 and reconcile every pilot theorem with the source. M5: independent statement, scope, build, and transitive-axiom review. If the Chapter 11 pilot fails, the correct outcome is “not yet feasible as a complete book,” not a relabelled Chapters 1–10 success. Chapter identities and section scope follow the [source text](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/atiyahmacdonald.pdf).

1. **Rings and Ideals.** Formalize ring homomorphisms, ideals and quotients, prime/maximal ideals, nilradical and Jacobson radical, ideal arithmetic, extension/contraction, and Chinese remainder using the existing `Ideal`, `RingHom`, quotient, and radical APIs. **Hard points:** preserve the book’s zero-ring convention; distinguish ideal extension from the image of a set; avoid a parallel elementary API whose simplification rules diverge from Mathlib.
2. **Modules.** Cover submodules and quotients, finite generation, exact sequences, tensor products, restriction/extension of scalars, algebras, and tensor exactness through `Module`, `Submodule`, `LinearMap`, and `TensorProduct`. **Hard points:** universe management, equality of bundled maps, and faithful right-exactness statements. The chapter should produce bridges, not duplicate basic module theory.
3. **Rings and Modules of Fractions.** Use `IsLocalization`, `Localization`, `IsFractionRing`, and module-localization APIs for fractions, local properties, and extended/contracted ideals. **Hard points:** transport between explicit fractions and arbitrary localizations; keep denominator choices and extension/contraction equalities definitionally manageable. This is the first major statement-fidelity review point because the library is deliberately more general than the book.
4. **Primary Decomposition.** Map primary ideals/submodules, associated primes, minimal decompositions, and both uniqueness theorems to `Submodule.IsPrimary` and `IsLasker`. Mathlib already contains Lasker–Noether decomposition and the two Atiyah–Macdonald-cited uniqueness results ([Lasker](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Lasker.html)). **Hard points:** colon submodules versus ideal quotients, radical/annihilator correspondence, irredundant indexing, and proving that library “minimal” is the book’s notion rather than merely sharing a name.
5. **Integral Dependence and Valuations.** Cover integral elements and closures, integrally closed domains, going-up/down, normalization, and valuation rings. Use the existing integrality and valuation APIs, including [going-up](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/GoingUp.html). **Hard points:** exact going-down hypotheses, normalization in field extensions, and choice-heavy existence statements. Do not strengthen hypotheses to match a convenient library theorem.
6. **Chain Conditions.** Reuse `IsNoetherian`, `IsArtinian`, finite-length, and composition-series machinery for the chapter’s ring/module equivalences. **Hard points:** align ring-versus-module typeclasses and avoid duplicate definitions with different reducibility or simplification behavior. This should be mostly reconciliation, but every source formulation still needs its own ledger entry.
7. **Noetherian Rings.** Assemble Hilbert basis, Noetherian-module results, primary decomposition, and the chapter’s Nullstellensatz consequences. **Hard points:** identify the exact library equivalent for each source formulation and preserve the book’s proof dependencies where practical. A collection of similarly named endpoint theorems is not chapter coverage; the source-specific intermediate statements matter.
8. **Artin Rings.** Use `IsArtinianRing`, Hopkins–Levitzki, and the Noetherian/Krull-dimension-zero equivalence in [HopkinsLevitzki](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/HopkinsLevitzki.html), then prove the decomposition into local Artinian factors. **Hard points:** keep commutative and noncommutative formulations separate and make product/decomposition statements faithful. The main risk is false confidence from having the headline equivalence while missing the chapter’s structural detail.
9. **Discrete Valuation Rings and Dedekind Domains.** Cover equivalent DVR characterizations, Dedekind domains, fractional ideals, factorization, and adic valuations using `IsDiscreteValuationRing`, `IsDedekindDomain`, and `FractionalIdeal`. **Hard points:** valuation normalization, conversions between ordinary and fractional ideals, and equivalences among definitions. This chapter is a key downstream API audit for algebraic number theory.
10. **Completions.** Formalize topologies from filtrations, graded modules and associated graded rings, Artin–Rees, adic completion, and exactness. Reuse `AdicCompletion.map_exact` for finite modules over Noetherian rings ([AdicCompletion.Exactness](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/AdicCompletion/Exactness.html)). **Hard points:** reconcile algebraic inverse-limit completion with uniform-space completion by natural comparison isomorphisms; do not replace the book’s algebraic statements with topological lookalikes. Artin–Rees and the graded statements are independent obligations, not corollaries of one exactness theorem.
11. **Dimension Theory.** Cover Hilbert functions and polynomials, Noetherian local dimension, regular local rings, and transcendental/polynomial dimension. Combine `ringKrullDim`, `Ideal.height`, [Krull’s height theorem](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Ideal/KrullsHeightTheorem.html), and [polynomial dimension formulas](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/KrullDimension/Polynomial.html). **Hard points:** the documented missing Hilbert-polynomial layer for finitely generated graded modules over Noetherian rings ([HilbertPoly TODO](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/RingTheory/Polynomial/HilbertPoly.html)), followed by equivalences among the book’s local-dimension characterizations. This is the schedule-critical fieldlet and the reason the project ranks first only conditionally.

**Hostile-review emphasis.** Check zero-ring and unit conventions, arbitrary-versus-explicit localization, irredundant decomposition indexing, commutative-only hypotheses, algebraic/topological completion equivalence, and `ENat`-valued versus natural-number dimension statements. The project’s value is a coherent source-faithful map through an already rich library; careless wrappers would squander exactly that value.

### 4.2 Wilf: build the algebraic kernel but spike asymptotics early

**Implementation order.** M0: freeze the third edition and its added sections, inventory every main-text node, and separate formal-series from analytic conventions. M1: Chapters 1–2. In parallel, run feasibility prototypes for Lagrange inversion and one Hayman/singularity-analysis result before Chapter 3 is allowed to dominate the schedule. M2: Chapter 3’s exponential-family interface. M3: Chapter 4, with WZ and cycle-index work as explicit side tracks. M4: Chapter 5. M5: reconcile the appendix/exercise exclusions and perform hostile review. The section order follows the [third-edition TOC](https://dokumen.pub/generatingfunctionology-third-edition-1568812795-9781568812793.html), with the [author-hosted second edition](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wilf/gfology2.pdf) used only as a mathematical cross-check.

1. **Chapter 1, Introductory Ideas and Examples (§§1.1–1.7).** Formalize the recurrence-solving method, ordinary and two-variable generating functions, coefficient extraction, binomial coefficients, set partitions, and Stirling-number examples in order. Use `PowerSeries`, `MvPowerSeries`, finite sums, binomial coefficients, and the enumerative library ([PowerSeries](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/RingTheory/PowerSeries)). **Hard points:** the three-term boundary-value problem and cubic-spline application require explicit finite linear systems and root cases; two-sided sums and generalized binomial coefficients need one reviewed convention. Grinberg’s recurrence examples can guide proofs only after statement comparison ([Grinberg blueprint](https://faabian.github.io/algebraic-combinatorics/blueprint/index.html)).
2. **Chapter 2, Series (§§2.1–2.7).** Build the ordinary and exponential generating-function calculi, composition and inversion, then analytic power series and formal Dirichlet series. Reuse derivative, inverse, substitution, exponential, logarithm, binomial, and multivariate APIs. **Hard points:** formal equality versus analytic convergence; exponential-versus-ordinary coefficient normalization; composition/inversion hypotheses; and multiplicativity of formal Dirichlet series. A general formal Dirichlet-series ring was not found in the pinned scan, so it must be budgeted rather than inferred from Dirichlet L-function modules ([LSeries tree](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries)).
3. **Chapter 3, Cards, Decks, and Hands: The Exponential Formula (§§3.1–3.19).** Introduce cards, decks, hands, exponential families, and the main counting theorems. Then instantiate the full application sequence: permutations and cycles, set partitions, restricted permutations, involutions, 2-regular graphs, connected graphs, labelled bipartite graphs, labelled trees, binomial-type polynomials, unlabelled cards/hands, money changing, integer partitions, and rooted trees/forests. Use finite types, `Equiv.Perm`, set partitions, `Nat.Partition.genFun`, composition/exp/log, and weighted-set ideas from Grinberg ([partition generating functions](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/Partition/GenFun.lean); [Grinberg](https://github.com/facebookresearch/algebraic-combinatorics)). **Hard points:** no dedicated species/exponential-family interface was found; labelled-versus-unlabelled equalities must arise from actual equivalences of finite structures; logarithms must track nonzero constant coefficients. Rooted-tree and connected-graph counts are integration tests for the entire design.
4. **Chapter 4, Applications of Generating Functions (§§4.1–4.13).** Cover averages, the sieve, Snake Oil, WZ pairs, unimodality/convexity, congruences, the symmetric-group cycle index, permutation square roots, polyominoes, exact covering sequences, and the third edition’s waiting-for-a-string and blocks-of-1 material. Use inclusion–exclusion, formal series, finite probability, group actions, and permutation libraries ([inclusion–exclusion](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Combinatorics/Enumerative/InclusionExclusion.lean)). **Hard points:** no general WZ/creative-telescoping engine or Pólya cycle-index API was found; unimodality requires real-polynomial/root analysis; polyominoes and exact covers add finite-geometry and recursive-type burdens. These are separate work packages, not applications that fall out of Chapter 2 automatically.
5. **Chapter 5, Analytic and Asymptotic Methods (§§5.1–5.5).** Formalize Lagrange inversion, asymptotics from poles, algebraic singularities, and Hayman’s method, connecting the earlier formal-series identities to complex analysis, Gamma/Stirling, and asymptotics ([Stirling](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Stirling.lean); [asymptotics](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/tree/9aeb63fe902365a0867a73624edf3fff955f8cd1/Mathlib/Analysis/Asymptotics)). **Hard points:** a dedicated Lagrange-inversion theorem was not found in the pinned scan; singularity analysis needs analytic-continuation, contour, and hypothesis control; Hayman’s method is the largest isolated gap. This chapter is why Wilf ranks below Atiyah–Macdonald despite its smaller inventory.
6. **Appendix, exercises, and solutions.** The Maple/Mathematica appendix is excluded as software documentation under the stated policy; exercises and solution material are excluded from the baseline node range. The manifest must say this explicitly and must still capture any mathematical definition or result that appears only in main-text worked examples. No chapter may be declared complete merely because its named theorems are covered while unnumbered main-text results remain outstanding.

**Hostile-review emphasis.** Check whether each equality is formal, analytic, or both; coefficient normalization in ordinary versus exponential series; convergence radii and composition hypotheses; labelled/unlabelled equivalences; asymptotic direction and constants; and whether computational examples are proved rather than trusted from CAS output. The early Chapter 5 spike is non-negotiable because it determines whether this is a compact book or a disguised multi-year analysis project.

### 4.3 Enderton: reconcile proof calculi before scaling Chapter 3

**Implementation order.** M0: freeze the second edition, record conventions for structures, numerals, variables, and deduction, and prototype both the Hilbert↔LK bridge and a Presburger/QE component. M1: Chapters 0–1. M2: Chapter 2 through Henkin completeness and its consequences. M3: Chapter 3 with two coordinated tracks—decision/QE and computability/incompleteness. M4: Chapter 4. M5: hostile semantics review and full axiom/build audit. Foundation and Mathlib reduce proof risk but do not supply Enderton’s ordered inventory or all of its semantics ([Foundation](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation); [publisher TOC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/book/9780122384523/a-mathematical-introduction-to-logic)).

1. **Chapter 0, Useful Facts about Sets.** Build the source-specific vocabulary for sets, relations, functions, sequences, cardinality, and induction using Mathlib’s set/function/cardinal APIs. **Hard points:** avoid overengineering a new foundations stack and record every prose definition even when its proof is a one-line library application. This chapter is the low-risk ledger and conventions pilot.
2. **Chapter 1, Sentential Logic.** Define well-formed formulas inductively; prove unique readability and the recursion theorem; define truth assignments, satisfaction, semantic consequence, and tautological equivalence; prove expressive adequacy, switching-circuit results, compactness, and effectiveness statements. **Hard points:** Enderton’s direct compactness and absence of a formal proof calculus in this chapter must not be “modernized” into a different theorem sequence. Parsing/recursion and direct compactness require source-faithful proofs even if Foundation’s propositional semantics can supply components ([Logic Matters chapter analysis](https://logicmatters.net/tyl/booknotes/enderton.html)).
3. **Chapter 2, First-Order Logic.** Reuse or adapt Mathlib’s `Language`, terms, formulas, structures, and realization; implement free variables and substitution, Enderton’s Hilbert calculus, and soundness; construct the Henkin model and prove completeness; derive compactness and Löwenheim–Skolem, interpretations, and the nonstandard-analysis/infinitesimals application ([Mathlib semantics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Semantics.lean); [satisfiability](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Satisfiability.lean)). **Hard points:** Foundation’s completeness proof is LK-based, so the project must either prove a faithful Hilbert↔LK equivalence or reuse only semantics-independent consequences while retaining Enderton’s order ([Foundation LK](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation/master/Foundation/FirstOrder/LK/Completeness.lean)). Substitution, nonempty-structure conventions, numerals, and equality semantics require explicit comparison.
4. **Chapter 3, Undecidability.** Cover successor and successor-plus-order theories; Presburger arithmetic and quantifier elimination; Robinson arithmetic with exponentiation; definability/representability and sequence coding; arithmetization; undecidability and both incompleteness theorems; recursive enumerability and the arithmetic hierarchy; partial recursive functions and register machines; and the set-theory/β-function applications. Mathlib supplies computability landmarks, and Foundation supplies incompleteness results ([halting](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Computability/Halting.lean); [reducibility](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/Computability/Reduce.lean); [second incompleteness](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation/master/Foundation/FirstOrder/Incompleteness/Second.lean)). **Hard points:** the quantifier-elimination chain, exact representability hypotheses, and register-machine↔partial-recursive equivalence. Mathlib’s Presburger file explicitly leaves the theory, QE, and completeness as TODOs, so Chapter 3 cannot be waved through as “already in Foundation” ([Presburger TODO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/master/Mathlib/ModelTheory/Arithmetic/Presburger/Basic.lean)).
5. **Chapter 4, Second-Order Logic.** Define second-order languages and full semantics, Skolem functions, many-sorted logic, and Henkin/general structures; prove the book’s comparisons and failures of completeness/compactness. Foundation’s second-order syntax, semantics, and derivations are a starting point ([Foundation summary](https://github.com/FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation)). **Hard points:** never confuse full second-order semantics with Henkin semantics or accidentally inherit first-order completeness and compactness. Many-sorted and general-structure statements need their own source-ledger entries even when they are morally similar to first-order constructions.

**Hostile-review emphasis.** Compare formula binders and substitution, equality and nonempty-structure conventions, numeral representations, Hilbert versus sequent derivability, exact arithmetic-theory hypotheses, machine encodings, and full versus Henkin semantics. The value is not another isolated incompleteness certificate; it is a checked, continuous teaching path in which those landmarks have the same meanings as the trusted text.

### Confidence rules applied to the report

**Live-web-verified facts** are bibliographic metadata, TOCs, publisher/author teaching statements, named project scopes, repository contents, raw proof-hole/status files, and named Mathlib declarations backed by direct URLs. They are labelled **high confidence** when the primary source itself is explicit; project-reported completion remains only high confidence that the project made that report, not that it passed an independent audit.

**Bounded absence claims** use the per-candidate labels in the shortlist. **Medium-high** means several registry types and exact-title/alias searches agreed and relevant partial repositories were opened. **Medium** means the search was useful but had generic naming, sparser cross-assistant checks, or access limits. **Medium-low/low** means the evidence is a lead only. Even “high within searched sources” would not mean worldwide nonexistence. The report therefore says “not found in the documented search,” never “this has never been formalized.”

**Analyst inference** covers all node ranges, effort classes, rankings, downstream-value judgments, implementation sequences, and hard-point diagnoses. Mechanical counts—such as Wilf’s 67 numbered labels, Norris’s 106 theorem-like labels, Hatcher’s 244 result labels, and Hall’s 401 numbered labels—anchor but do not complete those estimates. Training knowledge guided candidate discovery and mathematical interpretation of chapter dependencies; it was not used as evidence for a completion or nonexistence claim. Material existing-coverage, edition/TOC, canonical-status, and Mathlib statements in this report carry direct source links.

### Limitations and required pre-launch checks

- **GitHub and the wider web were not exhaustively searched.** Repository search does not inspect every Lean file, private or unindexed repository, fork, Zulip message, or differently named source attribution. Some queries encountered HTTP 403/500 or rate limits. Non-Lean library censuses were less exhaustive than Lean checks.
- **Prove2Me was not exhaustively searched.** Its unauthenticated mission API returned HTTP 401; public collections and bounded page crawls cannot exclude individual, later, or private missions. A null Prove2Me result therefore receives no more than bounded weight.
- **No independent Lean builds or transitive axiom audits were run.** The research environment lacked a Lean toolchain. Some repositories were cloned and text-scanned, but `lake build`, `#print axioms`, and full `Lean.collectAxioms` reports were not independently executed. Third-party “zero `sorry`” and standard-axiom claims remain claims until replayed.
- **No full hostile statement comparison or hand node census was completed.** Node ranges are planning estimates, not source inventories. A launch decision must freeze the edition, manually enumerate all in-scope nodes, include mathematical appendices and proofless main-text statements, and resolve examples/exercises explicitly.
- **Fast-moving projects can change the verdict.** ATLAS v2, ReasBook, Mathlib, Lean Pool, BrownianMotion, MarkovProcess, Foundation, CSLib, and PhysLib require fresh commit-level checks immediately before adoption. External code becomes a prerequisite only after version reconciliation, build, axiom, license, and statement-fidelity review.
- **“Trusted but unverified” is necessarily bounded.** This report identifies book-faithful sequences not located at the required standard; it does not claim that no formulation of any individual theorem has ever been kernel-checked somewhere. Existing theorem-level coverage is cited wherever found.

The immediate decision is therefore to approve **source-ledger and hard-chapter pilots**, not to certify any whole book as trivially feasible. A candidate should proceed to full implementation only after its pilot closes the named gap with zero placeholders, standard axioms, and statements that survive independent read-back against the frozen edition.
