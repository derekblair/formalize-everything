# What Next for `formalize-everything`? A Landscape Audit & Target Selection Report

**Date:** October 21, 2026  
**Target Repository:** [`derekblair/formalize-everything`](https://github.com/derekblair/formalize-everything)  
**Standard:** Complete-book coverage (every definition, lemma, proposition, theorem, and corollary in main-text order; exercises deferred to v2 per [`PROGRAM.md`](https://github.com/derekblair/rudin-lean/blob/main/PROGRAM.md)), `lake build` exit 0 with zero `sorry`s, Lean's three standard axioms only (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`, no `native_decide`), statement-fidelity verification, and independent hostile review.  
**Calibration Yardstick:** Walter Rudin, *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* (3rd ed., 1976) — 11 chapters, **~330 main-text nodes** (252 nodes completed across Chapters 1–7 in [`derekblair/rudin-lean`](https://github.com/derekblair/rudin-lean)).  
**Out of Scope (Already In Flight / Queued):** Rudin's *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*, *Real and Complex Analysis*, and *Functional Analysis*.

## 1. Executive Summary — Top 3 Recommendations

1. **M. F. Atiyah & I. G. Macdonald, *Introduction to Commutative Algebra* (Addison-Wesley, 1969)** — The undisputed "Baby Rudin of Algebra" (~230 main-text nodes across 11 chapters, 128 pp.): 100% untouched as a complete book across all proof assistants, ~85% scaffolded by `Mathlib.RingTheory` through Chapter 9, and culminating in high-value unverified targets (general Artin–Rees, Hilbert–Samuel polynomials, and the Krull–dimension equality for regular local rings) with known historical proof compression that rewards hostile review.
2. **David Williams, *Probability with Martingales* (Cambridge University Press, 1991)** — The canonical compact bridge from Rudin's *Real and Complex Analysis* to modern probability (~245 main-text nodes across 15 chapters, 251 pp.): completely unformalized as a book (whereas heavier alternatives like Klenke were targeted by PKU's ReasBook), backed by Mathlib's strong Doob martingale library for Chapters 1–12, and unlocking unverified gems in Chapters 13–15 (Kakutani's product martingale dichotomy theorem, the martingale proof of Birkhoff's Ergodic Theorem, and the Lindeberg–Feller CLT).
3. **Jean-Pierre Serre, *Linear Representations of Finite Groups* (GTM 42, Springer, 1977)** — The definitive three-part monograph on finite group representations (~215 main-text nodes across 16 chapters, 170 pp.): untouched as a book in Lean 4, zero analytic or topological prerequisites to block progress, and delivering major missing Mathlib theorems in Parts II–III (Mackey's irreducibility criterion, Artin's and Brauer's induction theorems, rationality of characters, and the modular $cde$ decomposition triangle).

## 2. Explicit Scoping & Effort-Class Conventions

Per [`PROGRAM.md`](https://github.com/derekblair/rudin-lean/blob/main/PROGRAM.md) and [`CATALOGUE.md`](https://github.com/derekblair/formalize-everything/blob/main/CATALOGUE.md), we state the scoping rules applied throughout this report:
- **Node Definition:** One node corresponds to one numbered or explicitly labeled main-text item (Definition, Theorem, Proposition, Lemma, Corollary, plus proof-bearing worked Examples). End-of-chapter exercises are excluded from v1 node counts unless explicitly noted (e.g., where a text delegates core proofs to structured exercises).
- **Effort Classes (calibrated against PMA ≈ 330 nodes = 1.0× standard book unit):**
  - **Compact Single-Book Sprint:** 90–220 main-text nodes (~0.30×–0.65× PMA), 60–170 pages.
  - **Standard Single-Book Project:** 220–350 main-text nodes (~0.65×–1.05× PMA), 170–350 pages.
  - **Large Single-Book Campaign:** 350–550 main-text nodes (~1.05×–1.65× PMA), 350–450 pages.
  - **Multi-Year / Mega-Tome (Rejected):** >600 main-text nodes or >600 pages (e.g., Dummit & Foote, Lang's *Algebra*, Hartshorne's *Algebraic Geometry*, Jech's *Set Theory*).
- **Landscape Distinction (Autoformalized Draft vs. `formalize-everything` Standard):** Bulk LLM pipelines like Meta's ATLAS (`facebookresearch/atlas-lean`, 71.3% statement coverage, CC BY-NC 4.0 license) and PKU's ReasBook (`optpku/ReasBook`) have swept dozens of open-access lecture notes and optimization texts. We reject any book already substantially covered in those corpora or in standalone repos, reserving our shortlist for canonical published texts with zero (or <10% fragmentary) book-level Lean 4 coverage.

### Detailed Candidate Profiles (1–15)

#### 1. M. F. Atiyah & I. G. Macdonald — *Introduction to Commutative Algebra* (Addison-Wesley / CRC Press, 1969)
- **Field:** Algebra (Commutative Algebra & Foundations of Algebraic Geometry).
- **Scope Estimate:** 11 chapters, 128 pages, **~230 main-text nodes** (Definitions, Propositions, Theorems, Lemmas, Corollaries). **Effort Class:** Compact-to-Standard Single-Book Project (~0.70× PMA). *(Note: The book's famous exercises are deferred to v2 per `PROGRAM.md`; main-text propositions are self-contained.)*
- **Existing-Formalization Status (Verified):** **Not done anywhere.**
  - *Lean 4 Ecosystems:* Absent from Meta's ATLAS (`facebookresearch/atlas-lean` v1 & v2), PKU's ReasBook (`optpku/ReasBook`), Lean Pool (`Vilin97/lean-pool`, 0 of 289 verified projects and 0 of 1,784 triaged candidate repos), Palomar Registry (`data.palomar-registry.org`, 0 of 697 entries), and `google-deepmind/formal-conjectures`. In Prove2Me's Formalpedia (`prove2me/formalpedia`, 235,867 declarations), only 2 isolated lemmas mention Atiyah–Macdonald.
  - *GitHub Search:* `gh search repos "Atiyah" --language Lean` returns only `azakhtyamov/atiyah-sutcliffe-disproof` and `fennecs-system/learning-atiyah-v3` (both concern the geometric Atiyah–Sutcliffe configuration conjecture $C_1$, not commutative algebra).
  - *Other Proof Assistants:* Isabelle/HOL AFP and Coq contain individual commutative algebra developments (Hilbert's Basis Theorem, Nullstellensatz, Gröbner bases), but no complete formalization of Atiyah–Macdonald exists in any theorem prover.
- **Mathlib Readiness & Biggest Gaps:**
  - *Ready in Mathlib (`Mathlib/RingTheory/`):* `Ideal`, `Prime.lean`, `Radical`, `Jacobson`, `Nakayama.lean` (Ch. 1–2); `Localization`, `TensorProduct`, `Flat`, `LocalProperties` (Ch. 3–4); `IntegralClosure`, `GoingUp`, `GoingDown`, `NoetherNormalization.lean`, `Nullstellensatz.lean`, `Valuation` (Ch. 5); `Noetherian`, `Artinian`, `HopkinsLevitzki.lean`, `Length.lean` (Ch. 6–8); `IsPrimary.lean`, `Lasker.lean` (Lasker–Noether primary decomposition, Ch. 4 & 7); `DiscreteValuationRing`, `DedekindDomain`, `FractionalIdeal` (Ch. 9); `AdicCompletion`, `Filtration.lean`, `GradedAlgebra`, `KrullDimension`, `RegularLocalRing` (Ch. 10–11).
  - *Biggest Gaps:* (1) The second uniqueness theorem for primary decomposition (isolated vs. embedded primary components via localization, Prop. 4.9 / Thm. 4.10); (2) Artin–Rees Lemma for general good filtrations and its direct deduction of Krull's Intersection Theorem (Thm. 10.9–10.11); (3) Additive functions on graded Noetherian modules, Hilbert–Samuel polynomials $P_M(n)$, and the three-way dimension equality $d(A) = \dim A = \delta(A)$ for Noetherian local rings (Thm. 11.14–11.22).
- **The Case For It:** Atiyah–Macdonald is the universal first graduate text in commutative algebra and the direct algebraic counterpart to Baby Rudin. Having every proposition kernel-checked in book order creates a canonical indexing layer over `Mathlib.RingTheory` while filling the exact Hilbert–Samuel / Artin–Rees / regular-local-ring bridges needed by downstream algebraic geometry. Furthermore, Atiyah–Macdonald's famously telegraphic 3-line proofs contain known subtle slips (such as Proposition 7.8 on finitely generated algebras, where an unstated hypothesis/step was famously caught during early Lean 3 exploration by Kenny Lau).
- **Risks:** Universe polymorphism friction in `TensorProduct` and `Localization` when translating Atiyah–Macdonald's set-theoretic "identify $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ with a subring of $K$" idioms; topological boilerplate in Chapter 10 when bridging `AdicCompletion` (inverse limit) with metric/uniform space completions.

#### 2. David Williams — *Probability with Martingales* (Cambridge Mathematical Textbooks, CUP, 1991)
- **Field:** Probability Theory & Measure-Theoretic Stochastic Processes.
- **Scope Estimate:** 15 chapters (Chapters 0–15) + 3 short appendices, 251 pages, **~245 main-text nodes**. **Effort Class:** Standard Single-Book Project (~0.75× PMA).
- **Existing-Formalization Status (Verified):** **Not done anywhere.**
  - *Lean 4 Ecosystems:* Zero repositories in Lean Pool (`Vilin97/lean-pool`), Palomar Registry (`data.palomar-registry.org`), ATLAS (`facebookresearch/atlas-lean`, which instead formalized 84/100 statements of Scott Sheffield's MIT 18.175 lecture notes), or ReasBook (`optpku/ReasBook`, which targeted Klenke's 600-page *Probability Theory* on branch `v4.29.0`, currently excluded from their active release profile). Only 4 isolated theorem references appear in `prove2me/formalpedia`.
  - *GitHub Search:* `gh search repos "Probability with Martingales" --language Lean` returns 0 hits; `gh search repos "Martingales" --language Lean` returns only `RemyDegenne/demo-martingales`, `Robby955/formal-martingales`, `SmaniaD/Burkholder`, and `MasaakiFukasawa/ftdap-lean`.
  - *Other Proof Assistants:* Kexing Ying and Rémy Degenne formalized Doob's martingale convergence theorems in Mathlib (CPP 2023, arXiv:2212.05486), inspired in part by Williams's exposition, but Chapters 0–15 as a complete book have never been formalized in Lean, Isabelle, or Coq.
- **Mathlib Readiness & Biggest Gaps:**
  - *Ready in Mathlib (`Mathlib/MeasureTheory/` & `Mathlib/Probability/`):* Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ system and monotone class theorems (Ch. 1–3); independence, `BorelCantelli.lean`, Kolmogorov's 0–1 law, `StrongLaw.lean` (Ch. 4, 7); `ConditionalExpectation.lean`, Jensen/Hölder/Minkowski (Ch. 6, 9); `Martingale/Basic.lean`, `Upcrossing.lean`, `Convergence.lean`, `OptionalStopping.lean`, `OptionalSampling.lean`, `Centering.lean` (Ch. 10–12, 14); `CentralLimitTheorem.lean`, `CramerWold.lean` (Ch. 15).
  - *Biggest Gaps:* (1) Kakutani's Theorem on infinite product measures and product martingales (Ch. 10.12 & Ch. 14.17 — dichotomy between equivalence $\mu \sim \nu$ and mutual singularity $\mu \perp \nu$ via Hellinger integrals $\prod E(\sqrt{Z_n})$); (2) Doob's $L^1$ / Uniform Integrability equivalence chain in exact textbook form (Ch. 13–14, including Levy's downward theorem for backwards martingales $\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}$ and the martingale proof of Birkhoff's Pointwise Ergodic Theorem); (3) Inversion theorem for characteristic functions and the Lindeberg–Feller triangular-array CLT (Ch. 15).
- **The Case For It:** While the programme's Rudin trilogy covers measure theory (RCA Ch. 1–3, 6–8), Rudin deliberately omits probability theory, conditional expectation, and martingales. Williams (1991) is the single most celebrated, compact, and mathematically sharp graduate probability text in print—matching Rudin's style while adding high-value stochastic machinery (uniform integrability, backwards martingales, Kakutani's theorem, Black–Scholes discrete-time pricing in Ch. 15/Appendix) directly atop Mathlib's `MeasureTheory`.
- **Risks:** Measure-theoretic "almost everywhere" (`∀ᵐ ω ∂μ`) bookkeeping and `MeasurableSpace` instance diamonds when working with two filtrations simultaneously ($\mathcal{F}_n \subseteq \mathcal{G}_n$) or with infinite product spaces (`Measure.infinitePi`) in Kakutani's theorem.

#### 3. Jean-Pierre Serre — *Linear Representations of Finite Groups* (GTM 42, Springer, 1977)
- **Field:** Algebra (Representation Theory, Character Theory, & Modular Representations).
- **Scope Estimate:** 16 chapters divided into Part I (Ch. 1–5), Part II (Ch. 6–13), and Part III (Ch. 14–16), 170 pages, **~215 main-text nodes**. **Effort Class:** Compact-to-Standard Single-Book Project (~0.65× PMA).
- **Existing-Formalization Status (Verified):** **Not done anywhere.**
  - *Lean 4 Ecosystems:* Zero repositories in ATLAS, ReasBook, Lean Pool, or Palomar. Only 3 isolated declarations in `prove2me/formalpedia` cite Serre GTM 42. (By contrast, Etingof's *Introduction to Representation Theory* is already being formalized in `formalfrontier/etingof-representationtheory-draft1` / `mathlib-initiative/EtingofRepresentationTheory`, and Serre's *A Course in Arithmetic* is underway in `are4c4/SerreNumberTheoryAI`.)
  - *Other Proof Assistants:* Gonthier et al. formalized character theory, Frobenius reciprocity, and Brauer's characterization of characters in Coq/Mathematical Components during the Odd Order Theorem (Feit–Thompson) project (2013), and Part I character orthogonality exists in Isabelle/HOL AFP (`Group_Representations`), **but no Lean 4 formalization of Parts II–III (Artin/Brauer induction, rationality, modular $cde$ triangle) or of the complete book exists.**
- **Mathlib Readiness & Biggest Gaps:**
  - *Ready in Mathlib (`Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/`):* `Basic.lean`, `Maschke.lean`, `Semisimple.lean`, `Irreducible.lean`, `Character.lean`, `FDRep.lean`, `FinGroupCharZero.lean`, `Induced.lean`, `Coinduced.lean`, `Invariants.lean` (covers most of Part I and basic definitions of Part II); `Mathlib/RingTheory/DiscreteValuationRing` and `DedekindDomain` provide the local-ring foundation for Part III.
  - *Biggest Gaps:* (1) Mackey's restriction-induction double-coset decomposition and Mackey's irreducibility criterion (Ch. 7); (2) Artin's Induction Theorem (every character is a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear combination of characters induced from cyclic subgroups, Ch. 9) and Brauer's Induction Theorem (every character is a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of characters induced from elementary subgroups, Ch. 10–11); (3) Rationality of representations over subfields of $\mathbb{C}$ and the Schur index (Ch. 12–13); (4) Part III modular representation theory: Grothendieck rings $R_K(G), R_k(G), P_k(G)$, the decomposition map $d$, the Cartan map $c$, the Brauer lift $e$, and the theorem that $c = {}^t d \circ d$ is invertible up to $p$-power determinant (Ch. 14–16).
- **The Case For It:** Serre GTM 42 is a masterpiece of mathematical exposition: short, self-contained, and escalating from elementary linear algebra (Part I) to deep theorems (Brauer induction and modular $cde$ theory) that are indispensable for algebraic number theory (Artin $L$-functions) and finite group theory. Because the group $G$ is finite throughout, every proof reduces to finite-dimensional linear algebra, cyclotomic fields, and discrete valuation rings—making it 100% immune to analytic or topological formalization bottlenecks.
- **Risks:** Bridging Mathlib's categorical `FDRep k G` API with Serre's concrete matrix/class-function sums $\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G} \chi(g)\overline{\psi(g)}$, and constructing the Brauer lift of eigenvalues from characteristic $p$ (`ResidueField R`) to characteristic $0$ via Teichmüller representatives (`Mathlib/RingTheory/Teichmuller.lean`) in Chapter 15.

### Deep Dive #1: M. F. Atiyah & I. G. Macdonald — *Introduction to Commutative Algebra* (1969)

#### Why It Fits the `formalize-everything` Doctrine
Atiyah & Macdonald (128 pages, 11 chapters) is structured with almost crystalline precision: every paragraph in the main text is either a numbered Definition, a Proposition, a Theorem, a Corollary, or a Remark. Like Rudin's *Principles of Mathematical Analysis*, its reputation rests on extreme economy of proof. Furthermore, Atiyah & Macdonald explicitly fix the convention on Page 1 that *"By a ring we shall always mean a commutative ring with an identity element"* (`[CommRing A]`), meaning 100% of the book maps directly onto a single Mathlib typeclass without non-commutative boilerplate.

#### Statement-Fidelity & Hostile-Review Watchlist
1. **No Tautological Definitions:** In Chapter 2, Proposition 2.12 constructs the tensor product $M \otimes_A N$ and states its universal property for bilinear maps $g : M \times N \to P$. Because Mathlib already defines `TensorProduct`, the formalization of Prop. 2.12 must state the existence and uniqueness of a linear map factoring a `LinearMap.BilinMap` through `TensorProduct.mk`, not `rfl`.
2. **Primary Decomposition Indexing (Ch. 4):** Atiyah–Macdonald defines a minimal primary decomposition $\mathfrak{a} = \bigcap_{i=1}^n \mathfrak{q}_i$. A sloppy formalization might parameterize theorems over a specific fixed decomposition `q : Fin n → Ideal A` where uniqueness only talks about `Set.range (radical ∘ q)` for *that* tuple. To be honest to Theorems 4.5 and 4.10, the statement must quantify over *any* two minimal primary decompositions of $\mathfrak{a}$ (or prove that the set of radicals equals the intrinsic set $\{\sqrt{(\mathfrak{a}:x)} \mid x \in A, \sqrt{(\mathfrak{a}:x)} \text{ is prime}\}$ which depends only on $\mathfrak{a}$).
3. **Dimension Types (Ch. 11):** Atiyah–Macdonald defines $\dim A$ as an element of $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$ (or $\mathbb{N}$ once $A$ is Noetherian local, Cor. 11.11). In Mathlib, `ringKrullDim A` has type `WithBot ℕ∞` (so the zero ring has dimension $-\infty$). When stating Theorem 11.14 ($d(A) = \delta(A) = \dim A$) for a Noetherian local ring $(A,\mathfrak{m})$, `IsLocalRing A` already guarantees `Nontrivial A`, so `ringKrullDim A` is non-negative and finite, allowing a clean statement in `ℕ` alongside a coerced equality in `WithBot ℕ∞`.

### Deep Dive #2: David Williams — *Probability with Martingales* (1991)

#### Why It Fits the `formalize-everything` Doctrine
Williams (251 pages, Chapters 0–15) is the beloved Cambridge undergraduate/first-year-graduate textbook on measure-theoretic probability. Unlike encyclopedic 650-page references (Kallenberg, Billingsley, Klenke, Durrett), Williams keeps the book to ~215 main-text nodes by ruthless pedagogical focus: $\pi$-$\lambda$ systems, independence, Borel–Cantelli, conditional expectation, and discrete-time martingales ($L^1$, $L^p$, $L^2$, UI, optional stopping, Kakutani's theorem, Radon–Nikodym via martingales, backwards martingales, SLLN, and the Central Limit Theorem). Moreover, Degenne, Ying, and the Mathlib probability team explicitly modeled `Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/` on Williams's discrete-time chapters, meaning Chapters 0–14 enjoy ~85% existing Mathlib infrastructure!

#### Statement-Fidelity & Hostile-Review Watchlist
1. **`AE` Equivalence vs. Pointwise Functions:** In Mathlib, `condexp μ m f` is an actual function `Ω → ℝ` chosen from the a.e. equivalence class, so statements like $\mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{G}] \ge 0 \text{ a.s.}$ are written `0 ≤ᵐ[μ] μ[X | m]`. A hostile reviewer will verify that "a.s." statements use `∀ᵐ ω ∂μ` on the ambient probability measure `μ` (`[IsProbabilityMeasure μ]`), never restricting the measure to `μ.trim hm` in ways that hide $\mathcal{G}$-measurability (`StronglyMeasurable[m]`).
2. **Sub-$\sigma$-Algebras (`hm : m ≤ m₀`):** Every theorem in Chapters 9–14 involves one or more sub-$\sigma$-algebras $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ on the same carrier space $\Omega$. Lean 4's typeclass inference cannot carry two `[MeasurableSpace Ω]` instances simultaneously; following Mathlib's `condexp` convention, the ambient $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{F}$ is the instance `[m₀ : MeasurableSpace Ω]` and sub-$\sigma$-algebras are explicit hypotheses `(m : MeasurableSpace Ω) (hm : m ≤ m₀)`.
3. **Extended Real vs. Real Expectations:** Williams carefully distinguishes $\mathbb{E}[X]$ for $X \ge 0$ (valued in $[0,\infty]$, i.e., `lintegral`) from $\mathbb{E}[X]$ for $X \in \mathcal{L}^1$ (valued in $\mathbb{R}$, i.e., `integral`). Do not conflate the two by silently taking Bochner `integral` on non-integrable random variables where Mathlib returns `0` by junk value! Every $\mathcal{L}^1$ theorem must carry an explicit `Integrable X μ` hypothesis (or prove `Integrable` as part of the conclusion).

### Deep Dive #3: Jean-Pierre Serre — *Linear Representations of Finite Groups* (GTM 42, 1977)

#### Why It Fits the `formalize-everything` Doctrine
Serre's *Linear Representations of Finite Groups* (170 pages, 16 chapters split into Parts I, II, and III) is a masterpiece of mathematical exposition, originally written with Three distinct audiences in mind:
- **Part I (Chapters 1–5):** Representations and characters over $\mathbb{C}$, written at an elementary level for quantum chemists and physicists (~55 nodes).
- **Part II (Chapters 6–11):** Representations in characteristic zero, algebraic integers, Burnside's $p^a q^b$ theorem, Mackey's criteria, and Artin's & Brauer's induction theorems, written for graduate algebraists (~90 nodes).
- **Part III (Chapters 12–16):** Modular representations and Brauer's $cde$-triangle connecting characteristic 0 and characteristic $p$ (~75 nodes).

With ~220 main-text nodes in total, it is compact, completely unformalized in any proof assistant, and resolves a famous 5-year gap on Mathlib's roadmap.

1. **Base Field Polymorphism vs. $\mathbb{C}$:** Part I (Chapters 1–5) is stated concretely over $\mathbb{C}$, whereas Part II (Chapters 6–13) works over subfields $K \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ or arbitrary fields of characteristic $0$, and Part III (Chapters 14–16) works over a $p$-modular triple $(K, A, k)$.
