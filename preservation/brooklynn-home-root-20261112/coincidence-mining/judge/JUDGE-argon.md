1. **Candidate 21: A093085 vs A112171 (INTEREST: 8)**
   This identity looks genuinely true because the 2-dissection of Ramanujan's theta function $\varphi(-x)$ decomposes $\varphi(-x)/\psi(x^4)$ into a sum of two components supported on $0 \pmod 4$ and $1 \pmod 4$ that match the $\eta$-quotient decomposition $A + 2q/A$ of the Monster group McKay-Thompson series $T_{32\text{c}}$ up to a twist $x^4 \mapsto -x^4$. It matters because it bridges a classical Ramanujan theta-function quotient (`A093085`) and a monstrous moonshine Hauptmodul (`A112171`) without either OEIS entry cross-referencing the other. The most plausible way it could be viewed as an artifact is that both sequences ultimately reduce to the same underlying level-16 $\eta$-quotient (`A029838` and its reciprocal `A083365`) under a fourth-root-of-unity sign twist.

2. **Candidate 50: A224067 vs A224165 (INTEREST: 7)**
   This equality looks true for all $n \ge 2$ because the reduced complex Collatz map $z \mapsto 3iz + (1+i)$ (with independent odd-part reduction on real and imaginary parts) decouples into two interleaved 1D maps, and the real-part trajectory starting at $3$ ($3 \to 5 \to -7 \to -5 \to 1$) coalesces with that starting at $1$ ($1 \to 1 \to -1 \to -1 \to 1$) after exactly 4 steps, whilst the imaginary-part trajectory depends only on $2n-1$. It matters because the author of both sequences only conjectured that their zero-sets coincide, completely overlooking that the entire stopping-time sequences are identical for all $n \ge 2$ and that the 2D complex Collatz dynamics actually factor into independent 1D dynamics. The most plausible way it could fail at some larger $n$ would be if a starting value $1 + (2n-1)i$ hit the target $1+i$ in fewer than 4 steps before the real coordinates merge, though $n=1$ appears to be the unique such preimage.

3. **Candidate 29: A037860 vs A037878 (INTEREST: 7)**
   The agreement between the base-10 down-variation (`A037860`) and half the $L^1$ distance between the digit tuple and its sorted permutation (`A037878`) looks compelling because it holds across all 88 listed OEIS terms and in fact for every integer $1 \le n \le 1009$.
