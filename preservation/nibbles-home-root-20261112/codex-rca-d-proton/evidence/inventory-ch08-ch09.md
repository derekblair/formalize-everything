| 8.1 | Definitions, pp.160–161 | Define cartesian product; rectangles A×B; measurable rectangles for A∈S, B∈T; elementary sets as finite unions of pairwise disjoint measurable rectangles; S⊗T as the smallest σ-algebra containing the measurable rectangles; monotone classes as families closed under increasing countable unions and decreasing countable intersections; sections Eₓ={y:(x,y)∈E} and Eʸ={x:(x,y)∈E}. |

| 8.3 | Theorem, pp.161–162 | The family S⊗T is precisely the smallest monotone class containing **all elementary sets**. Rectangles alone are insufficient as the printed starting family. The proof establishes that elementary sets form an algebra and that the monotone closure of this algebra is itself a σ-algebra. |
| 8.4 | Definition, p.162 | Define function sections fₓ(y)=f(x,y) and fʸ(x)=f(x,y); distinguish their source σ-algebras. |
| 8.5 | Theorem, p.162 | For any S⊗T-measurable f, every fₓ is T-measurable and every fʸ is S-measurable. This is everywhere section measurability, not merely almost everywhere. |
| 8.6 | Theorem, pp.163–164 | For σ-finite measure spaces (X,S,μ),(Y,T,λ) and Q∈S⊗T, the functions x↦λ(Qₓ), y↦μ(Qʸ) are S- and T-measurable respectively, and their nonnegative integrals are equal. Values +∞ are permitted. |
| 8.7 | Definition plus assertions, p.164 | Under 8.6's σ-finiteness hypotheses, define (μ⊗λ)(Q)=∫λ(Qₓ)dμ=∫μ(Qʸ)dλ for Q∈S⊗T. Assert that this is a countably additive positive measure and is σ-finite. |
| 8.8 | Theorem (Fubini), pp.164–166 | Assume σ-finite factors and S⊗T-measurable f. **(a)** For f:X×Y→[0,∞], the everywhere-defined section integrals φ(x)=∫fₓdλ and ψ(y)=∫fʸdμ are measurable and ∫φdμ=∫f d(μ⊗λ)=∫ψdλ. **(b)** For complex f, finiteness of ∫(∫|f(x,y)|dλ(y))dμ(x) implies f∈L¹(μ⊗λ). **(c)** If f∈L¹(μ⊗λ), its sections are integrable for almost every outer variable; the a.e.-defined section integrals belong to L¹ of the respective factors and satisfy the same integral equalities. Notes restate the consequence that absolute integrability of one iterated integral licenses reversal. |
| 8.9 | Counterexamples, pp.166–167 | **(a)** On the unit square with both measures Lebesgue, choose 0=δ₁<δ₂<⋯→1 and real continuous gₙ supported in (δₙ,δₙ₊₁) with integral 1. Set f(x,y)=Σₙ[gₙ(x)−gₙ₊₁(x)]gₙ(y). Each point has at most one nonzero summand. Both iterated integrals exist, but integrating y then x gives 1, while x then y gives 0. The absolute double integral is infinite; f is continuous away from (1,1). **(b)** On [0,1]² take Lebesgue μ, counting λ, and f=1 on the diagonal and 0 otherwise. It is product-measurable, its x-integral is always 0 and y-integral always 1, so iterated integrals differ; λ is not σ-finite. **(c)** Conditional on CH, choose a bijection j:[0,1]→W onto a well-order whose elements have countably many predecessors. For Q={(x,y):j(x)<j(y)} and f=1_Q, every vertical section is cocountable and every horizontal section countable; all sections are Borel and their integrals are the constants 1 and 0. Iterated integrals differ despite bounded f and probability factors: joint product measurability fails. **Do not introduce CH as a Lean axiom:** prove the conditional statement under the explicit well-order/bijection hypothesis; existence under CH is set-theoretic extra scope. |
| 8.10 | Exposition / counterexample, pp.167–168 | Completeness of both factors need not make the uncompleted product complete. If A is nonempty measurable μ-null and B⊆Y is nonmeasurable, then A×B⊆A×Y with the latter product-null, but A×B is not product-measurable by 8.2. In particular, Lebesgue m₁⊗m₁ is not complete and is not equal as a measure space to complete planar Lebesgue m₂; its completion is m₂. The explicit distinction between Borel, Lebesgue-completed, and product σ-algebras must be retained. |
| 8.11 | Theorem, p.168 | For dimensions r,s≥1 and k=r+s, complete k-dimensional Lebesgue measure mₖ is the **completion** of the product of complete measures mᵣ and mₛ, under the standard coordinate identification Rᵏ≅Rʳ×Rˢ. A theorem merely identifying Borel volume with Borel product volume does not cover this node. |
| 8.12 | Theorem with two internal lemmas, pp.168–170 | Assume complete σ-finite factor spaces and f measurable for the completion (S⊗T)* of the product. All conclusions of 8.8 remain valid, except section measurability and section integral definitions can be guaranteed only a.e. **Lemma 1:** for a positive measure ν on M and M*-measurable f, there is M-measurable g equal to f ν-a.e. (positive extended, real, or complex forms). **Lemma 2:** if completed-product-measurable h is product-a.e. zero, then for μ-a.e. x its section hₓ is λ-a.e. zero and T-measurable; likewise with x,y exchanged. Completeness of factors is essential for the exact section measurability conclusion. |
| 8.13 | Explanatory paragraph, p.170 | Motivates proving existence by showing a full-measure set of solutions. For nonnegative f,g∈L¹(R), the extended-valued convolution h(x)=∫f(x−t)g(t)dt is defined for every x; forthcoming 8.14 implies h∈L¹ and finiteness a.e. Notes that a product of two L¹ functions need not be L¹, using f=g=x^(−1/2) on (0,1), zero elsewhere. Other cardinality/Baire comments are motivation, not separately numbered results. |
| 8.14 | Theorem plus definition, pp.170–171 | If f,g∈L¹(R) for ordinary Lebesgue measure, then ∫|f(x−y)g(y)|dy<∞ for almost every x. Define h(x)=∫f(x−y)g(y)dy at those x (arbitrary a.e.-irrelevant values elsewhere). Then h∈L¹ and ‖h‖₁≤‖f‖₁‖g‖₁. Define h=f*g. This node does not yet use Chapter 9's normalized measure. |
| 8.15 | Definition with elementary claims, p.172 | For positive σ-finite μ and measurable f:X→[0,∞], define D_f(t)=μ{x:f(x)>t} for 0≤t<∞. D_f is nonincreasing and Borel measurable. State the layer-cake specialization ∫f dμ=∫₀∞D_f(t)dt of 8.16. Strict superlevel sets are the printed convention. |

## Faithfulness risks that require explicit accounting

1. Completeness in 8.10–8.12 is a statement about σ-algebras, not merely equality of measures on Borel sets.
2. 8.9(c) explicitly depends on CH in print. It cannot be unconditionally instantiated by adding a prohibited axiom.
3. 8.16 allows +∞ values and only local absolute continuity; a globally smooth finite function variant is weaker.
4. 8.18 uses Rudin's centered ball maximal function in every finite dimension, not an arbitrarily renamed or pre-assumed Lᵖ function.
5. Chapter 9 normalizes measure by (2π)^(−1/2) and uses exp(−ixt). Mathlib's conventional real Fourier kernel uses exp(−2πixt); any restatement must implement and prove the scaling bridge.
