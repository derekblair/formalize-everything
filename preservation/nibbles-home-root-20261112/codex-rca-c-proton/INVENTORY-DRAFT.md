## Chapter 6 — Complex Measures

General convention: a complex measure is countably additive and takes finite complex values; a positive measure may take +∞. All set evaluations below are on the stated sigma algebra. “Unique in Lp” means unique modulo almost-everywhere equality.

## Coverage adjudication: 6.10 and 6.14

**6.14 corollary's positive measures.** The printed equality `μ=λ₁−λ₂` is an equality of finite real-valued set functions under ordinary extended-real arithmetic. Applied to X, its being defined and finite forces λ₁(X),λ₂(X)<∞: exactly one infinite value would give an infinite difference, and two infinite values would make the difference undefined. A Lean theorem using finite positive measures and `toSignedMeasure` therefore makes the arithmetic domain condition explicit; state that rationale in its documentation. An encoding through arbitrary ENNReal values followed by `toReal` would be incorrect because infinity is then converted to zero.

**6.10 attached discussion (printed pp.123–124).** Record separate subclaim coverage within node6.10:

1. **Core(a):** unique decomposition of finite complex λ with positive sigma-finite μ, including positivity of both parts for finite positive λ.
2. **Core(b):** unique L¹(μ) density for λ_a (a.e. uniqueness).
3. **Attached sigma-finite-positive extension:** when both μ,λ are positive sigma finite, there is a unique positive Lebesgue decomposition and nonnegative measurable density h for λ_a; h is integrable on each member X_n of an exhaustion with μ(X_n),λ(X_n)<∞. Global L¹ need not hold. An elementary witness for the last qualification is μ=λ=counting measure on N, h=1.
4. **Attached failure of decomposition beyond sigma finiteness:** on X=(0,1), μ=Lebesgue, λ=counting on the Lebesgue sigma algebra. No positive Lebesgue decomposition of λ relative to μ exists. Proof route: λ_a vanishes on every singleton by λ_a≪μ, so λ_s assigns1 to every singleton. Singularity would concentrate λ_s on a μ-null set N, but every point must belong to N, contradiction with μ(X)=1.
5. **Attached failure of Radon–Nikodym beyond sigma finiteness:** same X, μ,λ satisfy μ≪λ (λ-null sets are empty) and μ(X)=1, but no h∈L¹(λ) represents μ. Every singleton integral would force h(x)=0, hence h vanishes everywhere, contradicting the mass of X.
